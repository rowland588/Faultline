# FC-144 — Impact and effort: which countermeasure first (the PICK chart) — the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-144 · **Knowledge:** `sixsigma.selection-matrices` (`knowledge/methods/sixsigma/selection-matrices.md`), `rca.pdca` · **Asked for:** Rowland, 31 October 2026 — the pathway of `docs/METHODS.md` §11 (used by five methods: PDCA Plan, DMAIC Improve, 8D D5–D6, A3 Countermeasures, Kaizen event Improve) · **Status:** built (M5, branch m5-pdca)

## 1. Purpose
A cause usually has several possible countermeasures and the order is settled by the loudest voice. This puts each one on two scores the team agrees — how much of the gap it closes, how much work it is — sorts it into the PICK chart's four quadrants, orders the list, and where the team can say it, puts the quick wins in money. The sentence it lets the team say to the sponsor: "we do these two this week, they are worth £10.5k a year for 26 hours of work; this one is a project; this one we decline."

**Scope.** A scored register (`#/scored/impact-effort`, the scored-register shape of `src/tools/scored/`): one row a countermeasure, impact 1–5, effort 1–5, and optionally what it is worth a year (£), the hours it takes and any other spend (£). Gives the quadrant, an order, the counts, the money of the quick wins, and a paper. **Not in this build:** raising the quick wins as Actions in one tap (the register's rows are read by hand into Actions for now); reading the impact from a 6M problem's £ automatically (typed — FC-146's money is the place to read it from); payback with discounting or NPV (FC-191, not built — the payback here is the plain division, said as such); the Pugh matrix (FC-143, its own definition); a second axis of cost in a chart (the register is a table with the quadrant named, not a drawn scatter).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The improvement lead with the team around a table or a whiteboard, at a desk or on a phone in the gemba; the sponsor reading the paper. Scenarios (food first): (1) a 6M on a packing line has four countermeasures for film breaks — which do we do this week? (2) a kaizen week's twelve ideas — which do we do before Friday, which go to the sponsor as projects, which do we decline? (3) an 8D's permanent actions — which first, with what money behind it?

## 3. Method selection
The PICK chart — Possible, Implement, Challenge, Kill — is a 2 × 2 of payoff (impact) against effort, developed at Lockheed Martin and printed in the Lean Six Sigma sources as: **Implement** = high payoff, low effort (do first); **Challenge** = high payoff, high effort (a project, planned with the sponsor); **Possible** = low payoff, low effort (when there is slack); **Kill** = low payoff, high effort (decline, and record that it was declined). Checked online on 31 October 2026 against six published descriptions (6sigma.us, learnleansigma.com, vertex42.com, process.st, sixsigmadsi.com, thinkinsights.net): the four quadrants and their meanings agree; none prints a numeric cut — that is the practitioner's. Alternatives (from the record's *When not to use it*): the Pugh matrix (FC-143) where the choice is on several agreed criteria, not two; an investment appraisal (FC-191) where it is capital. The register refuses to be read as an appraisal: it orders the work, it does not prove the option works (that is the PDCA's Check).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established:* the four quadrants and what each says to do (the PICK chart, above); the order — quick wins first, projects planned, fill-ins when there is slack, the rest declined (`sixsigma.selection-matrices`, *Procedure*); a plain payback is cost ÷ the saving a period (engineering economics — `knowledge/MONEY.md`, FC-191 row, I).
- *Assumed:* that the team has agreed the meaning of each score before scoring (the page prints a guide: impact 1 barely moves it · 3 closes a fair part of the gap · 5 closes most of it; effort 1 a day or less · 2 about a week · 3 a few weeks · 4 a month or two · 5 a quarter or more, or capital) — the guide is words, not a computation, and the scores are typed. That the £ a year typed is the team's honest estimate, from the cause's size (the Pareto bar, the 6M problem's £).
- *Chosen (Faultline's):* the cut — **impact 3 or more is high; effort 2 or less is low** — as the record's worked case places the film guide (3, 1) and the reel trolley (3, 2) as quick wins, the new unwind unit (4, 5) a project and the second operator (2, 2) a fill-in. A 3 on either axis is the midpoint and is said to be *on the line* (the row is marked and counted in a caveat) so a team that argues a 3 sees that the quadrant turns on it. The order within a quadrant: higher impact, then lower effort, then the larger £ a year, then the order typed. Whole numbers 1 to 5 only. Not agreed by Rowland as a cut; it is the record's.
- *Money (`knowledge/MONEY.md`, FC-144 row):* impact in the problem's £ a year (typed — the tool's own); effort in hours × the wage (L, labour) plus any other spend typed. The wage is **one box on the register**: "£ an hour for the people doing it", offered from the line's wage × on-costs (`lib/cost` `labourPerHour` is the crew's; the register uses one person's rate: wage × on-costs) with one tap to use it, or typed. Every money input is optional; a missing one is named and nothing waits on it. No causation is invented: the register states what each countermeasure is *worth* only from what the team typed.

## 4. Inputs
Per countermeasure (row): **Countermeasure** (text, required to count); **Impact** (whole number 1–5, required); **Effort** (whole number 1–5, required); **Worth a year, £** (number ≥ 0, optional); **Hours** (number ≥ 0, optional); **Other spend, £** (number ≥ 0, optional). On the register: **£ an hour** (number > 0, optional; offered from the line). Marked *agreed-before-measured*: the two scales' meaning. Read from the line instead of asked again: the wage × on-costs offered for the £ an hour box. A new ranking starts with one blank row.

## 5. Data suitability
Needs at least two countermeasures to be a choice (one is said: "one countermeasure is not a choice yet"); more than about twelve is a long meeting and is not refused. A row without both scores is named and left out of the ranking, never guessed. Scores outside 1–5 or not whole refused in words. A negative £ or hours refused. The scores are opinion until the team has evidence (a trial's result, the Pareto bar) — the page says so under *What this does not say*.

## 6. Calculations
For a row with impact *I* and effort *E* (whole numbers 1–5):

- quadrant = **Implement** if *I* ≥ 3 and *E* ≤ 2; **Challenge** if *I* ≥ 3 and *E* ≥ 3; **Possible** if *I* ≤ 2 and *E* ≤ 2; **Kill** if *I* ≤ 2 and *E* ≥ 3.
- on the line if *I* = 3 or *E* = 3.
- order: quadrant (Implement, Challenge, Possible, Kill), then *I* descending, *E* ascending, worth descending (a row without one last), then typed order.
- effort in money: hours *h* × the £ an hour *r* + other spend *s*. Stated only when every part that is needed is given: *h* > 0 needs *r*; *s* alone is enough when there are no hours.
- payback in weeks = (effort in money) ÷ (worth a year ÷ 52), stated only when both are known and the worth is above 0. Said as "under a week" below 1, whole weeks to 104, then years to one decimal. A plain division — not NPV; FC-191 will.
- the quick wins' money: Σ worth (of those given, "of n" said when not all) and Σ hours (likewise), and Σ h × r when the £ an hour is given.

**Independent worked cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-pick.py`, written apart from the module; each becomes a unit test and an acceptance example):
1. *Film breaks, priced at £18 an hour* — film guide (3, 1) £6,000 a year, 6 h; reel trolley (3, 2) £4,500, 20 h; new unwind unit (4, 5) £30,000, 200 h + £18,000 spend; second operator (2, 2) £2,000, 40 h. Quadrants: Implement 2, Challenge 1, Possible 1, Kill 0. Order: film guide, reel trolley, new unwind unit, second operator. Effort in money: £108, £360, £21,600, £720. Payback: 0.94 weeks (under a week), 4.16 (4 weeks), 37.44 (37 weeks), 18.72 (19 weeks). The quick wins: £10,500 a year (said £10.5k, as `fmtGBP` says every sum), 26 h, £468 of labour.
2. *A packing line, nothing priced* — sealer jaw spares kit (4, 2), splice tape change (3, 3), re-tune the auto-reject (2, 4), rewrite the changeover checklist (2, 1), replace the wrapper (5, 5). Implement 1, Challenge 2, Possible 1, Kill 1. Order: spares kit, wrapper (5, 5), splice tape (3, 3 — on the line), checklist, auto-reject. No money stated; the missing inputs named.

## 7. Outputs
On screen: the lean page — the band (the register's name, the line, the verdict), four tiles (Implement · Challenge · Possible · Kill, each with the count and what to do: "do first", "plan as projects", "when there is slack", "decline and record it"), the sentence, the table in order (order · countermeasure · impact · effort · quadrant · worth a year · hours · pays back), the money lines, *What this does not say*, the closing line.

Sentences (exact): **verdict** "2 to do first" (Implement count; "None to do first" when 0; "Not scored yet" when empty), tone neutral — never red, amber or green: nothing on this list is abnormal, it is an order. **Sentence**: "Do first: Film guide, Reel trolley. Plan as projects: New unwind unit. When there is slack: Second operator. Decline: none." — each clause only when it has rows, at most three names each then "and n more"; "No quick win on this list — plan the projects with the sponsor." when Implement is empty. **Money lines** (each only when its inputs allow): "The quick wins are worth £10.5k a year for 26 hours of work (£468 of labour at £18 an hour)."; missing: "Type what each is worth a year to say what the quick wins are worth." / "Give the hours, and the £ an hour, to say what the work costs." naming the register's box. **Closing**: "Agree the first list with the team, put each on the Actions with who and by when, and prove each with the number it should move."

**The report.** The paper is the register on one page through the shared scored paper (`lib/report/scoredPaper`): the band and verdict, the four tiles with their counts, the sentence, the table in order with every column the screen shows (impact, effort, quadrant, worth, hours, pays back — on the line marked), the money lines and the missing-input lines as the screen says them, the scale guide, *What this does not say*, the closing line; taken from the study's Report ▾ and in the line's combined report and the project's reports where a scored register is listed. It reads the same module as the screen; nothing missing is made up; no AI.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Table of rows with big number boxes (whole numbers) that fit a phone — on a 375-wide screen the table scrolls inside its own box, nothing off the page; Add a countermeasure; the quadrant word beside each row appears as the scores are typed, so the team sees the chart form in the meeting. Phone first, one hand: impact and effort are 1–5 boxes, the £ boxes optional and below.

## 9. Failure handling
A row missing a score: "“Film guide” needs an impact and an effort, each a whole number from 1 to 5." — left out, the rest ranked. All rows incomplete: "Score at least one countermeasure." Fewer than two scored: the verdict stands, with "One countermeasure is not a choice yet — add the others." A client reads and changes nothing; a team member edits; deleting follows the shell. Offline: the register is a study, kept on the device and synced like any. A closed study is read-only. No wage: the money line says what to give, never a zero.

## 10. Integration
Record: a `ToolStudy` with `tool: 'scored'`, `agreed.scored = { key: 'impact-effort', rows, rate? }` — no new noun, no migration (`agreed` is the study's JSON). Shows wherever a study shows: the scored hub (`#/scored`), the register's list (`#/scored/impact-effort`), the line's Tools page and its combined report, a project's Tools, a method page (PDCA Plan, DMAIC Improve, 8D D5–D6, A3 Countermeasures, Kaizen event Improve) as *Built* with New. Catalogue: `app` entry (`Impact and effort`, kind register) so it opens from the rail's tool list and from a method page.
