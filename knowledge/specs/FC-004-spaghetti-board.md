# FC-004 part 3 — The spaghetti diagram drawn on a board: the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-004 Spaghetti diagram (`spaghetti`) and FC-242 (several people, `spaghetti-walkers`) — part 3, a way of making one, not a new tool · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/lean/spaghetti.md`, `knowledge/BUILD-SHAPES.md` ("needs the layout to have a scale") · **Parts before it:** `FC-004-spaghetti-diagram.md` (one person, positions typed), `FC-242-spaghetti-walkers.md` (up to four, said or typed), the Line Talk set (`docs/LINE-TALK-TOOLSETS.md` §4.2) · **Status:** draft, waiting for go (7 November)

Rowland, 7 November: *"spaghetti diagram wont work through speech … spaghetti diagram would work better if I was similar to line standard — I have a blank page, I can make the layout whatever I am observing, basically draw the line or area out, whatever it may be, put people there and click the person to the objects I have on the map, as I do that lines are created."*

## 1. Purpose
Today a spaghetti diagram is typed (place names, metres, optional east/north) or said to Line Talk. On the floor what a person has in front of them is the layout, and the natural way to record a walk is to draw it: the line or area as it is, the people where they stand, the places they go, and a tap from a person to each place in turn. This part gives the spaghetti diagram **the Line standard's board** — a plain board or a photo of the floor, the same icons (people, kit, machines, safety), the same shapes to draw an area — and adds two things the board does not have: **a scale** (two taps, how many metres that is) and **the walk** (tap a person, then the places in order; each tap is a move and draws a line). Everything the diagram already works out (moves, metres, who walks most, the busiest place, the shared routes, the shift and its money, FC-004 §6 and FC-242 §6) is worked out from the board the same way — **the board is a way of filling the walkers' boxes, never a second calculation.** Line Talk's set stays as a second way in; the board becomes the first.

**Scope.** (1) The board on a spaghetti record (`spaghetti` and `spaghetti-walkers`): a plain board (wide · square · tall) or a photo, drawn on with the Line standard's shapes and icons, **and a place pin** (a named spot with no icon — "film store"); (2) the scale: two taps on the board and the metres between them, kept on the record, said in words ("1 cm on the board is 2.3 m"); every straight line on the board then has a length; (3) the walk: a person tapped, then places tapped in order, each tap a move, a line in the person's own style, Undo the last, Done; a second round on the same person adds to it; up to four people (FC-242's limit); (4) the walkers' boxes filled from the board — a place's name from its pin or icon label, a move's metres the straight line × the scale, **said to be straight-line from the board**; metres typed over a move win (FC-004 §3, "typed metres win over the straight line"); (5) the picture — the board with the paths, to scale when the scale is set and every place is on it, on screen and on paper, in place of the typed-layout picture; (6) **Walk on this map:** a spaghetti started from a product's Line standard copies that standard's board (people and marks) with a link back, the standard untouched (option A, `docs/TOOLS-WORLDS.md` W1). **Out:** a free-drawn path (a finger trace) — a walk is place to place, as the method says; a path through a wall or round a machine (the straight line is said to be straight); reading a scale off a photo by itself (the two taps and the metres are asked); more than four walkers; the Line Talk set changing.

## 2. Users and scenarios
The line lead on the floor with a phone, watching a line for ten minutes: draws the line as four rectangles and a pallet, drops three operators and the film store, the case store and the scale, sets the scale off the wrapper's known length, then taps Operator 1 → film store → line → case store → line as they walk. At a desk afterwards, the same board at full size, a move's metres corrected from the tape. The Kaizen team on a layout change: the same board, the places moved, the walk tapped again, before → after.

## 3. Method selection
The spaghetti (string) diagram: each person's path over one layout, place to place, read for distance, crossings and the place everyone returns to. The board changes how the path is recorded, not the method.

**Established · assumed · chosen.** *Established:* a move is from one place to the next; a round's metres is the sum of its moves; a shift is rounds × a round; minutes = metres ÷ pace ÷ 60; labour at the line's hourly rate (FC-004 §3, unchanged); a straight line between two points on a plan at a known scale is the shortest distance, never the walked one. *Assumed:* the board is drawn roughly in proportion — the scale is one number across the whole board, so a board drawn out of proportion measures wrong, and the page says so once the scale is set ("the distances are only as true as the drawing"). *Chosen:* the scale is two taps and a typed length (the Line standard has no scale; `BUILD-SHAPES.md` says a spaghetti needs one); a move's metres from the board are **straight-line and marked so** — a typed metres wins; a walk is a sequence of taps, not a trace; a tap on anything placed (icon, shape, pin) is a place; a round ends on Done or on the person tapped again; the lines are drawn in each person's own style exactly as FC-242 draws them (graphite, teal, dashed, dotted) and never a state colour.

## 4. Inputs
The board (shape or photo, marks — the Line standard's record shape, kept on the calculator record's facts as `board`); the scale (two points on the board and the metres between them); each walker's rounds as taps (person id, then place ids in order); the rest as before (rounds a shift, pace, hourly cost, each optional).

## 5. Data suitability
No scale set: the moves are counted and the picture is a schematic on the board (not to scale), said — exactly FC-242's "moves counted, no metre made up". A move whose two places are the same mark is refused (a tap twice on the same place is one visit). A person tapped with no place after: not a walk, nothing written. A place moved on the board after a walk: the moves keep their places and their lengths follow the new positions (unless typed over), said. A walker whose person mark is deleted: the walk is deleted with it, with Undo.

## 6. Calculations
The scale: `metresPerUnit = typedMetres / dist(p1, p2)` in board units (x, y as fractions of the board's width and height, the board's aspect from BOARDS). A move's straight-line metres: `dist(a, b) × metresPerUnit`, rounded to 0.1 m. Then FC-242 §6 and FC-004 §6 unchanged, the walkers' boxes holding what the board gives.

**Independent cases** (to be added to `scripts/knowledge/cases-kaizen.py` as S4, plain Python, before code): (a) the scale — two taps 0.5 of the width apart on a wide board (16:9), the board 32 m wide, typed 16 m → 1 width = 32 m; a move 0.25 across and (9/16 × 0.25) down = 0.25 × 32 = 8 m across, 8 m down → **11.3 m** straight-line; (b) S3's three operators placed so that the straight lines give S3's own metres (film store 40 m from the line, case store 25 m, scale 9 m) → the same 10 moves, 278 m, Operator 1 130 m, the line arrived at 5 times, the same shared routes and the same money — the board must reproduce S3 to the module's literals; (c) one move typed over (40 → 46 m measured round a machine) → 284 m, the typed one marked.

## 7. Outputs
The page: the board first, with its palette (shapes, people, kit, machines, safety, **a place pin**), the scale control (*Set the scale* → tap, tap, how many metres), the walk control on a tapped person (*Walk: tap each place in order · Undo · Done*), the paths drawn live; under it the sentence, tiles, routes and money exactly as FC-242 says them, the moves listed by person (place → place, metres, "straight-line" or "measured"), each editable. The paper: the board with the paths as the picture (to scale, or "not to scale", said in its caption), then everything FC-242's paper prints. The tool's own words in the picture caption say where the metres come from.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Phone first: the board the page's to scroll and pinch, a tap places, a drag moves, exactly the Line standard's (`line-standard.mjs` proves those), a person's walk a thumb's height; the scale set in two taps and one box. Nothing typed to draw a walk; the metres typed only to correct.

## 9. Failure handling
A tap off every mark during a walk does nothing and says "tap a place". Four walkers already: a fifth person's walk refused in words. A photo that fails to load: the plain board. A record from before this part (typed layout, or from Line Talk): opens as it was, with *Draw it on a board* offered; drawing one keeps the typed figures until a walk is tapped, then the board's win and the page says so.

## 10. Integration
The record: the existing calculator record (`spaghetti`, `spaghetti-walkers`); the board as `facts.board` (a `Standard`-shaped board: `board`, `photoKey`, `marks`), the scale as `facts.scale`, the walks as `facts.walks` — no new noun, no schema change (facts are JSON; `sync-schema.test` unchanged). Where it shows: the line's Tools (In use, with its finding), a Kaizen event's Observe row, the line report and the full package (the picture as the tool's own paper block), the project report. The Line standard's board code (`StandardScreen` map, `standard.ts` marks and shapes, `standardCard` drawing) is **reused by import, never copied**: the map's drawing part moves to a shared `src/tools/linestandard/Board.tsx` the Line standard and the spaghetti both draw, proved by `line-standard.mjs` unchanged. Line Talk's `spag` set fills the same boxes as before. Access: `can.edit` on every tap that writes.

## 12. Maintainability
One board model for the Line standard and the spaghetti (`standard.ts`); the spaghetti's figures stay in `src/tools/spaghetti/walkers`; the board only feeds its inputs. A new icon or shape on the Line standard is on the spaghetti's board too, for free. The catalogue: FC-004 and FC-242 gain `built: Board.tsx` and this spec; FC-242 may move from partial to available once criteria 1–10 pass.
