# FC-046 — Accumulation between machines: buffer minutes, recovery and what a longer stop costs

**Catalogue:** FC-046 (a mode of FC-034 Line capacity — it lives on a product's Line capacity page under the V-curve and on that product's paper; FC-047 line simulation, for many buffers and random stops together, is research and not built) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/capacity/accumulation-buffer.md` (full, practice), `knowledge/methods/capacity/v-curve.md`, `docs/CAPACITY.md` §4, `knowledge/MONEY.md` §4 (FC-046: **H** the minutes of accumulation, **T** a starvation) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem — *"a short stop on the labeller stops the filler"*; `docs/METHODS.md` §10–11, Theory of Constraints, *Identify the constraint* (FC-046 part-built); Rowland, 1 November — *"I want all tools made that get used in the projects"* · **Status:** specified and built 1 November (branch `m6-toc`); no decision of Rowland's outstanding — every input is Line capacity's and the stops are the log's.

## 1. Purpose
Line capacity already says whether a *typical* stop beside the bottleneck is covered by what can collect (`accumulation`). A planner needs the other three numbers the practice uses — how long that buffer takes to **come back** (a stop in a part-empty buffer meets less cover), what the stops **longer than the cover** really cost the bottleneck, and the buffer the neighbour's **usual long stop** would need. The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"The filler's accumulator holds 6.7 minutes of the bagger's work and takes an hour to refill; 4 of its 10 logged stops ran past it and starved the bagger 28 minutes — 2,550 packs, £1,020. To cover its usual long stop it would need 1,350 packs, or the filler's stops need to come down."*

**Scope.** On a product's Line capacity page, under the V-curve (a new **Buffers** block), and as one line on that product's paper: for every machine either side of the bottleneck that has a stop logged or something written to collect after it — the **cover** (units that can collect between it and the bottleneck ÷ the bottleneck's own speed), the **recovery** (the same units ÷ how much faster the stopped machine runs than the bottleneck), how many of its logged stops were **absorbed** and how many **ran past** the cover, the minutes, units and money the bottleneck lost to those, the **buffer that would cover the 90th percentile** stop (from five stops), whether the buffer is **back before the next stop** (recovery against the usual gap between that machine's stops, when the planned hours are given), and the weakest link. **Not here:** many buffers and random stops together (FC-047, research); the *bottleneck's own* stops (they cost output directly — the loss count and Stops timed); two stops close together beyond saying the recovery (not simulated); a buffer's stock tied up and its carrying cost (**H**, needs a cost a piece — said in the limits, not worked); sizing a buffer from the OEM's drawings (the person types what can collect); product that cannot wait (a hot bake, a chilled dwell) — a time limit the arithmetic does not know, said in the caveat.

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead or engineer at a desk with a line's Line capacity open, or in a meeting about the V margin. (1) *The record's case:* a filler at 100 a minute feeding the bagger (critical, 90) through a 600-pack accumulator, ten filler stops logged in four weeks. (2) *A buffer that never refills:* the margin is 11% and the filler stops every 52 minutes — the buffer is never full again, so the margin, not the buffer, is the fix. (3) *After the bottleneck:* a case packer stops and the table before it fills; the bagger is blocked when the table is full. (4) *Nothing written down yet:* the sheet says what to write. A client reads it; the paper carries one line.

## 3. Method selection
The cover/recovery/starvation arithmetic is the practice's (`docs/CAPACITY.md` §4; Hopp & Spearman, *Factory Physics*, ch. 8–9 for blocking and starving; EN 415-11 for the place of accumulation in a line model — scope only, text not read). Alternatives from the record: the V-curve's margin alone (kept — it is the *why* of the recovery); line simulation (FC-047) for many buffers and random stops at once (research first); Kingman's VUT (FC-045) for queues at a machine. The method refuses to size a buffer from fewer than five stops and says so; it does not charge a neighbour's stop to the bottleneck while nothing is written down as collecting (it says what to write instead).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established* (`capacity.accumulation-buffer`, worked in `scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`): cover = A ÷ r_c (a stop of t minutes with t ≤ cover costs the critical machine nothing); recovery = A ÷ (r_n − r_c); a stop of t > cover starves it for (t − cover) minutes = (t − cover) × r_c units; the buffer that covers a stop of t minutes holds t × r_c. Money: a minute lost *at the bottleneck* is a minute of the line (Goldratt & Cox 1984; `knowledge/MONEY.md` causation **T**) — units × contribution a pack, "if it cannot be made up".
- *Assumed*, and said on the page: one stopped machine with every other running; the refill runs at the slowest speed the product meets between the stopped machine and the bottleneck (before it) or at the stopped machine's own speed (after it), less the bottleneck's; speeds are the *running* speeds (`ownSpeed` — a plate speed at a machine, a person's standard time), never the speed with stops counted; the stops in the log are the machine's own timed stops over the last four weeks — a changeover is the whole line's planned stop and a wait is a neighbour's, so both are left out; a tally row (several stops with one total) is that many stops of its mean.
- *Chosen (the builder, 1 November — the owner asked for the tools to be made and not to be asked):* **the 90th percentile by nearest rank** (the ⌈0.9 n⌉-th of the sorted stops) because it is the record's "90th percentile of its stop lengths" in the plainest reproducible form; **five stops** before a buffer is sized (the repo's `MIN_SHIFTS` convention for "too few to plan on"); the **gap between stops** is (planned minutes in the window − the stops' minutes) ÷ stops, only when the planned hours a week are given on the sheet (`plannedHoursPerWeek`), never assumed; **amber** only where a stop ran past the cover or the buffer never refills before the next stop — a buffer by itself is a plan, neutral; **no new record** — the buffer is Line capacity's own `bufferAfter`.
- *Money line (MONEY.md §4):* causation **T** (starved minutes at the bottleneck × contribution a pack — the product's own price less materials, else the line's margin a pack, `contributionOf`); input **margin a pack**. When missing: *"Give the line a margin a pack (Set up the line) to say what the starved minutes cost."* — the units still said, nothing waits. **H** (the buffer's own stock and its carrying cost) needs a cost a piece and a carrying rate: **not built here**, said in the limits.

## 4. Inputs
Read from the line, asked nowhere new: each station's speed and crew (`ownSpeed`), what can collect after each (`Station.bufferAfter`, typed on the sheet in the station's own unit, converted to the line's unit by the chain), the stops timed in Capture on each machine in the last four weeks (`lib/useLineStops`, the Pareto's window), the planned hours a week (`Capacity.plannedHoursPerWeek`, optional), the contribution a pack (the product's price and materials, else the line's margin). Nothing is typed on the block.

## 5. Data suitability
Needs a bottleneck with a speed; a "can collect after" written for any of the stations (without one the block says nothing is written down and gives no money). Stops: five or more of a machine before its buffer is sized from them; under five the block says how many are logged and how many are wanted. A stop with no time is left out (only timed stops have a length). Lines made of several lines feeding one place (lanes) get no block (each line's own V is shown; the buffer between lines is not modelled). A client sees the block and changes nothing.

## 6. Calculations
For a stopped station *i* beside the bottleneck at index *at*, in the line's unit: `units` = Σ bufferAfter × factor of the stations between (i … at−1 before it; at … i−1 after it); `r_c` = ownSpeed(bottleneck); `cover` = units ÷ r_c; `gap` = min(ownSpeed over i … at−1) − r_c before it, ownSpeed(i) − r_c after it; `recovery` = units ÷ gap when gap > 0 (else the buffer does not come back); for the logged stops *t*: absorbed = t ≤ cover, passed = t > cover; `lost` = Σ max(0, t − cover) minutes, × r_c units, × contribution; `p90` = the ⌈0.9 n⌉-th stop; `need` = p90 × r_c; `between` = (planned − Σ t) ÷ n; `refills` = recovery ≤ between.
**Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`, plain arithmetic; each a unit test in `src/tools/linebalance/__tests__/capacityBuffer.test.ts`): the record's case — 600 ÷ 90 = 6.667 min cover, 600 ÷ 10 = 60 min recovery; a 12-minute filler stop starves the bagger 5.333 min = 480 packs, £192 at 40p; 10 minutes of cover needs 900 packs; a 25% margin refills 600 in 26.67 min and a 10% margin in 66.7; the stops [2,3,3,4,5,6,8,12,15,20]: 6 absorbed, 4 ran past, 28.333 minutes starved = 2,550 packs = £1,020, p90 = 15 min (nearest rank) needs 1,350 packs; a feeder at 120 with a filler at 100 between and the bagger at 90 refills at 10 a minute; a bagger at 90 with 450 collecting before a case packer at 100: cover 5 min, clears in 45; planned 2,400 min → 232.2 min between the filler's stops (refills), planned 600 → 52.2 (does not).

## 7. Outputs
On screen (the **Buffers** block): one sentence first (the whole — how many logged stops ran past what can collect, the minutes, units and pounds lost, the weakest link); a table — *Beside Bagger · Can collect · Holds · Back in · Stops · Ran past · Lost · Would hold* — a row a machine, amber-edged where a stop ran past; each row in words under it (cover, recovery, stops, what the buffer would need, whether it is back before the next stop); the money sentence or the one input to give; the limits (one stopped machine, the refill's speed, four weeks). On a phone the table's first three columns and the words.

**The report.** Line capacity's one-product paper gains **Buffers —** one line under *Three numbers*, from the same module (`capacityBuffer.bufferView().paper`): *"weakest link the filler: holds 6.7 min, back in 60 min; 4 of 10 logged stops ran past it — 2,550 packs, £1,020"*. A product's paper is one page (`list-paper.mjs` 1): where the line would cost that page the paper leaves it to the screen. The list papers and the combined line report print it through the same blocks. Nothing missing is made up: no buffer written → no line.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Nothing to type: the block reads the line. Taps to the answer: open the product's Line capacity — Buffers sits under the V. Phone: the table keeps its first columns, the words carry the rest, nothing off the side. Neutral colour; amber with words only where a stop ran past.

## 9. Failure handling
No bottleneck or speed: no block. Nothing written to collect: said, what to write, no money. No stops logged: the cover and recovery alone, said. Under five stops: how many logged and wanted, no sizing. A neighbour no faster than the bottleneck: "does not come back". No margin: the units said, the missing input named. No planned hours: the gap between stops not said, the input named. Lanes: no block. A client reads, changes nothing.

## 11. Verification
`capacityBuffer.test.ts` (the independent cases, edge cases, the log's filtering); `scripts/acceptance/accumulation.mjs` against a dev server (a two-machine line typed to the record's figures, ten stops put in the log, the block read against the module and the literals, the margin given, the paper downloaded and read back, the phone, a client).

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. Cover, recovery, absorbed/passed, minutes, units and pounds lost, the 90th percentile and the buffer it needs equal the independent cases — `capacityBuffer.test.ts`; `accumulation.mjs` 1–2 reads the page's table and sentences against those literals and the module.
2. A machine no faster than the bottleneck never refills; a refill is at the slowest speed on its way; an after-bottleneck stop blocks — `capacityBuffer.test.ts`.
3. Changeovers, waits, untimed stops, stops outside the window and deleted ones are left out of the stops — `capacityBuffer.test.ts`.
4. Nothing written, no margin, no planned hours, one stop, a buffer large enough, no stops: each said, nothing invented — `capacityBuffer.test.ts`; `accumulation.mjs` 1, 3.
5. The money appears only from a margin and names its missing input before — `accumulation.mjs` 3.
6. The paper, downloaded and read back, carries the Buffers line as the module says it, and a product page already full to one page stays one page — `accumulation.mjs` 4; `list-paper.mjs` 1.
7. On a phone nothing is off the side — `accumulation.mjs` 5. A client changes nothing — `accumulation.mjs` 6.
8. Existing behaviour unchanged: Line capacity's own `accumulation` sentence, the V and the sheet are untouched (`line-capacity.mjs` still passes); no record changed.
Not yet proved: a combined line report with the buffers of every product (the line is read through the same papers, not driven here); the paper line on a product page that is already full to its one page (left to the screen by design).
