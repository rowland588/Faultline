# FC-138 — DMAIC as a kind of change: the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-138 · the DMAIC method's project — its questions, its board of five phase gates, the *Charter* paper, its sections of the project report and its reading of the dashboard (`docs/METHODS.md` §4, §16, §17) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/sixsigma/dmaic.md` (ISO 13053-1:2011, confirmed 2022 — checked online 22 October; the phases' deliverables from knowledge) · **Asked for:** Rowland, 31 October — *"the method equals the project"*, DMAIC one of the eight; 1 November — *"I asked you to build the method and insert the tools where required"*; 20 November — *"Finish all of the partly made tools"* · **Status:** the board, the paper, the report sections and the reading built 1 November (`m7-dmaic`, `r9-dmaic`); the charter made one record with FC-142 on 1 November; **this specification written on 20 November, after them** (the standard says before — said here), with one gap closed against it the same day: the control plan (FC-133) read at the Control gate. The criteria below were proved against what stands.

## 1. Purpose
A data-heavy improvement — give-away, yield, a chronic seal-failure rate — run in five phases, each closed by a review on its evidence before the next begins, so the sponsor can say: *"Measure passed on 28 November — baseline Ppk 0.6, the gauge fit at 12%; Analyse is in hand"* and the team can say what the next review still lacks.

**Scope.** DMAIC as a method project (`#/projects?method=dmaic`, then `#/project/:id`): the five phases' questions as ISO 13053-1 asks them (Define's charter — problem statement, customer requirement, CTQ, scope, SIPOC, goal, business case, team, timeline, risks; Measure's baseline, data collection plan, gauge and capability; Analyse's causes, tests and vital few; Improve's options, solution risks and pilot; Control's plan, standard, benefit confirmed and handover); the board — a gate strip of the five phases, each phase's state in words, a review per phase (who, the day, what it decided — passed · passed with conditions · held — and the conditions), a held review said on the next phase and never a lock; the charter at Define, read as one record with the charter page (FC-142); the project's own tools speaking at the phase they answer (the charter page at Define; Capability at Measure's baseline and Analysis's gauge study at its gauge; Analysis at Analyse and at Improve's pilot; the control chart and **the control plan (FC-133)** at Control), each read only with Open or Start; the *Charter* paper; DMAIC's own sections of the project report (the five reviews, the charter at a glance, the next phase and what its review lacks) and its reading on the dashboard. **Not here:** a two-name sign-off of a review (one name is the record — the sponsor's); the holding period after Control as a date of its own (the control chart and the weekly number trend the gain); the sigma level (FC-102 — a Measure tool); SIPOC (FC-139), VOC → CTQ (FC-140), the Pugh matrix (FC-143) and DOE (FC-111) are their own tools, offered at their questions and not read into the gate; the timeline's dates are words and are not compared with the review days; no money of DMAIC's own (below).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The project lead (a Green or Black Belt, or the site's CI engineer) and the sponsor at each phase review, at a desk or on a phone in the meeting; the team writing the phases' answers as the work is done. (1) The give-away project of the record: Define signed, Measure's baseline Ppk 0.6 on 120 packs and the checkweigher's %GRR 12%, Analyse by head, Improve's DOE and pilot, Control's X̄-R chart and control plan row. (2) A review held: Measure held because the gauge is unfit — Analyse says it waits, nothing locks. (3) A review booked and gone by with no decision — amber, and the next step is to hold it.

## 3. Method selection
DMAIC (ISO 13053-1) when the cause is in the data and several factors may interact; the record's alternatives stand and are said in the method's guide (`lib/methodGuide`): a floor problem fixed in a week → the 6M, the A3 or PDCA; new equipment with its gates → the stage gate; the capability arithmetic → FC-094; the sigma conversion → FC-102. The method's own work is the gates; the analysis is the tools'. Unsuitable: tollgates as paperwork — the board shows each phase's written answers and its tools' sentences beside the review, so a review can be seen to rest on evidence or not.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established (ISO 13053-1; the record):* five phases in order — Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control — each closed by a review on evidence; Define's deliverable is the charter (problem, customer requirement and CTQ, scope and SIPOC, goal, business case, team, timeline, risks); Measure proves the gauge before the data are believed and takes the baseline with its capability; Analyse proves causes with data; Improve pilots the solution and proves it on the CTQ; Control holds the gain with a control chart at the point of control and **a control plan row**, the standard, and the handover to the process owner. Control handed to nobody is the common decay.
- *Assumed:* the words written are the team's facts; the person named on a review held it on the day written; a study made in the project is about the project's CTQ.
- *Chosen (Faultline's, consistent with the record):* (1) a review's decision is one of three words — Passed, Passed with conditions, Held — and a phase's state is said in words (Not started · Under way · Ready for review · Passed · Passed with conditions · Held), never colour alone. (2) A review **never locks** the next phase: a held or unheld review is said on the next phase's card, and the dashboard notes a phase started early (advice, not a gate — the floor starts Measure while Define's review is booked). (3) The current phase is the first not passed (with or without conditions). (4) A tool speaks at the phase it answers by its newest record in the project, in its own sentence (`lib/studyLinks studyLine`); one with none is said so and offered — never required, never invented. (5) Control reads two records: the control chart (the gain held) and the control plan (what is checked, how often, what if not) — the control plan added 20 November because the record's Control phase names it and FC-138 depends on FC-133. (6) The charter is one record: Define's questions *are* the charter; the charter page (FC-142) offers the sheet's words, nothing is stored twice.
- *Money (`knowledge/MONEY.md` §4, FC-138):* "the project's worth: the measure's gap × its £ (the tool's), before → after · causation: the tool's; I". DMAIC has no money of its own: the business case and the benefit confirmed are the team's words (with how they were worked out), and the project report's *What everything is telling us now* carries each tool's own money clause, by tool, never summed. A missing input is the tool's to name. Nothing is estimated.

## 4. Inputs
None needed to start. Written (project notes `method:dmaic:<field>`): Define's ten, Measure's four, Analyse's three, Improve's three, Control's four (`lib/methodSheets/dmaic`); a review per phase — *Reviewed by* (words), *Review date* (a date), the decision (one tap of three), *What it decided, and any conditions* (words) — kept as `rv_<phase>_who|when|result|note`. Read: the project's studies (Capability; Analysis by its kind — gauge, change, the others; the control chart; the charter page; the control plan).

## 5. Data suitability
Nothing is refused: a phase with nothing written is *Not started*; a passed review with no name or day is flagged (a review is only a record if it says who and when); conditions with none written are flagged; a review booked and gone by with no decision is flagged; Control passed with nobody named for the handover is flagged. A gauge study or a baseline is the tool's to judge (its own sentence says whether the gauge is fit, how sure the Ppk is).

## 6. Calculations
No figure of its own. Per phase: written = its questions with any words, of its count; state from the review's decision, else from written (0 → Not started; all → Ready for review; else Under way). Current gate = first phase not passed. The strip's sentence: *n of 5 phase reviews passed* and the phase in hand. **Independent cases** (hand-worked, `src/project/method/__tests__/dmaicModel.test.ts`): an empty project — five Not started, 0 of 5; Define fully written — Ready for review; Define passed with conditions by Tanya on 14 Nov and Measure held — current Measure, Analyse waits, *1 of 5 phase reviews passed*; all five passed — *All five phases have passed their review*; a control plan register in the project read at Control in its own sentence, an FMEA register not taken for it.

## 7. Outputs
On the board: the strip's sentence; each phase's card — what it asks, its state and *n of m written*, the review, and its tools' sentences; the charter block at Define (what is written, what is still to write). On the dashboard: the held review in red with its reason, the next step (book, hold, or put right the held review).

**The report.** Two papers from the same model the board reads (`shapes/dmaicModel`). The **Charter** (Report ▾ on the method page; `lib/report/charterPaper`): the charter row by row with *Not written yet* named, the five phase reviews (state, who, day, decision, conditions), and what the project's tools say at their phases (the control plan among them). The **Project report** (`#/project/:id/method-report`, its paper by Report ▾ › Project report): DMAIC's three sections — *The five phase reviews* (a table: phase, state with written n of m, who, date, what it decided, toned by state), *The charter at a glance*, *Next: <phase>* (what is unwritten, what the review lacks, the project's own tools at that phase) or *Where it stands: closed* — and the dashboard's attention. Nothing missing is made up; a client may read and print both.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
The phase in hand open, every other phase one line with its state in words (`BoardFold`); a review is three boxes and three chips; every control 44 px; one column on a phone. The tools are one tap from their phase (Open lands in the project; Start makes one there).

## 9. Failure handling
No studies: each slot says *no … in this project yet* and offers Start (can.edit only). A held review: said in red on its card, on the next phase's card and on the dashboard; nothing locks. A client: reads the reviews as a sentence (*Review: Passed with conditions — Tanya, 14 Nov 2026*) and has no box, chip or Start. A day that is not a date is shown as typed.

## 10. Integration
Records: project notes (`method:dmaic:*`) — no new noun, no migration; the project's studies, read only. Shows on: the method page (board, the section boxes with their tools), the dashboard (`lib/methodInsights/dmaic`), the project report (`project/method/report/dmaic`), the Charter paper, the charter page (FC-142, `charter-define.mjs`). Access: `useAccess` — `can.edit` writes answers and reviews and starts tools; a client reads. Sync: as project notes.

9. The project report's DMAIC sections equal the model line by line (the review table row by row, the charter's facts, the next phase's gaps), the dashboard says a held review in red with what to do next and a booked review gone by in amber, and the paper read back carries them — `report/__tests__/dmaic.test.ts`, `methodInsights/__tests__/dmaic.test.ts`; `scripts/acceptance/method-report-dmaic.mjs` steps 1–4.
