# FC-016 — Kaizen event: a focused week with its 30-day check — the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-016 · the Kaizen event method's project — its questions, its board (the week day by day and the 30-day check), the *Kaizen event report* paper, its sections of the project report and its reading of the dashboard (`docs/METHODS.md` §8, §16, §17) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/lean/kaizen-event.md` (Imai 1986 as the root; the event as Laraia, Moody and Hall's *The Kaizen Blitz* frames it — from knowledge) and `knowledge/methods/rca/pdca.md` (the cycle each change follows) · **Asked for:** Rowland, 31 October — *"Kaizen event as the sixth"* of the methods; 1 November — *"I asked you to build the method and insert the tools where required"*; 20 November — *"Finish all of the partly made tools"* · **Status:** the board, the paper, the report sections and the reading built 1 November (`m7-kaizen-event`, R9); **this specification written on 20 November, after them** (the standard says before — said here), with one gap closed against it the same day: the readings since the week read for a trend at the 30-day check. The criteria below were proved against what stands.

## 1. Purpose
A team is released for a few days on one scoped problem — a filler's changeover, a packing hall's flow — and its gains are tested a month later. The event lets the team say to the sponsor: *"The week took the changeover from 42 to 24 minutes, but at 30 days it is 28 and rising — the gain is creeping back, and the three open actions are why."*

**Scope.** The Kaizen event as a method project (`#/projects?method=kaizen-event`, then `#/project/:id`): the charter's questions (problem and goal, sponsor, the measure and its target, team, dates, the baseline taken before the week); the week as day columns — five, a sixth and seventh by *Add a day* — each with what was seen, what was changed and the measure at the day's close, dated from the first day with the weekend skipped; the week's own answers (the waste seen, the changes tried and measured, the measure after); the new way written and who is trained; the actions carried to the check, read from the project's Actions (raised, closed and the share closed, open, late, open with nobody named); **the 30-day check** — booked in one tap thirty days after day five, judged in a word (Holding · Slipping · Lost), said overdue when gone by unrecorded, with the open actions and the measure at 30 days, and **the readings since the week, typed in order, read for a trend (Spearman's ρ on their order) against which way is better for the measure — a rise the wrong way said as the gain creeping back**; the project's own tools speaking where they answer (the baseline's tools, SMED and Before and after for the week, the Gemba walk's freshness, Actions and the control chart at the check), each read only with Open or Start; the *Kaizen event report* paper; the event's sections of the project report and its reading on the dashboard. **Not here:** the before → after test on the baseline and the measure after (they are the team's words; the test is Analysis, *Did it change?* — FC-105 — made in the project, whose sentence the report quotes); the readings drawn as a run chart (the control chart, FC-086, draws them); the event's own cost, team days × rate (below); a report-out to the sponsor as a question of its own; a programme of events (`projects.ci-programme`).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The facilitator and the team during the week — at the line on a phone, at the day's close at a desk; the sponsor at the report-out and a month later at the check. (1) The record's SMED week on a filler: baseline eight changeovers about 42 minutes, after about 24; 11 actions raised, 8 closed at 30 days (73%), 3 open; the last six changeovers 25, 24, 28, 31, 30, 33 — rising, the gain creeping back. (2) A week whose third day was not written up — named. (3) A check booked for a day gone by and not recorded — red, and the next step is to hold it.

## 3. Method selection
The rapid improvement event for one scoped process when a team can be released for days; the record's alternatives stand and the method's guide says them (`lib/methodGuide`): daily small steps → the Improvement Kata (FC-017); the cause of one problem → the 6M tool; the changeover's own method → SMED; the cycle each change follows → PDCA. Unsuitable: an event with no baseline (said), an event run by the calendar not a problem (the charter asks the problem first), heroics instead of method (the new way written and taught is asked).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established (the record):* a charter (problem, scope, measure and target, team, sponsor, dates); a baseline taken before the week, not remembered; the week's record (waste, changes, readings after, the new standard, the actions); the new way written and the people trained — the usual reason a gain holds; the actions carried with owners and days to a 30-day check, the share closed and the open ones past their day; the 30-day check as the test of the week — "an event whose gains decay was a demonstration, not an improvement"; holding read from the readings between the event and the check, a trend back toward the baseline (Spearman's ρ on the order, or the run rules) the warning before the gain is gone. The section names and order — Prepare · Observe · Improve · Standardise · Sustain — are practice's (`docs/METHODS.md` §8).
- *Assumed:* the words written are the team's facts; the readings since the week are the same measure taken the same way, in the order they were taken; an action on the project's Actions page belongs to the event.
- *Chosen (Faultline's, consistent with the record):* (1) the week is five day columns by default, more only when added or written (up to seven); dates skip Saturday and Sunday. (2) The check is suggested thirty days after the fifth day (or the last day with words, if later), moved to the next working day; booking is one tap, never automatic. (3) The verdict is the team's word — Holding, Slipping or Lost — never worked out; the readings' trend is advice beside it, and the dashboard flags a Holding verdict the readings contradict. (4) The trend is Spearman's ρ of the readings against their order with its two-sided p from Student's t on n − 2 (as SciPy's `spearmanr`; the app's `lib/ie/relate`, FC-109); a trend is said when p < 0.05 — the same 5% the team's weekly number uses (FC-117) — and never from fewer than four readings (relate's floor); all readings equal is no trend. (5) Which way is better for the measure is the person's tap (Lower is better · Higher is better), never guessed from the words; with none given the trend is said and the reading of it asked. (6) Readings are typed as numbers separated by commas or spaces; a word that is not a number is named and left out, never guessed.
- *Money (`knowledge/MONEY.md` §4, FC-016):* "the before → after of the tools it used, each in its own money; the event's own cost = team days × rate · causation L, I · inputs: wage; the tools' inputs · when missing: each tool names its own". Built: each tool's own money clause, by tool, in the project report's *What the week changed* and *The 30-day check* (never summed, never invented); *What the week bought* is the team's words. **Chosen — not built:** the event's own cost (team days × the line's rate) and its payback; it needs the number of people released and the hours of a team day, which the charter holds as words, and the project's line's wage read into the board, the paper and the report — said on the entry, and on the project report and the paper in one sentence (*The week's own cost — the people released × the days × the line's rate — is not worked out here.*), and Rowland's to confirm (the hours of a team day, and whether the days drawn or the days released count).

## 4. Inputs
None needed to start. Written (project notes `method:kaizen-event:<field>`): the sheet's fifteen (`lib/methodSheets/kaizen-event`); the board's — *First day of the event* (`start`, a date), each day's three (`day-see@n`, `day-change@n`, `day-measure@n`), *The day of the check* (`check-date`), the verdict (`check-verdict`), **the readings since the week, in order** (`check-readings`, numbers) and **which way is better** (`better`: lower · higher). Read: the project's studies and standards (the tools' sentences), its Actions (`owed`), the Gemba walk's freshness on its lines.

## 5. Data suitability
Said, never refused: no baseline once the week has begun; days gone by with nothing written; the week over with no measure after or no check booked; a standard written and nobody trained; an open action with nobody named; fewer than four readings since the week (*a trend needs 4 or more*); all readings the same (*no trend*); a word among the readings (named, left out); no way said as better (the trend said, its reading asked). The before and the after are words, so their difference is not worked out — the report says so and names Analysis for the test.

## 6. Calculations
Days: `dayDates` — working days from the first day. The check: thirty days after day five (or the last day with words), next working day; overdue when its day is before today and no verdict. Actions (`actionsOf`): raised = the project's open-or-done *next* and *found* items not of the method sheet; closed; open; late = open past their day; no owner; share = round(closed ÷ raised × 100). **The trend at the check** (`holdingOf`): x = 1…n, y = the readings; ρ = Pearson's r of their ranks (ties averaged); t = ρ √((n − 2)/(1 − ρ²)), p two-sided on n − 2; mean of the readings. **Independent cases:** `scripts/knowledge/cases-lean.py` (the record's) and SciPy 1.18 — the readings 25, 24, 28, 31, 30, 33: mean 28.5, ρ 0.8857, p 0.0188 → rising, the wrong way when lower is better; the same reversed: ρ −0.8857, falling; 24, 25, 24, 26, 25, 24: ρ 0.1234, p 0.816 → no steady trend; four readings 25, 24, 28, 31: ρ 0.80, p 0.20 → no steady trend; actions 11 raised, 8 closed → 73% (`kaizenReport.test.ts`).

## 7. Outputs
On the board: the line under the title (*1 of 5 days written. The 30-day check is on Mon 7 Dec 2026 — in 17 days.*); each day's state in words; the check's sentence and word (Holding · Slipping · Lost · Overdue · Not recorded); the trend's sentence, e.g. *6 readings since the week: mean 28.5, rising (Spearman's ρ 0.89 on their order, p 0.02) — the wrong way for this measure: the gain is creeping back.* On the dashboard: an overdue check in red with the next step to hold it; a Slipping or Lost verdict; Holding with no measure at 30 days; **Holding while the readings creep back**; the week over with no check booked or no measure after; no baseline; days not written; nobody trained; actions with nobody named.

**The report.** Two papers from the same module the board reads (`lib/kaizenEvent`). The **Kaizen event report** (Report ▾ on the project report page; `lib/report/kaizenPaper`): the charter, the week day by day (each day's state, what was seen, changed and measured), what the week found and changed, the new way, the 30-day check with its verdict in its word, the open actions, the measure at 30 days, **the readings since the week and the trend's sentence**, what the week bought, and the closing caution that every figure was counted, timed or written by a person. The **Project report** (`#/project/:id/method-report`): the event's six sections — the charter, the week day by day as a table, what the week changed (before and after side by side, the tools' own sentences and money), the new way, the actions carried to the check (raised, closed, share, open, late, nobody named, the late and unowned listed), and the 30-day check (its word, the day, the verdict, the measure at 30 days, **the readings' trend**) — with the dashboard's attention. A client may read and print both; nothing missing is made up.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
The day in hand open (today's, else the first not written), every other day one line with its state in words; once every day is written the check is the step in hand. Every box keeps its words when the person leaves it; the check is booked in one tap; the verdict and *which way is better* are one tap each; every control 44 px; one column on a phone.

## 9. Failure handling
No first day: the days have no dates and the check asks for the first day or a date. No tools used: each says *None used in this project yet* and offers Start. A client reads every box as words and has no box, chip or button. A reading that is not a number is named and left out. A check day typed in the past with no verdict is overdue, said in red with the days.

## 10. Integration
Records: project notes (`method:kaizen-event:*`) — no new noun, no migration; the project's studies and Actions, read only. Shows on: the method page (the board, the section boxes and their tools), the dashboard (`lib/methodInsights/kaizen-event`), the project report (`project/method/report/kaizen-event`), the Kaizen event report paper. Access: `useAccess` — `can.edit` writes and starts tools; a client reads. Sync: as project notes.
