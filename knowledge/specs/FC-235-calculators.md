# FC-235 — Calculators: the shape thirty sums share

**Catalogue:** FC-235 (the shape and its hub) · carries FC-009 kanban sizing, FC-183 safety stock and the reorder point, FC-131 what a sampling plan protects, FC-044 WIP and lead time (each its own spec) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/BUILD-SHAPES.md` ("Calculator — 30 entries"), `knowledge/MONEY.md`, the four methods' records · **Asked for:** Rowland, 25 October — "go through everything, return when everything's completed and functional inside the app" (`docs/TOOLS-BUILD-BRIEF.md`); the shape is the one `knowledge/BUILD-SHAPES.md` names first for building once and well · **Status:** built on `build-calc` (25 October; the integrator merges). **Written before the code.**

## 1. Purpose
A sum — how many kanbans, what a sampling plan protects, how long product waits — is done once on a phone or a spreadsheet; nobody can see what went into it, what it meant for the line, what it was worth, or whether the next one is better. The shape makes a sum a **saved record on a line, a project, a product or nothing**, with the figures put in (each with its unit and where it came from), the answer in a sentence and a few tiles, how it was worked out, what it does *not* say, the money (or the one sentence saying what to add), a paper, and a **before → after** once the figures are saved twice around a change. The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"8 kanbans of 60 trays — the shelf holds at most 480; the refill takes 2 hours, and at 1 hour it would be 4."*

**Scope.** This build covers the **shape**: the record (a `ToolStudy` of tool `calc`, `agreed.calc = { key, inputs, src?, before? }`, nothing derived stored); the **definition** every calculator is (`src/tools/calc/defs/<key>.ts`, found by `import.meta.glob`: its inputs with units, help and sources; its checks; its formula, thresholds, wording, caveats, money and — only where it earns one — picture; its worked examples from an independent source; its refused inputs); the page (the hub `#/calc`, one calculator `#/calc/:key`, one record `#/calc/:key/:id` — figures left, live answer right, a what-if, the saved before, Report ▾, rename and delete with Undo, access); the paper (one generic page from the definition's own result, in `lib/studiesPdf` via `lib/report/calcPaper`); the wiring (the one hub entry in the rail's Tools, the front door, a line's and a project's Tools, New ▾ and Jump to — read from the catalogue's registry; the line's Tools page one row "Calculators" counting this line's saved ones; the line report listing each saved calculation under its calculator's name); and the table-driven proof (`src/tools/calc/__tests__/calc.test.ts`, `scripts/acceptance/calc-shape.mjs`) that covers every definition with no change to either. It proves the shape with **four calculators** (FC-009, FC-183, FC-131, FC-044), each specified on its own. Not in this build, said on the hub's limits: the other twenty-six calculators of the shape (each is a definition, a spec, tests and a catalogue entry, added by the next builder); FC-129 designing a plan from the AQL and risks, FC-045 utilisation and variability (**not built**; FC-129's research is in `knowledge/methods/quality/acceptance-sampling-attributes.md`); comparing two *saved records* of one calculator with each other (the saved before is the comparison); a scored-table input (the ergonomics screens, FC-018, FC-141 — their lookup tables are those calculators' own).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead at a desk, the shift lead on a phone beside the line, an engineer in a meeting. (1) The packing line keeps running out of film: *Safety stock* from the last weeks' daily counts and the supplier's delivery days, the reorder point said, what holding it costs a year. (2) Trays to a packer: *Kanban sizing* at 2 hours to refill is 8; a SMED-style cut to 1 hour — saved first as the before — says 4 and what that takes off the shelf. (3) A goods-in check "take 50, allow 1": *what a plan protects* says what it lets through, with the producer's and the consumer's risk. (4) Cooked product in a chiller: *WIP and lead time* says 3 hours — 6% of the shelf life. A client reads any of them and takes the paper; a project's own calculations list under its Tools.

## 3. Method selection
Why one shape for many sums: every one is *inputs → a formula → a sentence with a threshold and a caveat → money*, and each differs only in the formula, its thresholds, its wording, its caveat and sometimes a picture — `knowledge/BUILD-SHAPES.md`. Alternatives considered: (a) one study tool per sum — thirty screens, thirty papers, and thirty times the wiring (rejected; `docs/STRUCTURE.md` §14 wants the catalogue to be the one list of tools); (b) a spreadsheet-like generic formula box — a template that lets anything be typed and nothing be trusted (rejected: `knowledge/STANDARD.md` point 1, "a shape shares plumbing and never a formula"). The shape therefore takes only what is the same, and a **definition** brings everything that is the method's own, with its formula in `src/lib/ie/` proved by independent cases. When a calculator is unsuitable for the data (a bell-shaped model for lumpy demand, a lot too small for a percentage) the definition says so in `check` or `caveats`, on the screen and on paper — the shape guarantees that every definition has at least one caveat and a worked refusal.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established:* each calculator's formula and thresholds are its own spec's, each with its source (Monden; Silver, Pyke & Thomas; the binomial and hypergeometric distributions; Little 1961). The shape itself holds no figure from a source.
- *Assumed:* a calculation's inputs are what a person typed or took from the line's cost model (`lib/cost`: the wage with on-costs, a pack's materials, the rated speed, the margin) — no figure is read from a machine (CLAUDE.md, "measured by people"); a result is worked out again from the inputs and the definition *as it is now*, so an improved definition changes what an old record says, never its figures.
- *Chosen (the builder, 25 October — the owner asked not to be asked):* (1) one calculator is one definition and one catalogue entry; a "second view" such as FC-183 beside FC-009 is a sibling calculator on the same hub group, linked from both pages (`see`), not a mode of the first — so each entry is provable alone; (2) the saved **before** is a copy of the inputs inside the same record (`agreed.calc.before`), its result worked out from them, rather than a second record — a before → after is then one page and one paper; (3) an input has **no default** unless it is Faultline's own said choice, and then the page says *"A choice, not a measurement"* and whose it is (a safety factor, a service level); (4) money inputs are optional and grouped under *In money — all optional*; a missing one is named in a sentence and nothing is withheld (`knowledge/MONEY.md` rule 5); (5) the shape offers the line's cost figures as a button ("Use Line 7's wage with on-costs: £14.70") and copies the figure into the inputs, saying where it came from, so a result never moves under a saved before when the line's wage changes; (6) `can.edit` types the figures, `can.remove` deletes, and **`can.agree` is not used** — a calculation holds no agreed limit; where one is agreed (a project's acceptance risk) it is that tool's, not a figure here; (7) an amber verdict is for a check the method itself supports (a risk above the customary 5% and 10%, a pile that grew), never a house threshold on a number the method does not judge; (8) no picture unless it is the answer (the OC curve); a reorder point, a kanban count and a lead time have none.

## 4. Inputs
Per definition (`Field`): an id, a label in the floor's words, a unit shown after the box, a kind (number, choice, text), min / max / whole-number rules, one line of help saying what to put in and where on the floor to find it, optional, money, an optional `showIf` (a choice that changes what is asked), an optional offer from the line's cost model (`from`: the wage with on-costs; a pack's materials; the rated speed; the margin a pack — each saying where to give it when the line has none), and `chosen` when a starting point is Faultline's own. The record is `agreed.calc.inputs` (a number kept as a number, "1,500" read as 1500), `src` (where an offered figure came from, for the paper's Source column) and `before`. The new record asks only *what is it of* and, optionally, the line; a project's calculation is on the project and its line.

## 5. Data suitability
Each box is checked on its own by the shape (needed and blank, below its minimum, above its maximum, not whole) — blocking, the answer withheld, the boxes still needed named — and across boxes by the definition's `check` (blocking, or a warning beside a result that still stands). A figure typed that is not a number is a blank. A client sees every figure disabled and is told so. A calculator whose definition is missing from this build (a record from a newer phone) keeps its figures untouched and says so, never throws.

## 6. Calculations
The shape does no sum of its own beyond (a) reading the inputs, (b) the change since the saved before for each tile with a number (`lib/lean` `since`: "8 kanbans fewer than the saved before", coloured only when the tile says which way is better), and (c) a what-if (the same definition run on the same inputs with one box changed). Every calculator's formulas, cases and worked examples are its own spec's and `scripts/knowledge/cases-calc.py` (SciPy / NumPy / hand-worked textbook figures, each named); the unit test `src/tools/calc/__tests__/calc.test.ts` runs *every* definition's examples and refusals.

## 7. Outputs
On the screen (the lean page): the band (the calculator's name, the record's name, the verdict in its colour); the tiles each *of* something and since the saved before; the sentence and what else it says; the checks beside it; the picture where the definition has one; *How it is worked out* (each step with its numbers and its method); *In money* (a sentence with its "because", the figures it is made of, or the sentence naming what to add); *What this does not say*; the saved before; the what-if; where it sits (the one Belongs to); Used for and the action plan; the closing line. On the paper (`lib/report/calcPaper`, the same result): the band with the verdict, the tiles with what moved, the sentence under *What it says*, what else it says and the checks, the picture drawn from the same description as the screen's, *The figures put in* (each with its unit and where it came from — typed, the line's set-up, or Faultline's starting point — a choice), *How it is worked out*, *In money*, *Before → after* (the before's sentence and the figures that changed), *What this does not say*, the action plan and the closing line.

**The report.** One generic calculation paper, on the report engine and the lean page, from the definition's own result: purpose and context (the calculator, the record's name, where it sits, the day), the data with their units and sources, the method and its working, the answer in the sentence and tiles, the picture, the money or what to add, the limits, and — when a before is saved — what changed. A list's paper (the hub's, a calculator's, a line's or project's) is each calculation a page; the line report prints each saved calculation under its calculator's name with its own blocks. It reads the same module as the screen; nothing missing is made up; a missing money input is said, never filled.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Start from the calculator's page or the hub card (≤ 3 taps to a first answer: New, a name, then the figures); the answer is worked out as each figure is typed, saved half a second after the last key (never a write for each); the phone is one column with every box and tile inside 390 px and thumb-sized; the money boxes fold away; the line's own figure is one tap away where it exists. The lean page's order throughout: where are we (tiles, sentence), why (the working, the checks), what are we doing (action plan, closing line).

## 9. Failure handling
A box left blank or out of range: no answer, *Waiting for the figures* naming each; a cross-box fault: the box says it; a doubtful input: a warning beside a standing answer. A definition that throws on figures it did not foresee shows *This could not be worked out from these figures* and the page stays up. A saved before that can no longer be worked out says so. A calculation whose calculator is not in this build keeps its figures. A client: read-only, told so, takes the paper. Offline: as every study (IndexedDB, synced by the engine).

## 10. Integration
**Record:** a `ToolStudy` with `tool: 'calc'` (already in `STUDY_TOOLS` and the cloud's `studies_tool_check` — `supabase/SHAPE_TOOLS.sql`); `agreed.calc` is part of the `agreed` jsonb the mapper already carries whole, so **no migration and no mapper change** (`src/cloud/__tests__/sync-schema.test.ts` unchanged and passing). **Where it shows (CLAUDE.md rule 5):** the rail's Tools, the front door, a line's and a project's Tools, New ▾ and Jump to list *Calculators* from the catalogue's registry (one entry, FC-235, `kind: study`, `reads: 'calc'`); a line's Tools page has one row counting this line's saved calculations with the latest finding (`lib/studyLinks` `studyLine`); a project's calculation is its own and lists in its Tools (`?project=`); the line report lists each saved calculation as a record ticked in by default, labelled with the calculator's title, with the section the calculator's own paper prints (`line/report/lineBundle` `itemsOn`); the studies lists' papers and the client report's evidence read it through `studyLine`; Delete says what goes (`lib/studyLinks` `studyRemovingSays`); the page is in its scope (`lib/scope`; the context bar *Factory › Line 7 › Tools › Calculators*). **Access:** `useAccess(projectId)` as every tool. **Pages:** `docs/PAGES.md` and `scripts/inventory/pages.mjs` have the hub, one calculator's list and a record; `scripts/smoke.mjs` opens the three; the seed (`src/dev/seed.ts`) has a kanban with a saved before, an OC curve and Little's law.

## 11. Verification
`src/tools/calc/__tests__/calc.test.ts` (every definition: sound, its examples exactly as the independent literals say, its refusals, a needed box blank stopping and a money box blank never, the same twice, the house's words, a picture inside its axes, a new record asking for figures; the shape's what-if, change since a before, offers, typing over, a missing calculator); `src/lib/ie/__tests__/pull.test.ts` (the four methods' arithmetic against SciPy and the textbooks); the catalogue and registry tests; `scripts/acceptance/calc-shape.mjs`, table-driven over every definition.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. A definition added as one file in `src/tools/calc/defs/` is listed on the hub, opens at `#/calc/<key>`, and is covered by the unit test and the acceptance script with no change to either — `calc.test.ts` (the registry loop) and `scripts/acceptance/calc-shape.mjs` (steps 0 and *a*, which read the registry).
2. Every definition's worked examples, typed through the real boxes, give the sentence, verdict, tone, tiles and money of the independent literals and of the definition's own computation in the page — `calc.test.ts` ("worked examples"); `scripts/acceptance/calc-shape.mjs` step *b*.
3. Every refused input is refused in the words it names and gives no answer — `calc.test.ts` ("refuses"); `calc-shape.mjs` step *c*.
4. A needed box blank stops the answer and names it; a blank money box never stops it and the money says what is missing — `calc.test.ts` ("stops, and says which"); `calc-shape.mjs` steps *a*, *b*.
5. The what-if changes one box, shows the answer and the money beside the one now, and keeps nothing until Make it so — `calc.test.ts` ("a what-if"); `calc-shape.mjs` step *d*.
6. The saved before reads the next figures as a before → after: each tile says what moved, coloured only where it knows which way is better — `calc.test.ts` ("the saved before"); `calc-shape.mjs` step *d*.
7. The paper, downloaded and read back, has the sentence, the tiles, every figure put in with its name and where it came from, the working, the money, what it does not say, the picture's title where there is one, and Before → after once saved — `calc-shape.mjs` step *e*.
8. The figures are kept after a reload; rename, delete (the ask says what goes) and Undo work where it sits — `calc-shape.mjs` steps *d*, *g*.
9. The phone (390 px): nothing off the side, every box thumb-sized, the picture inside — `calc-shape.mjs` step *f*.
10. A line's Tools page has one row *Calculators* (not a row a calculator), its saved ones counted and the latest said; the line report lists each as a ticked record and prints it — `calc-shape.mjs` step *L*.
11. A client reads a project's calculation read-only, is told so, and takes its paper — `calc-shape.mjs` step *C*.
12. The record needs no migration: `src/cloud/__tests__/sync-schema.test.ts` and `scripts/check-live-schema.mjs` unchanged. Nothing else broken: the catalogue and registry tests, smoke, the inventory.
Not yet proved: the two-device sync of a calculation (it is a study row; `scripts/sync-two-devices.mjs` is the integrator's gate, not run here).

## 12. Maintainability
**How to add a calculator** — create `src/tools/calc/defs/<key>.ts` (a `CalcDef`; copy `kanban.ts`), put its arithmetic in `src/lib/ie/<name>.ts` with a unit test against independent cases (a SciPy case in `scripts/knowledge/cases-calc.py` or a textbook figure with its source), write `knowledge/specs/FC-nnn-<name>.md` from the template before the code, and set its catalogue entry's `form: 'tool'`, `kind: 'study'`, `built.where: ['/calc/<key>']`, `built.code/tests`, `acceptance: ['calc-shape.mjs']`, no `app` face. Nothing else changes: not the router, the registry, the hub, the paper, the test or the script. The shape is in `src/tools/calc/` (types, calc, registry, format, the screen, form, view and picture) and `src/lib/report/calcPaper.ts`; shared pieces are reused (`lib/lean`, `ui/Lean`, `lib/report`, `lib/cost`). Left for later, each with its number: the other twenty-six calculators of `knowledge/BUILD-SHAPES.md`; a scored-table input (FC-207, FC-018, FC-141); FC-129 and FC-045.
