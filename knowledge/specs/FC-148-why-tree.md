# FC-147 / FC-148 — Choosing what to verify, and the why tree: the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-147 Fishbone with evidence on each cause, FC-148 Five whys (both Available today; this extends them — it adds no new entry and promotes nothing) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/rca/fishbone-5whys.md` (to be extended with the two practices below when built) · **Asked for:** Rowland, 1 November 2026: after the fishbone the person "can only pick one of them … no science up to that point … based on my perception … I'm going to take some of these, not all of them, and start a 5-why"; and "the whys could branch off, which then lead to multiple root causes … it's likely I have multiple sub-causes that ultimately lead to one … I need to know if I'm correct" · **Status:** draft — written 2 November; nothing is built until Rowland says go (CLAUDE.md, "Nothing new is built until Rowland says go"); the four decisions at the foot are his.

## 1. Purpose
Between the fishbone and the five whys the method has a step that every source names and the app does not yet make: the team **chooses which suspected causes to check, checks them, and only then drills the ones that survive** — and the drill is a **tree**, not a single chain, because one "why" can have several true answers and several branches can end in one shared root. Today a person can start the whys on any cause, confirmed or not, and a why can have only one answer (a second answer has to be written as a second cause on the bone, which loses the tree).

The sentence it lets one side of the project say to the other that they could not say before: *"Of the nine causes we thought of, we checked three; two held; here are the four roots they lead to — two of them are the same root — and here is the evidence for each."*

**Scope.** Three parts, each shippable alone, in this order:
- **Part A — pick what to verify.** On the fishbone, each suspected cause is rated (how likely, how much of the problem it could explain), the few worth checking are ticked, each ticked cause is given the check that would confirm or rule it out (what, who, by when), and the result is recorded. The whys open on a cause only once it is *confirmed*.
- **Part B — the why tree.** Any why can have several answers; a branch ends in a root (something that can be put right so it does not come back) or joins a root that another branch already reached; the "therefore" read-back runs per branch; the fixed "five" is a prompt, not a limit.
- **Part C — the Pareto picks the start** (smaller, optional, Decisions for Rowland, 3): the Pareto already ranks by count, time and cost; this adds a "start here" mark on the vital-few bar that costs the line most, which needs the constraint and the margin the app already holds.

Left out, on purpose: any scoring that is not the person's own judgement (no weighted-score formula is invented — see *Established · assumed · chosen*); a fault tree (FC-152, built); stratification (FC-153, built); an automatic choice of causes by the app — the app suggests, the person decides, as the whole method does (`docs/DIRECTION.md`: no AI is the driver); changes to the Pareto's data (FC-190 and the stop log are untouched).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The line's team in a meeting at a desk or on the floor on a phone; one person types, the team talks.
1. *Film breaks on the Bagger.* The fishbone has nine causes across six bones, five of them the app's own suggestions from the stops. The team rates them, ticks three to check, writes "pull the splice log for 4 weeks — Sam — Friday" against the splice cause, and the result comes back "14 of 19 breaks were at a splice": confirmed, counted. Only then does *Why?* open on it.
2. *"Poor training."* The cause is confirmed (observed: two new starters on nights could not name the changeover steps). *Why?* has three answers — no written standard, no sign-off, the trainer has no time. The third goes one more level to "training is not planned into the roster"; the first two each go one more level to the same words, and the team marks them as the *same* root. Roots: two, and one is shared; one countermeasure on the shared root fixes both branches.
3. *A cause that does not hold.* The check finds 2 of 19 at a splice: ruled out, kept on the fish with its evidence (so the next person does not re-ask), and the whys never open.

## 3. Method selection
The practice is the one the sources agree on; Faultline's choice is only how to show it.
- *Cause selection after a fishbone.* Brainstorm for breadth, **select the most likely** (multi-voting or a cause-and-effect matrix), then **verify with data or by going to see** before the whys. A vote is a priority, not proof (ASQ's cause-and-effect guidance; Ishikawa 1982; the verify step is Toyota's *go and see*, genchi genbutsu — Ohno 1988, Liker 2004). *Alternatives considered:* a weighted cause-and-effect matrix with scores multiplied (rejected as the default — it dresses opinion as arithmetic, which the fishbone-5whys record already warns against); affinity (KJ) grouping (the six M's already are the groups).
- *The why tree.* The five whys is a rule of thumb, not a rule; stop when the answer is something that can be put right so it does not come back; the technique "can be branched to yield multiple root causes" and each branch is questioned until it reaches a root (Wikipedia, *Five whys*; Mind Tools, *5 Whys*; Kainexus, *5 root cause analysis techniques*). A single chain is the known weakness: it follows one line and different people reach different roots (same sources; ThinkReliability, *Top criticisms of the 5-why approach*). Several branches ending at a shared root is the normal shape of a systemic cause (training, scheduling, maintenance planning); no named standard prescribes how to *draw* it, so the "same root" link is Faultline's choice and is said to be.
- *Unsuitable:* a problem whose cause is already known and obvious (use "Just do it", already offered); a problem with interacting failures that need probabilities (use FC-152, the fault tree — the page says so in one line); where and when it happens is unclear (Is / Is not first — already on the problem).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established:* the sequence brainstorm → select → verify → drill (ASQ, Ishikawa, Ohno/Liker as above); the five whys branches and has no fixed depth (Wikipedia, Mind Tools, Kainexus); a "therefore" read-back from root to effect is the check that a chain holds (Ohno; already built); a why that stops at a person should be asked "what let that happen?" (already built).
- *Assumed:* that the person rating likelihood has enough knowledge of the process to rate three levels (said on the screen next to the rating: "your judgement, not a measurement").
- *Faultline's choice (to be agreed — Decisions for Rowland):* ratings are three plain levels each (*Unlikely · Possible · Likely*; *A little · Some · Most* of the problem) and **no multiplied score** — the list is ordered by the two words, likelihood first, then share, and the person ticks; the whys open only on *confirmed* causes; "same root" is a link between two branch ends; the depth prompt appears at the fifth level but never stops the person.

## 4. Inputs
Every field is optional until the step it belongs to; nothing blocks the existing flow of a cause that already has whys (§9).
- *Part A, per cause:* **How likely** (Unlikely · Possible · Likely), **How much of the problem** (A little · Some · Most), **Check it** (tick), **The check** (words: what would show it, who, by when — three boxes), **What the check found** (words) and the cause's **status** (suspected · confirmed · ruled out — exists today) with its **how known** (measured · counted · observed · reported — exists today; the evidence grades above, `Grade`).
- *Part B, per why:* **Why?** answers (one or several, each a text), each with its grade (exists), **This is a root** (tick: "it can be put right so it does not come back") or **Same root as…** (pick another branch end), who and when (exist).
- *Read from the line, never asked again:* the problem's number and its money (the head of the fish), the stops and snags that suggested a cause (`lib/fishbone`), the line's constraint and margin (Part C).

## 5. Data suitability
No sample sizes — this is judgement made visible. What is checked: a cause with no rating is listed after the rated ones, not hidden; a confirmed cause with grade *reported* (hearsay) is allowed but says so ("confirmed on what was told — go and see before spending"); a root with no countermeasure is flagged on the report; a branch that ends at a person gets the existing "What let that happen?" prompt; a tree of one branch and one level says it is a single chain.

## 6. Calculations
There is no statistic. The only computations are structural and are unit-tested against hand-worked trees:
- *Order of suspected causes:* likelihood (Likely > Possible > Unlikely), then share (Most > Some > A little), then the order they were added. No score is multiplied.
- *Tree reading:* depth of a branch = number of whys from the cause to its end; **roots** = branch ends marked root, counting a shared root once; **branches** = leaf count; **orphans** = whys not reachable from the cause (an error state, repaired by re-attaching to the cause).
- *Hand-worked case (the "poor training" tree, scenario 2):* cause C; whys W1 *no written standard*, W2 *no sign-off*, W3 *trainer has no time*; W1→W4 *nobody owns the standard*, W2→W5 *sign-off sheet not issued*, W3→W6 *training is not planned into the roster*; W4 and W5 end with *same root as W6*. Expected: branches 3, roots 1 (shared by 3 branches), depth 2 on each branch, a countermeasure on W6 covers all 3 branches; with W5 left unmarked: roots 1, open branch 1 ("W5 has not reached a root").
- Independent check: the tree functions are re-implemented in `scripts/knowledge/` (a second implementation reading the same JSON) and compared on the case above and on two others (a linear chain of five; two unrelated roots).

## 7. Outputs
On screen and on paper from one model (`lib/sixm` / `lib/fishbone`, a new `lib/whyTree`):
- *Fishbone:* each cause mark shows its check state in words — *Not rated · To check · Checking (by Friday) · Confirmed (counted) · Ruled out*; "Add to the fishbone" suggestions unchanged.
- *The step page "What to check":* the suspected causes in the order above, each with its two ratings, a tick, and the check boxes; a sentence at the top: "3 of 9 causes ticked to check — 1 confirmed, 1 ruled out, 1 checking (due Friday)". Colour only on the abnormal (a check past its day, red; none on the rest).
- *The why tree:* an indented outline on the phone, a drawn tree on a laptop; each node its words and grade; roots marked **Root**; shared roots drawn once with the branches pointing at it; a branch not yet at a root marked **Open**; the "therefore" read-back printed under each branch.
- *The sentence:* "Poor training: confirmed (observed). 3 branches, 1 root — training is not planned into the roster — shared by all three. Countermeasures: 0 of 1."
- *Report (the 6M report and the project report's 6M sections):* the problem; the causes thought of, which were checked, what each check found, the ruled-out causes with their evidence; for each confirmed cause its tree as an outline, its roots, and the countermeasure on each root; open branches listed as "not yet at a root".
- *Money:* none of its own. The head of the fish already says its hours a week in money by the one cost model, and a countermeasure its worth (FC-146); this spec adds none and names no missing input (`knowledge/MONEY.md`: the fishbone is not a cost method).
- *What it does not claim:* that a confirmed cause is *the* cause; that the ratings are measurements; that a tree with one root has no other contributors.

**The report.** The 6M problem report and the project report's 6M sections gain a *Causes thought of and checked* table (cause · bone · how likely · how much · check · found · status) and a *Why tree* section per confirmed cause (outline, roots, shared roots, open branches, countermeasure on each root). Purpose and scope are the problem's, as today; the method line states the practice and its sources in one sentence; limits state that ratings are judgement. It is drawn from the same model as the screen by `lib/report` blocks; nothing missing is made up (a cause never rated says "not rated"). Formats: the PDF the 6M report already makes.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Phone first, one hand: rating is two taps per cause; ticking is one; writing the check is three short boxes (voice-note capable like the other typed boxes); adding a branch is one tap on a why ("Another answer"); marking a root or joining one is one tap. Meeting mode at a desk shows the ordered list and the tree large. Taps for the scenario-1 flow from the fishbone to a confirmed cause: ≤ 10; from a confirmed cause to a drawn three-branch tree: ≤ 14. The lean page's order is kept (the sentence first, the picture, what it means, the action plan = the countermeasures on the roots, the closing line).

## 9. Failure handling
- *Existing data:* every cause that already has whys keeps them and stays editable; its chain is read as a tree with one branch (`of` absent ⇒ each why answers the one before it). Such a cause with status *suspected* is **not locked** — it shows "drilled before it was checked" and the check boxes, nothing is lost or hidden. Nothing is migrated in the database.
- *No ratings:* the order is the order added; the page says "nothing rated yet" and the ticks still work.
- *A tick with no check written:* allowed (the check is the team's to word later) but the cause shows "To check — no check written".
- *Two devices adding branches to the same cause at once:* the cause is merged by id and its whys by id (see §10), so two different branches both survive; two edits to the same why's words are last-writer as every text field is (a limit, said in the tool's page).
- *A cycle or an orphan (an `of` pointing at nothing):* the tree is repaired on read by attaching the orphan to the cause and saying so; the app never loses a why.
- *Offline:* works as the rest of the fishbone does (local first, syncs after).
- *A client:* reads the tree and the checks, writes nothing (`can.edit`); the data's suggestions stay hidden from a client, as today.
- *Misuse:* stopping at a person → the existing prompt; marking a root with no words → refused with a sentence; "same root as" pointing at a branch of another cause → not offered.

## 10. Integration
- *Records:* no new table, no new noun (BLUEPRINT §2 unchanged). `Cause` (`src/lib/sixm.ts`) gains optional `likely`, `explains`, `check {what, who, by}`, `found`; `Why` gains optional `of` (the why it answers; absent on legacy chains), `root` (bool), `sameAs` (another why's id). Deliberately later: the 8D reuse; a probability on a branch (that is the fault tree, FC-152); the Pareto ranking in money (part C, below); a printed *A3 root cause* panel drawn from the tree (A3, built, can read `lib/whyTree` when asked).

**Part C, in detail.** The Pareto bars are ranked by the person's chosen measure (count · time · cost; cost ranks like time because it is the time priced). Add, beside the vital-few cut, one mark — *Start here* — on the vital-few bar whose lost time sits **at the line's constraint** and costs most, taken from the constraint (Line capacity or the problem's source) and the margin the cost model already holds; where those are missing the mark is not drawn and the sentence says which input is missing (nothing invented). Count, time and cost stay selectable and unchanged; the other vital-few bars are listed as *Next*, each still one tap to open. Proved by `scripts/acceptance/pareto-start.mjs` (a bar at the constraint beats a larger one off it; no constraint ⇒ no mark and the input named; the chosen measure unchanged); unit test on the ranking function.

**Decisions for Rowland (nothing below is guessed).**

2. **The ratings:** three plain levels each (above), no multiplied score — or no ratings at all and just the tick? Recommended: the three levels, because the order they give is what lets a team agree quickly which to check.
3. **Part C (Pareto "Start here"):** build it with this, later, or not? It changes how the Pareto reads, so it is yours to say.
4. **The word for a shared root:** "Same root as …" (proposed) or "Joins …".
