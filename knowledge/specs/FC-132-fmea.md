# FC-132 — Process FMEA with an action priority (a register of cards on the scored shape)

**Catalogue:** FC-132 Process FMEA with action priority (reads FC-133 Control plan, which reads it back). **Knowledge:** `quality.fmea`. **Programme:** `docs/METHODS.md` §11 (DMAIC Control, 8D D4 and D7). **Asked for:** Rowland, 1 November 2026 — *"I want all tools made that get used in the projects."* **Status:** built 1 November 2026 (branch `m6-analysis`).

## 1. Purpose
Before the failure, not after: a team walks each step of the process and asks what could go wrong (the failure mode), what it would do (the effect, rated for severity), why it would happen (the cause, rated for how often) and whether the controls now in place would catch it (rated for detection). The ratings set a priority; the high ones get an action — a prevention or a better detection — and the control plan is written from what remains. The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"Line 2 wrapper: 7 failure modes; 1 High — the date code (S 7 · O 5 · D 7) has no action yet; 5 High or Medium modes still have neither an action nor a written reason."*

**Scope.** A process FMEA is a register on the scored shape (`/scored/fmea`, a ranking per line or project): a card a failure mode — step, failure mode, effect, cause, prevention now, detection now, **severity · occurrence · detection each 1 to 10** picked against anchors written for food manufacturing, an **action priority** High · Medium · Low from a stated rule (below), an action with who and by when and the day it was done, **a written reason when there is no action**, the three ratings again after the action with the priority now, a cost of one occurrence (optional), a food-safety tick (the row belongs in the HACCP plan too), and a priority that can be **set by hand from the AIAG-VDA handbook** by a team that holds it. The register is ranked by priority, then severity, occurrence, detection — never by their product. Not in this build: the **AIAG-VDA 2019 action-priority table** (licensed; not reproduced; see §3); the seven-step structure and function analysis (the steps are typed, not read from the value stream map or the project's stages); an action on the project's **Actions page** and in Needs you (the action is a field of the card; wiring it into `lib/portfolio` is a follow-up); the HACCP plan (FC-168); a design FMEA.

## 2. Users and scenarios
A quality engineer and the line's operators round a table with the scales in front of them; the engineer at a desk afterwards. (1) A wrapper: seal, reel, print, metal detector, date code, weight, splice — seven modes. (2) A packaging change: the new film's failure modes before the first run. (3) After an action: the reel barcode scan is in, the rating comes down, the priority with it, the evidence written.

## 3. Method selection
`quality.fmea`: S, O and D rated 1–10 against anchors, a priority from them, an action, a re-rating after it is done. **The priority.** The AIAG–VDA FMEA Handbook (1st ed., 2019) replaced the RPN (S × O × D) with an **action priority** read from a table; the table is the handbook's and is licensed, and neither the handbook nor an authoritative copy could be read from this environment (web search returned excerpts and vendor pages whose tables are garbled or disagree — one gives *severity 9–10 with occurrence 1* as Medium, one reads a *severity 1* row as High, one gives *severity 2, occurrence 2, detection 3* as Low). What the sources agree on is the principle: severity weighs most, then occurrence, then detection; three levels; **High** means improve prevention or detection, *or document why the current controls are acceptable*; Medium should; Low may. The record's reconstruction "from memory" is therefore **not coded**. This build uses **Faultline's own rule**, shown to the person in full, built only on that principle, and overridable row by row.
*Alternatives and when not:* food-safety hazards → the HACCP plan (the card has a tick that points to it); a failure that has already happened → fishbone, 8D; equipment criticality → FC-064; a top-down logic of one serious event → fault tree.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
*Established:* the FMEA method and its S/O/D; the older RPN and the reason the handbook retired it (a severity-10 failure with occurrence 1 and detection 1 has an RPN of 10, one of 100 with a hazardless S 4 · O 5 · D 5 — `quality.fmea`); that the handbook's priority is a lookup that weights severity, then occurrence, then detection, in three levels (public summaries; the table itself not read).
*Assumed:* a cross-functional team rated each mode with the scales in front of it; a rating is re-made when the control changes; the controls named are the ones really in place.
*Chosen by Faultline — not the handbook's, and not yet put to Rowland:* **the anchors** for the three scales (written in food-manufacturing words; occurrence by about 1-in-n of packs or events); **the rule**: *step 1* severity band — 9–10 critical, 7–8 major, 4–6 moderate, 1–3 minor; *step 2* the strength of the controls from occurrence (O) and detection (D) — O of 8 or more is **weak** whatever D (catching what should have been prevented is not a control); otherwise O 1–3 is strong with D 1–6 and middling with D 7–10, O 4–6 is strong with D 1–3, middling with D 4–6, weak with D 7–10, O 7 is middling with D 1–3 and weak with D 4–10; *step 3* critical → weak or middling High, strong Medium (**never Low**); major → weak High, middling Medium, strong Low; moderate → weak Medium, else Low; minor → always Low. It is a lookup and is **monotone** (raising any rating never lowers the priority; checked over all 1,000 combinations). **No RPN** is computed, shown or ranked by. A row ticked food-safety, and every row of severity 8 or more, is said to belong on the control plan whatever its priority. Late = an action with a day before today and no done day. An after-rating counts only once the action is marked done (before that it would be a prediction).
*Money (`knowledge/MONEY.md` FC-132):* none of its own; **a failure mode's cost when known** (typed, £ one occurrence costs) is said beside its priority and never used to rank; a High mode without a cost is named in one sentence and nothing waits on it.

## 4. Inputs
Per card: step, failure mode (the card's name), effect, cause, prevention now, detection now (text); severity, occurrence, detection (1–10, a pick); action, who, by when, done on (day), why no action, evidence; the three ratings after; priority from the handbook (optional); what one occurrence costs (£, optional); a food-safety tick. A register is started on a line or a project (a name and a line); cards are added and taken off with Undo. A client reads and changes nothing (`can.edit`, as the scored register's).

## 5. Data suitability
A card is ranked only when it has a failure mode and all three ratings, whole numbers 1–10; a half-rated card is listed as *not rated yet* and not ranked; a register with none rated says what to rate. Ratings out of range are refused by the pick; typed data is trimmed.

## 6. Calculations
`src/tools/fmea/fmea.ts`: `severityBand`, `controlStrength`, `actionPriority`, `readRow` (the priority, the hand-set priority, the after-priority, late, needs an action), `rankRows`, `fmeaAnalyse` (the result, the table). **Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-m6-analysis.py`, written with explicit conditions where the app uses tables; unit tests `src/tools/fmea/__tests__/fmea.test.ts`): all 1,000 combinations — hash `49929e6a9c63d98c`, H 244 · M 249 · L 507, monotone in S, O and D; the leaker S9 O3 D4 → Medium; the reel S8 O2 D9 → Medium; the smudge S3 O9 D8 → Low (an RPN would rank it above both: 216 against 108 and 144); metal detection S10 O2 D3 → Medium; the date code S7 O5 D7 → **High**; the weight S7 O4 D2 → Low; the splice S6 O7 D8 → Medium; S10 O1 D1 → Medium; S9 O8 D1 → High; S1 O10 D10 → Low. The worked register (seven modes): H 1 · M 4 · L 2, in the order date code, metal detector, leaker, reel, splice, weight, smudge.

## 7. Outputs
The lean page: the band (the verdict — *1 High*, *3 Medium*, *All Low*), five tiles (Failure modes · High · Medium · Low · Need an action), the sentence (the counts; the High modes first, each with its ratings and its action, owner and day, or "controls accepted, reason written", or "no action yet"; how many High or Medium modes have neither an action nor a reason; how many actions are late), its money lines, *How the priority is decided* (the rule in four lines), the cards, *What this does not say*, the closing line. **Colour:** a priority is a **word** with weight (High bold with a heavy edge, Medium a medium edge, Low quiet) — never a state hue, so it can never be read as red/amber/green of the five states; the only red is an action that is late. **Never claimed:** that the priority is the handbook's; that a rating is a measurement; that a Low is safe; that an FMEA by one person at a desk is complete.

**The report.** The paper (`src/lib/report/scoredPaper.ts`, the report engine): the band and tiles, the sentence, the register as a table — # · step · failure mode · effect · cause · controls now · S · O · D · priority (marked *handbook* where set by hand, *HACCP* where ticked) · action, who, by when, done and the priority now or LATE — the money lines, *How the priority is decided*, *What this does not say* and the limits. It reads the same module as the screen.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Cards that stack to one column on a phone; the three ratings as picks with the anchor said under the chosen one; the long fields folded (*Action, and the priority from a handbook*); controls a thumb's size; a failure mode typed at the machine and rated later (it is listed *not rated yet*).

## 9. Failure handling
Nothing rated, a half-rated card, a rating out of range (not offered), an action past its day (late, red, said), a done day with no after-ratings (the priority stays as it was), a hand-set priority that differs from the rule (both said), a client's view (read only), an empty register (says what to do).
