# FC-215 — Critical path and float in a project plan: the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-215 (the stage gate FC-073 and its pace forecast FC-078 are the neighbours it reads beside) · **Knowledge:** `projects.critical-path` (full, textbook) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem, "nobody knows which slipping task moves the handover"; the stage-gate path's *Install* step lists it as *critical path is not built* (`docs/METHODS.md` §11, 31 October: build every method's tool) · **Status:** built as a calculator on `m5-stagegate`; **partial** — not yet wired to the stage-gate plan. **Written before the code.**

## 1. Purpose
Say which tasks of a project decide its end date and how many days each of the others can slip before it does, from the tasks, their days and what each must wait for: *"The last task cannot finish before day 33. The critical path is Deliver labeller → Install labeller → SAT labeller → Line trials → Handover: a day lost on any of them is a day later at the end. The other 3 tasks can slip first — the tightest, Deliver filler, has 5 days of float."* — the sentence the site lead can say to the OEM or the sponsor that they could not say before: *this delivery is late and it moves the handover; that one is late and it does not.*

**Scope.** The critical path method (Kelley & Walker 1959) over a **typed network** of up to 16 tasks: for each a letter, an optional name, its working days and the letters of the tasks it waits for (finish to start). It gives the earliest end, every critical path (more than one when chains tie), each task's early and late start and finish and its total float, the tasks within a chosen number of days of critical, the picture of the plan as it can run, **what one task being late does to the end** (the end moves by max(0, late − float)), and — when the person gives what a day of delay costs — that in money. **Not here, and said on the page:** reading the network from a stage-gate project (the plan holds each machine's stages and days but **no relation saying what a stage waits for** — storing it is a schema change, not made; this slice changes no stage-gate screen or paper); the machine-by-machine critical chain and its place on the plan and the client report (FC-073, after that relation exists); dates, calendars and shutdowns; lags, overlaps and start-to-start relations; resource limits; progress (remaining days re-run from today — re-typed here); three-point or probabilistic durations (PERT, schedule risk); the handover forecast from the pace of tests run (FC-078, a different sum that should agree with it).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The site lead or project engineer on a new-line project, at a desk or in a steering meeting, typing the plan's chains once. (1) *The record's new line:* a filler's chain (deliver 10 → install 5 → FAT/SAT 6) and a labeller's (deliver 14 → install 4 → SAT 8), then trials 5 and handover 2 — the end is day 33, the labeller's chain decides it, the filler's has 5 days of float; the labeller's delivery 3 days late moves the handover 3 days, the filler's does not. (2) *Two chains tie* — both are critical and two tasks one and two days from it are named, amber. (3) *A slip inside its float* costs nothing yet. (4) *Money:* the same slip at £2,500 a day of delay is £7,500.

## 3. Method selection
CPM is the exact arithmetic of "what decides the end": two passes over the network, float = late start − early start (`knowledge/methods/projects/critical-path.md`). Alternatives from the record: the handover forecast from the pace of tests already run (`commissioning.ramp-up`, FC-078 — a different question, from evidence rather than a plan), the gates and their contents (`commissioning.stage-gate`), the readiness list, the tasks as actions (`visual.action-tracking`). PERT/Monte Carlo durations are not built and not claimed.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established:* the forward pass ES = max EF of what a task waits for (0 at the start), EF = ES + d; the end = max EF; the backward pass LF = min LS of what waits for it (the end for the last), LS = LF − d; total float = LS − ES; critical where it is none (Kelley & Walker 1959; PMBOK Guide 7th ed., schedule network analysis; Lock 2013). A slip d on a task with float f moves the end by max(0, d − f) (the record). Independent cases: `scripts/knowledge/cases-calc.py` §FC-215, **by listing every start-to-end path** (the longest is the end; a task's float is the end less the longest path through it) — not by the two passes the module runs.
- *Assumed:* every wait is finish to start; days are working days from day 0 with no calendar; each task's days are fixed; what a task waits for has been typed completely (a wait left out makes a task look free — said in the caveats and in the help); resources are not limited.
- *Chosen (the builder, 31 October — the owner asked for the build to go fast and not to be asked):* **near critical** is a number the person can change, starting at **2 days** — said on the box as "a choice, not a measurement" (the method's own line is exactly none; "within a few days" is the record's wording) — a task with float above none and up to that is named, and the verdict is **amber** when any is; critical is never red (a state colour is a failure, and a critical task is the plan working as drawn); a task is a **letter A to P** so a wait is typed as letters (no free-text name matching); up to 16 tasks (the record's worked case has 8; a plan of more is split by machine); a task with float is listed first by its tightest float, ties in the order typed; **money** is a day's delay typed by the person, never filled in: contribution not earned for a day plus any standing charge (MONEY.md rows T and C) — not derived from the line's margin because the hours a day a new line will run are not held anywhere (a limit, not a gap in the method).

## 4. Inputs
Per task A to P (a task appears once the one above has its days): **Task X** — optional name (text, ≤ 40); **Task X takes** — working days (number ≥ 0, half days allowed, 0 is a milestone; required for Task A, optional after it); **Task X waits for** — optional letters ("C, D", "c d", "C and D"). Then **Call float this small near critical** — days (number ≥ 0, optional, starts at 2, a choice); **What if this task is late** — a letter, optional; **Late by** — days, optional; and, under *In money — all optional*, **What one day of delay to the end costs** — £ a day (number ≥ 0, optional, never filled in; the sponsor or finance has it). Nothing is read from a line: the cost model has no figure for a project's cost of delay.

## 5. Data suitability
The network must be a network: a wait for a letter that is not a task (A to P) is refused; a wait for a lettered task that has no days yet is left out and said; a **loop** (A waits for B, B for A) is refused with the loop named ("B → A → B"); a task waiting for itself is a loop; all tasks at 0 days is refused ("no path to find"); a negative number of days is refused. A late task that is not a task is refused; one with no days is left out and said; one task late without its days (or the days without the task) is said, not blocked. Two chains with the same end are both critical, and both are named. A very short plan (one task) is still a plan: that task is the critical path.

## 6. Calculations
Forward pass in dependency order (Kahn), ES = max over waits of EF (0 for none), EF = ES + d, end = max EF; backward pass in reverse, LF = min over successors of LS (end for none), LS = LF − d; float = LS − ES with floating-point dust removed to 10⁻⁹ days (0.1 + 0.2 is 0.3); critical where float is none; **critical paths** are the chains of critical tasks where each task's ES equals the one before's EF, from each critical task that waits for no such task to one finishing at the end (`lib/ie/criticalPath`). A slip re-runs both passes with the task's days + d: `moved` = the new end − the end (equal to max(0, d − f) for one task, proved for every task of three networks and four sizes of slip). Money: a day on the critical path = the typed £ a day; a slip = moved days × £ a day. **Independent cases** (`cases-calc.py` P1–P5, each a unit test in `criticalPath.test.ts` and a worked example here): P1 the record's network — end 33; B-D-F-G-H critical; A, C, E 5 days' float; ES/EF/LS/LF of G 26/31/26/31 and of E 15/21/20/26; P2 — end 19; A-B-D-G-H critical; C 5, E 6, F 5; P3 — end 12; two critical paths A-C-E-G-H and A-C-F-H; B 1 and D 2; P4 — B late 3: 36, A late 3: 33, A late 8: 36 (= 33 + 8 − 5); P5 — 3 days × £2,500 = £7,500.

## 7. Outputs
Verdict "33 days" (amber when a task is near critical); the sentence above; under it the near-critical task(s) ("Watch Task B (1 day) and Task D (2 days) — within 2 days of critical, so a slip that uses up their float makes them critical too.") and the late task's reading ("If Deliver filler is 8 days late it uses all 5 days of its float and the end moves 3 days, to day 36."); tiles *Earliest end*, *On the critical path* ("of 8 tasks"), *Least float off it*, *Near critical*, *If it slips*, *A day late costs*; the working (the two passes and the float in words, then each task's days, early and late start and finish, float, critical, and what it waits for); **the picture** — each task a bar from its earliest start to its earliest finish (dark when critical), its float a dashed tail, the end marked, the same description drawn on the page and the paper, because the plan as a drawing is the thing the method gives; four caveats (the network is the method; estimates and near-critical paths; finish-to-start and working days only; typed, not read from a project); the money — *"Each day the critical path slips costs £2,500 — a day's delay to the end, at the figure you gave. Deliver labeller 3 days late moves the end 3 days: £7,500."* — or, with no cost given, the sentence saying what to give and nothing else lost; the closing line ("Day 33 is the earliest end. 5 tasks of 8 have no float and decide it — chase those first; the other 3 can slip, the tightest by 5 days.").

**The report.** The generic calculation paper (FC-235, `lib/report/calcPaper`): band with the title and verdict, tiles, the sentence and its readings, the picture, the figures put in (the tasks, their days, what each waits for, the watch line marked "a choice, not a measurement", the late task, the cost), the working (every task's passes), the money or what to give, the caveats, Before → after when a saved before exists (a re-planned network read against the one before — the earliest end and the near-critical count), the closing line. It reads the same module as the screen. Nothing is made up: a missing cost is named, never filled.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
A desk or meeting tool more than a phone one: eight tasks are 24 boxes, each task opening when the one above has its days; the letters make a wait three keystrokes; on a phone the boxes stack and the picture scales down with the page (nothing off the side). The what-if is two boxes, so the question "if the labeller is late?" is answered live without disturbing the plan; the saved before keeps the plan as it stood for the next re-run.

## 9. Failure handling
Every refusal above is said in words under the box it belongs to, and the result does not show until the network works ("Waiting for the figures"). A cost left blank: the answer stands, one sentence says what to give. A client reads the saved calculation and changes nothing. A calculator improved later re-reads old saved figures with the new wording (FC-235's rule).

## 11. Verification
`criticalPath.test.ts` (P1–P5 against the path-listing cases, order independence, milestones, half-day dust, the refusals, the slip rule for every task of three networks); `calc.test.ts` (the shape's sound-definition, examples, refusals, blank boxes, house words, picture-inside-its-axes, record and before tests, run over this definition with no change); `scripts/acceptance/calc-shape.mjs` (table-driven over every definition: the examples typed through the real boxes, the paper downloaded and read back, the phone, rename/delete/Undo, reload, before → after, the what-if); `python3 -I scripts/knowledge/cases-calc.py`.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The end, every critical path and each task's float equal the independent path-listing cases P1–P3 — `criticalPath.test.ts` ("the two passes"); `calc.test.ts` examples 1, 2 and 4; `calc-shape.mjs` step *b* (the sentence and tiles typed through the real boxes).
2. A task late by d moves the end by max(0, d − float), exactly, for every task — `criticalPath.test.ts` ("a slip"); examples 1, 3 and 4 (+3 days; +3 days with 5 of float used; no change inside the float).
3. Two tied chains are both critical and both named; a task within the watch line is named and turns the verdict amber; the line is a stated choice — example 2 and 4 (`warns`/tone); `calc-shape.mjs` step *a* (the choice says it is a choice, not a measurement).
4. A loop, a wait for a task that is not there, a plan of nothing but zeros, a negative number of days, a late task that is not a task, and no days at all are refused in the words named — `calc.test.ts` ("refuses what it should"); `calc-shape.mjs` step *c*.
5. The money: a day and a slip in pounds (3 × £2,500 = £7,500) equal P5; a slip inside its float costs nothing yet; no cost given names what to give and the answer stands — examples 1, 3 and 4; `calc.test.ts` ("stops… never for a money box").
6. The picture's bars, tails and end lie inside its axes and it is the same description on the page and the paper — `calc.test.ts` ("draws a picture… inside its own axes"); `calc-shape.mjs` (the paper read back).
7. Paper, phone, rename, delete, Undo, reload, before → after, what-if — `calc-shape.mjs` steps *d*–*g*.
8. Existing behaviour unchanged: no stage-gate screen or paper moves — the stage-gate acceptance scripts and the paper comparison are the lead's to run at the merge (not run in this slice).
Not proved / not built: reading the network from a stage-gate project and showing the critical machine on the plan and the client report (needs the stored relation, §10); progress re-runs from today; calendars; the handover forecast and this agreeing on a project (FC-078).
