# FC-056 — Speed loss: running against ideal speed, slow running told from short stops

**Catalogue:** FC-056 (a mode of FC-049 Loss count — it lives on a loss count's page under *Top causes* and on that count's paper; the stops counted by a tap are FC-055's reliability study, offered here, never re-counted) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/equipment/short-stops-speed.md` (full, textbook), `docs/CAPACITY.md` §5–6, `knowledge/MONEY.md` §4 (FC-056: **T** packs short of ideal speed × contribution, and **L** the labour spread over fewer packs) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem — *"the line runs, but slower than it should, and nobody measures by how much"*; `docs/METHODS.md` §10–11, Theory of Constraints, *Exploit it* (FC-056 part-built); Rowland, 1 November — *"I want all tools made that get used in the projects"* · **Status:** specified and built 1 November (branch `m6-toc`); no decision of Rowland's outstanding.

## 1. Purpose
The loss count already splits a shift's "rest" into **slow running** and **short stops** once the running speed is timed. What the person acts on is missing: each in packs and money side by side, *which is the bigger and what that means to do*, how **often** the short stops come and how long **each** lasts, and what running under the plate does to the crew's labour a pack. The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"Of the 70 minutes of speed loss, 35 are the bagger running at 108 against 120 all shift and 34 are short stops — six an hour, 50 seconds each. They cost about the same: £1,273 and £1,235 of contribution; fix the speed setting and the jams."*

**Scope.** On a counted loss count (planned minutes, packs made, a rate and the stops in its window given), under *Top causes* a **Speed loss** panel and, on the count's paper, a **Speed loss** paragraph: the total speed loss; **slow running** (net operating time less the packs at the ideal speed) and **short stops** (run time less net operating time) in minutes and in packs at the ideal speed; the running speed against the ideal as a percentage; **which is the bigger** (within 5% of the total: about the same) and the practice's reading of it; **how often** short stops come and **how long each lasts** once short stops are counted over a watched stretch (typed on the count, or taken from a reliability study's count of them with one tap); the two money parts of each (lost contribution and idle labour — never summed) and the crew's labour a pack at the ideal and at the speed it ran. **Not here:** the split itself (the loss count's, FC-049, unchanged — `lossFigures` is read, not reworked); counting short stops all shift (a burden — a watched stretch, repeated, gives the frequency); a short stop's *reason* (the reliability study's reasons, FC-055/FC-015); speed loss off the constraint behind a buffer (it costs nothing until the buffer runs out — FC-046, said in the limits); OEE's "performance" (never — `equipment.oee`, left out on purpose).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The shift lead or engineer after counting a shift, at a desk or on the phone, deciding where the next hour goes. (1) *The record's shift:* planned 450, stops 62, ideal 120, running speed timed at 108, 38,200 good. (2) *Mostly jams:* timed at 119 — almost on the plate, 67 minutes of short stops: count them by reason. (3) *Mostly speed:* timed at 105 — slow running the bigger: the speed setting or the product. (4) *No running speed timed yet:* the lump is said as one and the page says how to split it. (5) *A wrapper run at 90% "because it jams less there"* — speed bought with a stop reduction, read as both.

## 3. Method selection
Nakajima's two hidden losses (*Introduction to TPM*, 1988): reduced speed and minor stoppages, told apart by the running speed measured while the machine runs, as the loss count already does by net operating time. Alternatives from the record: OEE's performance ratio blends the two with the rate (left out on purpose); the reliability study counts short stops by a tap with MTBF (kept, offered here for the frequency). The method refuses to tell them apart without a timed running speed and says what to do; it refuses to call a speed above the plate "slow running".

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established* (`equipment.short-stops-speed`; `scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`): run time = planned − timed stops − changeovers; net operating time = packs run (good + rejected) ÷ the running speed timed; slow running = net − packs ÷ ideal; short stops = run time − net (the loss count's `lossFigures`, Nakajima's net operating time); frequency = counted ÷ watched hours; mean length = short-stop minutes ÷ stops in the run; money — each lost minute at the pace-setter is a minute of the line (Goldratt & Cox 1984; `knowledge/MONEY.md` **T**), the idle crew **L** (Maskell).
- *Assumed*, and said on the page: the count's rate is the pace-setter's ideal speed, so both losses are line time at the constraint by definition and priced there; the running speed timed once is one reading (the record says it is timed again when the product or the setting changes); stops in a watched stretch are representative of the run (a watch shorter than the run is extrapolated and said "about").
- *Chosen (the builder, 1 November — the owner asked for the tools to be made and not to be asked):* **the record's own simplification is corrected** — the record's worked case (run time × (1 − 108 ÷ 120) = 38.8 min slow running, 30.9 short) ran the machine at the timed speed through its short stops too, over-counting slow running; the loss count's net-operating-time split (35.4 and 34.3 — Nakajima's) is the one used, and the record's case is corrected in the same change; **"about the same"** when the two differ by 5% of their total or less (a convention — the record has no threshold); **amber** when either part is over a tenth of the lost minutes (the loss count's own `REST_TENTH`) or the timing says more than the count leaves or is above the plate; a mean length is said in seconds under two minutes; **the mean length is derived** (short minutes ÷ stops in the run) and, when the study timed each stop, set beside the study's measured mean as a cross-check, never replaced by it; **the short-stop tally lives on the count** (`facts.losses.shortStops`, inside the study's JSON — no column, no migration), because it is a fact about *this* shift's watch; a reliability count is offered only when it is named for short stops (short stop, minor stop, micro-stop, jam, misfeed, blockage, snag) and, when the count names a machine, on that machine.
- *Money line (MONEY.md §4):* causation **T** and **L**; inputs **margin a pack** and **crew × wage** (the loss count's cost model — `contributionOf`, `labourPerHour`). When missing: *"Give the line a margin a pack (Set up the line) to say the lost packs in money."* and *"Give the line its crew and a wage (Set up the line) to say the idle labour."* — the packs and minutes still said, nothing waits. The crew's **labour a pack** (the labour spread over fewer packs) needs the crew and a wage and a running speed timed.

## 4. Inputs
Read from the count: planned minutes, good packs, rejects, the rate, the stops in its window and the running speed timed (all the loss count's). **Typed on the panel (optional):** *Short stops counted* (a whole number) and *In how many minutes of running* (minutes, > 0) — kept on the count; or **Use these** from a reliability study's count of short stops on the line (its events and watched hours as the study's own module counts them; its timed mean when each was timed); *Clear it* takes the count off with Undo. A client sees the panel and changes nothing; on a closed count the form is not offered.

## 5. Data suitability
Needs a counted shift (planned, made, a rate) with the stops given. Without a timed running speed: the lump, not told apart. A timing above the plate speed: no slow running, said, amber. A timing that says more slow running than the count leaves: capped at what the count leaves, said, amber. Stops logged beyond the loss: the loss count says so and the panel adds nothing it cannot support. A tally of zero or without minutes is refused (the Save button waits).

## 6. Calculations
`run` = planned − stops − changeovers; `net` = (good + rejects) ÷ running; `slow` = net − (good + rejects) ÷ ideal (0 if running ≥ ideal), capped at the rest; `short` = rest − slow where rest = lost − stops − changeovers − rejects; packs = minutes × ideal; running % = running ÷ ideal; `perHour` = n ÷ (watched ÷ 60); `inRun` = perHour × run ÷ 60; `mean` = short ÷ inRun; labour a pack = crew £ an hour ÷ (speed × 60) at the ideal and at the running speed; money = minutes × ideal × contribution a pack (T), minutes ÷ 60 × crew £ an hour (L).
**Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`, plain arithmetic; each a unit test in `src/tools/losses/__tests__/speedLoss.test.ts`): planned 450, stops 62, ideal 120, timed 108, 38,200 good → run 388; net 353.70; slow 35.37 + short 34.30 = 69.67; packs 4,244 + 4,116 = 8,360; 90% of ideal; 41 stops in the run = 6.34 an hour, 0.836 min (50 s) each; at 30p a pack £1,273.33 and £1,234.67, idle labour £35.37 and £34.30 at £60 an hour; labour a pack 0.83p at 120 and 0.93p at 108. Timed 119 → slow 2.68, short 66.99 (jams); timed 105 → slow 45.48, short 24.19 (speed); timed 108.6 → slow 33.42, short 36.25 (within 5% — about the same); timed 125 → no slow running.

## 7. Outputs
On screen (**Speed loss**, under *Top causes*): one sentence (the total, the slow running with its packs and "ran at 108 a minute against the plate speed of 120, 10% under", the short stops with their packs, and which costs more with its reading); the lines under it (how often and how long, the study's timed mean beside it, the crew's labour a pack, any warning); the money in two parts; what to give for what is missing; the counting form and the reliability studies on offer. Amber only where a part is over a tenth of the loss or the timing wants checking, with the words.

**The report.** The loss count's paper gains **Speed loss** — the same sentence, lines and money, from the same module (`speedLoss`), under *What it means* and the action plan, before the closing line. Purpose and context are the count's own band (the shift, the line, the day); the data and assumptions are in the sentence (the running speed timed, the plate speed) and the lines; the method is Nakajima's net operating time, named in the loss count's *How known* line already on the page. Nothing missing is made up: not told apart → the lump and what to do; no cost → the packs and the input named.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
The count is made on a phone at the end of the shift; the running speed timed beside it as before. The panel adds one optional step — a number of short stops counted and for how long, or one tap on a study — and answers at once. Phone: one column, 44 px buttons, nothing off the side. The lean page's order: where we are (the count's tiles), why (the waterfall, top causes, this), what we are doing (action plan).

## 9. Failure handling
Not counted or no stops given: no panel. No speed loss left over: no panel. Not told apart: the lump, how to split it. A timing above the plate speed or beyond the rest: said, amber. No cost: the inputs named. A tally refused when zero. No reliability study of short stops: the form alone. A client: read-only. A closed count: the form is not offered, the figures stand as they were.

## 10. Integration
Reads the count (`facts.losses`, its stops, `facts.timings`), the line's cost model, and the line's reliability studies (`facts.watch`, `facts.events`, through `relAll`). Writes only `facts.losses.shortStops` (an optional field inside the study's existing JSON — **no migration**; `sync-schema.test.ts` unchanged). Shows where its parent shows (CLAUDE.md rule 5): the loss count's page (the panel) and paper (the paragraph); the count's bars, the line's waterfall and the fishbone read the same `lossFigures` and are unchanged. Access: `can.edit` types and uses an offer; a client and a closed count change nothing. The method page: FC-056 is in Theory of Constraints' *Exploit it*; it opens the Loss count (`methodTools` `inside`).
