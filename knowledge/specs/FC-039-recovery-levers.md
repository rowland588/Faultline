# FC-039 — Recovery levers: what taking a share off the top losses buys

**Catalogue:** FC-039 (a report on the line's capacity page — *What if we took some of it back?*, with its own paper; it reads FC-038's constraint and FC-052's ranked losses, both available, and changes neither) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/capacity/theory-of-constraints.md` (full, textbook), `docs/CAPACITY.md` §5–6, `knowledge/MONEY.md` §4 (FC-039: the share recovered × the loss's **L + T**) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem — *"we know the losses but not what a realistic cut is worth"*; `docs/METHODS.md` §10–11, Theory of Constraints, *Exploit it* and *Elevate it* (FC-039 part-built); Rowland, 1 November — *"I want all tools made that get used in the projects"* · **Status:** specified and built 1 November (branch `m6-toc`); no decision of Rowland's outstanding.

## 1. Purpose
The panel could say a shift's recovery in packs, but not a week in hours, packs and pounds per lever, kept the two money parts apart (the idle crew and the lost contribution), name where the prize is whatever share was chosen, or go on paper for the meeting where a 6M project's target is set. The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"Taking 20% off breakdown on the Bagger and 50% off changeovers gives 26 minutes a shift back — 3,120 more packs and £1,092 of lost contribution a shift; a week of ten shifts is 4.3 hours, 31,200 packs and £10,920. The jams on the Filler give minutes and no packs — the line is held at the Bagger."*

**Scope.** On a line's capacity page (*What a line can make*), in the *What if we took some of it back?* panel, and as a one-page paper (**Recovery plan on paper**): each of the line's top six losses (its causes and changeovers, from the counted shifts of the last six weeks, ranked as the waterfall ranks them) with a slider of the share to take off; per lever the minutes back a shift and a week, the packs a shift and a week (only at the constraint), the lost contribution (only at the constraint) and the idle labour (wherever the stop is), kept apart; the whole plan in one sentence a shift and a week; the lever with the most to give at the constraint; what is still to give; how it is known (the shifts counted). **Not here:** which causes to attack and how (the 6M project and the loss count's *Make it better*); a time to recover or a probability that it will (a share is a what-if, said on the paper); the week without the line's running pattern (asked for, never guessed); a recovered minute beyond what demand will take (FC-040/FC-042 judge the week's orders and mix); the loss count's own split of the rest into slow running and short stops (FC-056).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead or project lead at the start of a 6M project on a packing line, with a weekly meeting to set a target. (1) *The case:* breakdown on the Bagger (the constraint) 40 min a shift, jams on the Filler 30, changeovers 36; taking 20% off the breakdown and 50% off the changeovers. (2) *The mirage:* the team wants to fix the jams on the Filler first — it gives minutes and not one more pack, and the page says where the prize is. (3) *A line with no margin or crew set:* the packs and minutes are still said and each missing input named. (4) *A line whose running pattern is not set:* a shift only, the week asked for.

## 3. Method selection
Goldratt's constraint arithmetic (*The Goal*, 1984; *What Is This Thing Called Theory of Constraints?*, 1990): a minute recovered at the constraint is a minute of output for the whole line — minutes × the constraint's rate in packs; a minute recovered anywhere else makes no more (the mirage). Alternatives from the record: simulation when several constraints are near equal (`capacity.simulation`, FC-047, research first); the loss count's *Make it better* for the cause itself. The method refuses to claim packs off the constraint and says what the prize is.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established* (`capacity.theory-of-constraints`; `scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`): packs back = minutes back × the constraint's ideal speed; off the constraint, minutes with no packs; money — lost output priced where a minute stops the line (throughput accounting, Goldratt & Cox; `knowledge/MONEY.md` **T**), idle labour of the crew standing (**L**), labour and lost output two figures, never one (`docs/CAPACITY.md` §6, Rowland 14 October).
- *Assumed*, and said on the paper: the period's counted shifts are representative (6 weeks; the number counted is said); a share taken off a loss comes back as minutes at the same rate it was lost (a what-if, not a promise); the packs are made only if there is demand and the line is not then held somewhere else (the line's next constraint is the Line capacity's V and *Elevate it*'s business); a loss with no machine named cannot be said to be on the constraint — a changeover's minutes are said "only if on the constraint".
- *Chosen (the builder, 1 November — the owner asked for the tools to be made and not to be asked):* **the two money parts are shown apart on the plan** (the existing `levers.pounds` summed them — the panel now says the parts, the module's arithmetic and its tests untouched); the idle labour is shown **wherever the stop is** (the loss count's own model: the crew stands while the line is down, `knowledge/MONEY.md` rule 3), the contribution **only at the constraint**; a lever's week = its shift × the shifts a week the line's SMED says (never assumed); the prize is the loss with the most minutes a shift among those not known to be off the constraint; the plan is a **what-if kept for nothing on screen** (the sliders write no record, as before) and its paper prints exactly what is on screen when pressed.
- *Money line (MONEY.md §4):* causation **L + T**; inputs **margin a pack** and **crew × wage**. When missing: *"Give the line a margin a pack (Set up the line) to say the lost contribution."* / *"Give the line its crew and a wage (Set up the line) to say the idle labour."* — minutes and packs still said, nothing waits.

## 4. Inputs
Read from the line, asked nowhere new: the counted shifts of six weeks (loss counts), the stops in their windows, each product's Line capacity (the constraint), the line's cost model, the SMED's running pattern (shifts a day, days a week). Typed: the share to take off each loss (a slider, 0–100% in tens) — a what-if kept for nothing. Nothing is stored.

## 5. Data suitability
Needs at least one counted shift with stops (without one the panel is absent). The constraint is Line capacity's — where it is not known, a lever's packs are said "if it is on the constraint". A loss that is a changeover has no machine to test, so it is said "only if on the constraint". With under five counted shifts the page says how many it stands on (the period's basis line); the paper repeats it.

## 6. Calculations
Per lever: `back` = share × minutes a shift (the band's minutes ÷ shifts counted); `packs` = back × the constraint's ideal speed when on (or not known to be off) the constraint, else 0; `contribution` = back × (the band's contribution ÷ the band's minutes) at the constraint; `labour` = back × (the band's labour ÷ the band's minutes); `week` = shift × the line's shifts a week. The plan sums the levers; the prize = the lever with the most minutes a shift that is not off the constraint.
**Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`, plain arithmetic; each a unit test in `src/lib/__tests__/leversPlan.test.ts`): two shifts each with breakdown 40 min on the constraint, jams 30 min off it, changeover 36; constraint 120 a minute, 35p a pack, crew £1 a minute. 20% of the breakdown = 8 min = 960 packs = £336, labour £8; 50% of the changeover = 18 min = 2,160 packs = £756, labour £18; together 26 min = 3,120 packs = £1,092 + £26 a shift; ten shifts a week: £10,920, 31,200 packs, 4.33 hours, labour £260. 100% of the jams off the constraint: 30 min, no packs, no contribution, labour £30; the prize breakdown on the Bagger, 40 min. The existing tests of `leversOf` (`intelligence.test.ts`: 900 packs at 50% of a 30-minute misfeed; the off-constraint loss giving none) still pass unchanged.

## 7. Outputs
On screen (under the sliders): each lever's line (the share, the minutes, the packs, the contribution and the idle labour a shift, the week beside it; the mirage in the amber-edged words for a loss off the constraint), the plan's sentence, where the prize is, what is still to give, and **Recovery plan on paper**. Packs and money are in the plan's words; only a loss off the constraint wears the mirage.

**The report.** **Recovery plan on paper** — a page from the report engine, from the same module as the screen (`leverPlan`): the line and the day, the plan's sentence under the heading *Recovery levers — what taking a share off the top losses buys*, *The levers chosen* (each lever's line), *Where it is held* (the constraint, and that a minute recovered elsewhere makes no more), the prize, *How it is known* (the shifts counted over six weeks, and that a share is a what-if, not a promise), and each missing input named. It prints exactly the plan on screen when pressed; with no lever slid it says so. Nothing missing is made up.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Slide, read, print — three taps to the plan a meeting needs. Phone: the sliders full width, nothing off the side. The lean page's order: where we are (the losses), what is worth getting back (the plan), what we are doing about it (the paper to the meeting).

## 9. Failure handling
No counted shift: no panel (the line page says so). No lever slid: the prompt. A loss off the constraint: the mirage said. No margin or crew: each named. No running pattern: the week asked for. A line whose constraint is not known: packs "if on the constraint". A client reads the panel (the sliders write nothing) and can print the paper.

## 10. Integration
Reads the line's counts, stops, standards, workspace and SMED through `lib/lineRead` (the same read the capacity page and its paper use); writes nothing, adds no record, column or policy — **no migration**. Shows where its parent shows (CLAUDE.md rule 5): the line's capacity page (the panel) and the new paper; the line report's front and *The week, to send* keep their own sentences, unchanged. Access: the sliders are a local what-if. The method page: FC-039 is in Theory of Constraints' *Exploit it* and *Elevate it*; it opens Line capacity (`methodTools`).
