# FC-179 — The week's schedule against capacity, with changeovers

**Catalogue:** FC-179 (a mode of FC-034 Line capacity — it lives on a line's Line capacity page beside *Will next week's orders fit?*, under *The week's schedule*, and on *The week's plan on paper*; the order of the work by a rule is FC-180, the best order of changeovers FC-070 — neither is here) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/planning/scheduling-rules.md`, `knowledge/methods/changeover/sequencing.md`, `knowledge/methods/capacity/load-vs-demand.md`, `knowledge/MONEY.md` §4 (T for the packs short and the changeovers at the constraint, L for the crew's overtime) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem — *"the schedule is a spreadsheet that ignores the line's real rate and changeovers"*; `docs/METHODS.md` §10–11, Theory of Constraints, *Subordinate the rest*; Rowland, 1 November — *"I want all tools made that get used in the projects"* · **Status:** specified and built 1 November (branch `m6-toc`); no decision of Rowland's outstanding.

## 1. Purpose
Line capacity says what a line can make. A planner's question is the next one: *will these orders, in this order, make their days?* The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"The four orders need 54.3 hours of running and 2.1 of changeovers — 56.4 of the 75 hours the line runs; it fits with 18.6 spare, but King Edward is a day late against Wednesday: put it before Tesco or move the date."*

**Scope.** On a line's Line capacity page: orders typed against a product of the line (a quantity, a day it is due, an order in the week), laid end to end on the line's constraint in the order given — each run its quantity at the rate the line can be **planned** on (Line capacity's speed times the share four shifts in five proved, never the plate speed), a changeover between two different products (the minutes the SMED agreed for the product changed into, from the product changed from where that is known), through the minutes a day the line's running pattern gives, from a chosen first running day. It says when each run starts and ends, which orders are late against their day, what does not fit the week, and the changeover time in the week — in money where the line has it. **Not here:** choosing the order by a rule — earliest due date, shortest first, Johnson's (FC-180); the best sequence of changeovers for allergens, colours and flavours (FC-070); more than one constraint, or a mix across lines (FC-048); a calendar of holidays and shifts differing by day; the orders themselves from an ERP (nothing is read from a system).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead or planner on Monday, at a desk or on a phone. (1) *The record's week:* four products, a 42-minute changeover, a 900-minute day, five days. (2) *A rush order added:* Baby 1kg doubled — the week no longer fits, and what is not made is said in packs and pounds. (3) *Two orders swapped:* the sentence follows the order given. (4) *No running pattern set:* the week is asked for, never guessed. (5) *A product with no speed worked out:* its orders are left out, and said.

## 3. Method selection
Forward finite loading of one resource in a given sequence — the textbook first step of scheduling (Pinedo, *Scheduling*, ch. 1; Hopp & Spearman, *Factory Physics*, ch. 13): completion time of job *j* = sum of earlier set-ups and processing times; lateness = completion − due date. The constraint is the one resource scheduled (Goldratt & Cox, *The Goal*, 1984: the constraint sets the line's output, so a schedule on it is the line's schedule). Alternatives from the record: a rule-chosen order (FC-180), a Gantt of every machine (more than the line's constraint — not asked for by the floor), simulation (FC-047).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established* (`planning.scheduling-rules`; `scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`, FC-179): start of a run = end of the one before + its changeover; end = start + quantity ÷ rate; lateness = the day it ends − the day it is due; the week fits when the last end is within the minutes the line runs; the changeover into a product after a different one is a set-up on the sequence (Pinedo).
- *Assumed*, and said on the page: the rate is the line's **plannable** rate — the 20th percentile of the counted shifts as a share of the theoretical, applied to each product's worked-out speed — or, when no shift is counted, each product's worked-out speed alone (and the page says so); the constraint is the line's pace-setter in every product (one constraint on one line); a changeover that is not timed removes nothing from the week and is named; a day's running time is the minutes the pattern gives, the same every running day; a run that crosses the end of a day continues on the next running day.
- *Chosen (the builder, 1 November — the owner asked for the tools to be made and not to be asked):* **the order is the planner's** — it is never re-sorted; **a due day means the end of that running day**, so an order that ends on its day is on time; **orders live inside the product's line standard** (`Standard.capacity.orders`, the standard's own JSON — no column, no migration), because an order is for a product and the line's week is every product's orders put in order; **the week starts on a date the person picks** (today by default) and runs Monday to Friday for five days, to Saturday for six, every day for seven; **amber** when an order is late, **red** when the week does not fit; a changeover is looked up by **the product changed from** (the agreed minutes for that pair), else the one agreed with no "from", else the newest agreed from another product (said), else the SMED as timed (said "not yet the standard").
- *Money line (MONEY.md §4):* causation **T** for the packs not made in the week and the changeovers' minutes at the constraint (each is a minute of the line; priced "if it cannot be made up"), **L** for the crew's time to make it all — at the normal rate, an overtime premium said to be more; never summed. Inputs: **margin a pack** and **crew × wage** (the loss count's cost model — `contributionOf`, `labourPerHour`). When missing: *"Give the line a margin a pack (Set up the line) to say the changeovers and the packs short in money."* and *"Give the line its crew and a wage (Set up the line) to say the overtime to make it all."* The packs and hours are still said; nothing waits.

## 4. Inputs
Typed on the panel: a **product** (from the line's products with a Line capacity), a **quantity** (> 0, in the line's unit), a **due day** (optional), the week's **first running day**; the order is moved up and down; a quantity and a due day are changed where they sit; an order is taken off with Undo. Read, asked nowhere new: each product's Line capacity (speed), the line's running pattern (the SMED's), the counted shifts (the plannable share), the SMEDs' agreed changeovers, the line's cost model. A client sees the plan and changes nothing.

## 5. Data suitability
Needs a running pattern (shifts a day, hours, days a week — set on the SMED; without it the week is asked for, not guessed), a product with a speed (without it its orders are left out and named), a quantity above zero. A changeover not timed: none taken off, the missing step named. No counted shifts: planned at the speed worked out, said. An order with no due day is placed but cannot be late.

## 6. Calculations
`run` = quantity ÷ rate; `start` = end of the previous run + `change`; `end` = `start` + `run`; the end's day = the running day containing the minute `end` (a run ending exactly at a day's end is on that day); `lateDays` = calendar days between the due day and the end's day when after; `spills` = `end` beyond the week's minutes; `spare` = week minutes − the last end (negative: over); `unmade` = for each run that spills, the minutes beyond the week (at most its run) × its rate; changeover units = `change` × the rate of the run after it; pounds = units × the margin a pack; overtime = minutes over ÷ 60 × the crew's cost an hour.
**Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py`; each a unit test in `src/tools/plan/__tests__/schedule.test.ts`): 2 shifts × 7.5 h = 900 min a day, five days = 4,500; Maris 54,000 at 100 → 540 min (ends Mon, 540 in); Tesco 49,500 at 110 → 450 min after 42 (582 → 1,032); King Edward 150,000 at 90 → 1,666.7 min after 42 (1,074 → 2,740.7, Thursday 40.7 in; due Wednesday: 1 day late); Baby 72,000 at 120 → 600 min after 42 (2,782.7 → 3,382.7); total running 3,256.7 + changeovers 126 = 3,382.7 of 4,500, 1,117.3 min (18.6 h) spare; the three changeovers 42 × (110 + 90 + 120) = 13,440 packs, £4,704 at 35p. Baby at 360,000: ends 5,782.7, 1,282.7 min over — 153,920 packs not made, £53,872 at 35p, £1,282.67 of crew at £60 an hour. The last two swapped: the sentence follows the order given.

## 7. Outputs
On screen (*The week's schedule — will every order make its day?*): one sentence (orders, running and changeover hours, the hours of the week, fits or does not with what is spare or over, what is late), the orders as a list in order with each run's line (rate, hours, changeover and how it is known, when it starts and ends, the due day, on time or days late — in words, a late order also marked), money lines in two kinds (never summed), what is missing named, and the limit said in a sentence. **The report.** *The week's plan on paper* — the same sentence, one line per run, the money lines apart, *How it is known* (the planned rate and its source, the changeover, the running days), and what is missing, from the same module (`planBlocks`, `tools/plan`), laid out by the report engine. Purpose and context are the line's name and the print date; the data, assumptions and method are in *How it is known*. Nothing missing is made up: no pattern → asked for; no speed → named; no cost → the input named.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Orders typed on a phone or a desk in a minute each (product, quantity, day); reordered with ▲ ▼; the answer is on the same screen as the orders. The lean page's order: where we are (the sentence), why (which order is late and by how much), what we are doing (move it, change the date, add a shift — said by the numbers; the choice is the planner's).

## 9. Failure handling
No pattern, no orders, no speed, no changeover timed, no cost, an order with no day, a quantity of zero (refused), a week that does not fit (red, with the packs not made), a client (read-only), an order deleted (Undo). A line with no product that has a Line capacity shows nothing.

## 10. Integration
Reads the line's products (`Standard.capacity` — speed, orders, agreed changeovers), the line's SMEDs (the running pattern), the counted shifts, the cost model. Writes only `Standard.capacity.orders` (an optional field inside the standard's JSON — **no migration**; `sync-schema.test.ts` unchanged). Shows where its parent shows (CLAUDE.md rule 5): the line's Line capacity page (the panel) and its paper (*The week's plan on paper*); the line report prints the capacity page's own blocks and is unchanged. Access: `can.edit` on the project (or the owner off a project) types and moves; a client changes nothing. On the method page it opens Line capacity (`partOf` FC-034).

## 11. Verification

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The week's sentence, spare time, lateness and each run's line equal the independent cases — `schedule.test.ts`; `week-plan.mjs` 2.
2. The order given is the order laid: moving an order moves the schedule — `week-plan.mjs` 2.
3. No running pattern: the week is asked for, not guessed; no order: asked to type them — `week-plan.mjs` 1.
4. Money: the changeovers at 13,440 packs = £4,704 at 35p; no margin names the input and gives no pound — `schedule.test.ts`; `week-plan.mjs` 3.
5. A week that does not fit: packs not made £53,872, crew's time £1,283, apart, red — `schedule.test.ts`; `week-plan.mjs` 4.
6. The paper, downloaded and read back, carries the sentence and each run as the module says them — `week-plan.mjs` 7.
7. On a phone nothing is off the side; a client changes nothing — `week-plan.mjs` 8–9.
8. No record shape changed (an optional field in existing JSON); Line capacity's own figures, V and paper are unchanged (`line-capacity.mjs`).
Not yet proved: a product with no speed (the unit test covers it; the browser drives only rated products); a changeover timed on a SMED but not yet agreed.
