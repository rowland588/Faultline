# FC-143 — Pugh matrix: choosing between solutions — the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-143 (FC-144 impact and effort and FC-191 what an improvement is worth are **not built** — left, below) · **Knowledge:** `sixsigma.selection-matrices` (full, practice; Pugh 1991 *Total Design*, from knowledge) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem, "countermeasures are chosen by whoever argues best" — and Rowland, 31 October, M5: build the method's tools from the pathway of `docs/METHODS.md` §11 (A3 Countermeasures, 8D D5–D6, DMAIC Improve) · **Status:** built on `m5-a3`. **Written before the code.**

## 1. Purpose
Choose between several solutions — three fixes for film breaks, four ways to cut a changeover — on criteria the team agreed before scoring, against one **datum** (usually how it is done now), so the choice is on the page and not in the loudest voice. The sentence it lets the lead say to the sponsor that could not be said before: *"We scored the film guide, the reel trolley, the new unwind unit and a second operator against how we run now on five criteria we agreed first. The film guide is better on three and worse on none (weighted +11); the reel trolley is next at +9, and is better than the guide on safety — so we take the guide and fold the trolley's safety into it. The lead holds if any one weight moves a step."*

**Scope.** A **Pugh matrix** as a scored register at `#/scored/pugh`: options (as many as the floor has), criteria with a whole-number weight 1–5 (all 1 unless the team weights them), one named datum scoring 0 on every criterion, each option marked **+ better / S same / − worse** than the datum on each criterion; the pluses, minuses and sames counted; the weighted score Σ wⱼ·sᵢⱼ; the options ranked; the leader(s); where the leader is beaten by another option on a criterion (the strengths worth folding in — Pugh's *controlled convergence*, offered as a list, the re-run done by editing the matrix); a sensitivity check (one weight a step up or down — does the lead change?); an optional **cost typed per option**, shown beside the score; the page and its paper. **Not here:** impact and effort / the PICK chart (FC-144, `knowledge/methods/sixsigma/selection-matrices.md`'s other half); payback and NPV of a capital choice (FC-191 — the record says "where the choice is capital, the appraisal decides; the matrices order the work"); a stored history of successive convergence rounds (the matrix is edited and re-read; *Save a copy* is not built); marks on a five-point scale (−2…+2) — Pugh's own marks are three; a datum that is itself one of the options and a matrix with a different datum in each column (one datum a matrix); turning the leader into an Action automatically (the closing line names it; raise it with a problem or on Actions). It **does not measure**: it orders options against a datum by the team's judgement, said on the screen and the paper.

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead or continuous-improvement lead with the team and the operators round a table, a laptop or a phone at the line (A3 *Countermeasures* box; 8D D5–D6; DMAIC *Improve*). (1) *Film breaks:* four countermeasures from the 6M, five criteria — cuts breaks (5), cost (3), time to do (3), safety (4), hygiene (4) — against the present state. (2) *A close call:* two options within one point, the sentence says so and names the weight that flips it. (3) *Nothing is better than now:* every option scores zero or less — the page says to keep the datum or find a stronger option. (4) *A client or team reader* sees it and changes nothing.

## 3. Method selection
Pugh's matrix (S. Pugh, *Total Design*, Addison-Wesley 1991 — the controlled-convergence matrix; the Lean Six Sigma Pocket Toolbook's Pugh matrix; both from knowledge, texts not re-read — `checked` line in the record). Alternatives from the record's *When not to use it*: impact–effort (FC-144, a different question — which first, not which one); a capital appraisal (FC-191 — money decides); a weighted-sum *decision matrix* with 1–5 scores (not Pugh's method: scores of "how good" hide that they are relative to nothing; refused as the default here, the three-mark form kept). Variants: **unweighted** (every weight 1 — the score is pluses less minuses, Pugh's own form) and **weighted** (the weights, 1–5, typed per criterion). The matrix is unsuitable, and the page says so, when the criteria were chosen after the options were scored, when marks are by people who do not run the line, and when the options differ in kind so much that "better than the datum" means nothing (then FC-191 or FC-144).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established:* the datum scores 0 on every criterion; an option is marked + (better), S (same) or − (worse) than the datum, criterion by criterion; pluses and minuses are counted; where criteria are weighted, the score is the sum of the weight times the mark; the leaders are the options with the highest score; the strong features of the weaker options are folded into the leaders and the matrix run again (Pugh 1991; George et al. 2005, from knowledge; `knowledge/methods/sixsigma/selection-matrices.md` *Procedure and calculation*). The record's own worked case (film breaks): guide +3 −0 weighted +11; trolley +2 −0, +9; new unwind unit +1 −2, −1; second operator +1 −1, 0. Pugh himself cautioned against reading the sum as a measurement (the totals orient the discussion; the strengths and weaknesses are the content) — said under *What this does not say*.
- *Assumed:* the criteria and weights were agreed **before** the options were scored (said on the page; not checkable); the marks are the team's, with the floor present; the datum is a real, describable state.
- *Chosen (the builder, 31 October — Rowland asked for speed and no questions; every choice below is stated on the page where it applies and can be changed in one place, `src/tools/pugh/pugh.ts`):* weights are whole numbers 1 to 5 and default 1 (the record's worked case uses 3, 4 and 5); the **order** is by weighted score, then more pluses, then fewer minuses, then as typed — a tie in the weighted score is shown as a tie in the verdict and rank, never broken by the tie-break; "beats the datum" means a weighted score above 0; the **sensitivity** moves one weight at a time by one step (kept within 1–5) and asks whether the set of leaders changes — "thin" if it does; an option not marked on every named criterion is **left out of the ranking and named**, never scored as if blank were S; the leader is called *clear* (neutral) or *to look at again* (amber) when it is tied, thin, or does not beat the datum — never green or red, because a choice is not done and is not a failure. The datum defaults to "Present state".
- *Money (knowledge/MONEY.md §4):* FC-143 has **no cost of its own** ("— of its own; each option's cost (I) a typed row"). So each option may have a **cost typed** (£, optional); it is shown beside the option and in the leader tile, **never weighed in the score**, and where none is typed nothing is said of money and nothing waits. No causation is invented. Payback of a capital option is FC-191 (not built) — said in the caveat.

## 4. Inputs
**Datum** (text, default "Present state") · **Criteria**, each: name (text; a blank-named criterion is ignored), weight (whole number 1–5, blank = 1; anything else is refused with the criterion named) · **Options**, each: name (text; a blank-named option is ignored), a mark on every named criterion (+ · S · −), cost (£, optional, ≥ 0). Marks: `scores` on the option, keyed by the criterion's id (`+1`, `0`, `−1`). Nothing is read from the line: a Pugh matrix is the team's judgement, not a line fact. Agreed-before-measured fields: the criteria and weights (the page says to set them first and leave them).

## 5. Data suitability
At least **one named criterion** and **one named option** marked on every named criterion; fewer is refused in words ("Add at least one criterion", "Add at least one option and mark it on every criterion"). Two options and three criteria is the smallest matrix that is worth a table; a matrix of one criterion is a single comparison — allowed, said in the sentence only by the numbers. A matrix where one option is best on **every** criterion "did not need a matrix" (the record): not detected, not claimed. Marks are three-valued; anything else in a saved record is read as not marked. Ties and thin leads are reported, not resolved. An unweighted matrix (all weights 1) is very sensitive to a weight, and says so (the sensitivity runs the same way).

## 6. Calculations
For option i with marks sᵢⱼ ∈ {+1, 0, −1} on criteria j with weights wⱼ ∈ {1…5}: plusᵢ = #{j : sᵢⱼ = +1}; minusᵢ = #{j : sᵢⱼ = −1}; sameᵢ = #{j : sᵢⱼ = 0}; netᵢ = plusᵢ − minusᵢ; **scoreᵢ = Σⱼ wⱼ sᵢⱼ**; rankᵢ = 1 + #{k : scoreₖ > scoreᵢ} (ties share a rank); leaders = {i : scoreᵢ = max}; the margin = the leader's score less the next distinct score. **Where the leader is beaten:** for the first-ranked option L, every criterion j with some option k having sₖⱼ > s_Lⱼ, naming those options. **Sensitivity:** for each j and each d ∈ {+1, −1} with wⱼ + d ∈ [1, 5], recompute the set of leaders; any change (a different option, or a tie) is a flip, named.
Independent cases (`scripts/knowledge/cases-pugh.py`, a second implementation written without the app's code; each becomes a unit test in `src/tools/pugh/__tests__/pugh.test.ts`): **(1)** the record's film-breaks case — guide +3 −0 S2 weighted +11; trolley +2 −0 S3 +9; second operator +1 −1 S3 0; new unwind unit +1 −2 S2 −1; leader Film guide; **no** one-step weight change alters the leader; the guide is beaten on safety by the reel trolley. **(1b)** the same with all weights 1: scores +3, +2, 0, −1; one weight change (safety up) ties the guide and the trolley. **(2)** X [+,S,+] and Y [S,+,+] with weights 3, 2, 2: X +5, Y +4; cost down a step or safety up a step ties them — a thin lead; X beaten on safety by Y. **(3)** P [+,−,+], Q [−,+,+], R [S,S,−] with weights 2, 2, 1: P +1, Q +1 tie, R −1; four one-step changes each separate the tie. **(4)** M [−,+] and N [−,S] with weights 3, 2: −1 and −3; nothing beats the datum. **(5)** the order depends on the datum: the same ratings (D = 3,3,3; A = 4,4,1; B = 5,2,2, higher is better, marked by the sign of the difference): against D the leader is A (+1 against −1); against A the leader is B (+1; D −1); against B it is D (+1; A −1). Hand-worked check of (1): 5 + 3 + 3 = 11; 5 + 4 = 9; 5 − 3 − 3 = −1; −3 + 3 = 0.

## 7. Outputs
Exact wording, in the module (`src/tools/pugh/pugh.ts`), on screen and paper together.
- **Verdict (the band):** `Film guide leads` · `Film guide and Reel trolley tie` (three or more: `3 options tie`) · `None beats the datum`. Tone: neutral for a clear lead that holds; amber for a tie, a thin lead, or none beating the datum.
- **Sentence:** clear — `Film guide leads: better than Present state on 3 criteria, worse on none — weighted +11. Reel trolley follows at +9.` (unweighted: `— +3` with "weighted" omitted); tie — `Film guide and Reel trolley tie for the lead at +9 against Present state.`; none — `No option beats Present state: the best, Film guide, scores 0.`
- **Tiles:** *Leads* (the name, or the tie) of the score against the datum; *Beat the datum* (n of m options); *Margin* (the leader's lead over the next, or "tied"); *If a weight moves a step* (Holds / Changes, naming the first flip); and *Leader's cost* only when a cost is typed.
- **Before you choose** (the list under the sentence): `On safety: Reel trolley scored better than Film guide — worth taking into it.` and `If cost counted a step less, Film guide and Reel trolley would tie.`
- **Closing line:** clear — `Take Film guide forward as the countermeasure to try — the matrix orders the choice; a trial on the number proves it.`; tied — `Don't choose yet: … tie. Look at what separates them on the criteria that matter most, or fold the stronger marks of each into one option and score again.`; thin — `The lead is thin: … Agree the weights again before choosing.`; none — `Keep Present state for now or find a stronger option — none scored better than it.`
- **What this does not say:** it ranks options against the datum and is **not a measurement** — change the datum and the order can change (case 5); the marks say better, same or worse, not by how much; criteria and weights should be agreed before scoring and left alone (a weight chosen to pick a favourite defeats it); a clear winner on every criterion did not need a matrix; the typed costs sit beside the score and are not weighed in it, and a capital choice is decided by its payback (FC-191, not built); options left out until marked on every criterion are named; the matrix does not prove the chosen option works — the trial does.
- **Money:** the typed cost beside each option and in the leader tile; none typed → nothing said of money, nothing waits.

**The report.** One page ("Pugh matrix") on the lean page: the band (the verdict, the line, the day), the tiles, the sentence, **the matrix itself** (criteria down with their weights, the datum column, one column an option, the marks + · S · −, then the foot: better (+), worse (−), same (S), weighted score, rank, and cost where typed — the table wraps its cells and breaks between rows, never inside one), *Before you choose*, *What this does not say* with the datum named, and the closing line. It reads the same module as the screen (`pugh`, `summarizePugh`, `pughTable`), so the two cannot part; nothing is made up for a missing input. Taken from the page's Report ▾ and in the line report and the client's pack with the other studies.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
At a table or at the line on a phone: *New ranking* (named for what is being chosen and the line) opens an empty matrix with the datum set to "Present state" and a blank row for each of three criteria and two options; type the criteria and weights first; add options; tap a cell to choose + · S · − (a native picker on a phone, one tap and one choice, no typing); the score and rank re-work under the columns as each mark lands; the leader's column is marked in words ("leads"), not by colour alone. The matrix scrolls sideways inside its own frame on a phone; the page does not. Remove an option or criterion with a button, with **Undo** until the next edit. Add a criterion / Add an option. The record is a study (rename, delete, where it sits, in a project or on a line, as every register).

## 9. Failure handling
No criterion / no option: refused in words, nothing ranked. A weight not a whole number 1–5: named and refused. An option not marked on every criterion: left out and named, the rest ranked; all left out: the problem is the sentence. A cost that is negative or not a number: ignored with the option named. A tie, a thin lead, none beating the datum: the verdict says so in amber. A client reads, changes nothing (`can.edit`). A study from before has no such record: nothing changes for the existing criticality registers (their `criteria`/`datum` absent). Offline: the same as any study.
