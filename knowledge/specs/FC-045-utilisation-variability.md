# FC-045 — Utilisation and variability: why queues explode (Kingman's VUT)

**Catalogue:** FC-045 (a calculator on the FC-235 shape — each calculator is a definition, not a screen; FC-044 Little's law is its sibling: the queue it finds is Little's law's WIP; FC-047 line simulation, for a whole line with real stops and buffers, is research and not built) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/capacity/kingman-vut.md` (full, peer-reviewed), `knowledge/methods/capacity/accumulation-buffer.md`, `knowledge/MONEY.md` §4 (FC-045: the queue's holding **H** and the spare capacity's **L**, side by side) · **Asked for:** the catalogue's problem — *"running the busiest machine harder makes the waits worse, not better"*; `docs/METHODS.md` §10–11, Theory of Constraints, *Subordinate the rest* (FC-045 not built); Rowland, 1 November — *"I want all tools made that get used in the projects"* · **Status:** specified and built 1 November (branch `m6-toc`); no decision of Rowland's outstanding.

## 1. Purpose
Product waits for a shared spiral freezer, a wrapper or a metal detector, and the answer people reach for is to run it harder. The wait before a station is, near enough, variability × utilisation × time — and the utilisation term u ÷ (1 − u) is a hyperbola: at 50% a unit waits one process time, at 85% nearly six, at 95% nineteen. The sentence it lets one side say to the other: *"At 92% utilisation a unit waits about 17.6 process times before the wrapper — 10.6 minutes — and about 16 units stand in front of it. At 85% the wait would fall to 5.2 minutes; halving the wrapper's variability does nearly as much."*

**Scope.** One station (one machine, or several identical machines taking from one queue): the **wait in the queue** and the **time through** (wait + process time) by Kingman's VUT (1961) as Hopp & Spearman give it, in **process times**, and the **units waiting** by Little's law; **what lower utilisation buys** (the wait at 85% when it is above, at 95% when below — the hyperbola made plain), **what lower variability buys** (halving the station's own, then the arrivals' as well); **what a busy station passes on** (c_d, one machine); the **picture** of wait against utilisation with the station marked; the machine's own variability **from its stops** (Hopp & Spearman's effective process time) when it runs a fixed cycle and stops; and the money — the queue's holding and the spare capacity's idle crew side by side. **Not here:** a line of stations or a real stop pattern with buffers (FC-047 simulation, research first); the variability measured *for* you from a cycle log (the person types c, with how to get it); a due-date or service-level cost of a long wait; the inventory buffer chosen against demand variability (safety stock, FC-183).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead or engineer at a desk, in a meeting about loading a shared station. (1) *The wrapper:* 92 an hour arriving at 0.6 minutes each (92% busy), a high-variability machine (c 1.5) behind a moderately variable feed (0.9): about 17.6 process times of waiting. (2) *The simplest queue:* random arrivals and times at 85% — the formula is exact — 5.7 process times. (3) *Two wrappers from one queue:* the multi-machine form. (4) *A depositor on a fixed cycle that stops* — 90% up, a 10-minute average stop: its variability is made by the stops (c 1.9), not its cycle. (5) *A station asked for more than it can do:* refused — no steady queue, only a pile that grows.

## 3. Method selection
Kingman's approximation for the G/G/1 queue (Kingman 1961, "The single server queue in heavy traffic"), in the VUT form of Hopp & Spearman's *Factory Physics* ch. 8; Sakasegawa's (1977) form for several identical machines; Little's law for the queue (`capacity.littles-law`); Hopp & Spearman's effective process time for a machine whose variability comes from its stops. Alternatives from the record: simulation for several interacting stations and real stop patterns (FC-047, research first — not built); the accumulation buffer (FC-046) for *where* the waiting product should sit; Little's law (FC-044) for how long product waits with no cause. The method refuses a utilisation of 100% or more (no steady state) and says it understates the wait above about 90%.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established* (`capacity.kingman-vut`; validated in `scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py` and `src/lib/ie/__tests__/queue.test.ts`): CT_q ≈ ((c_a² + c_e²) ÷ 2) × (u ÷ (1 − u)) × t_e — **exact when c_a = c_e = 1** (M/M/1: Wq = ρ ÷ (1 − ρ) × t_e, checked at 50%, 85% and 95%); for m machines CT_q ≈ ((c_a² + c_e²) ÷ 2) × (u^(√(2(m+1)) − 1) ÷ (m (1 − u))) × t_e (Sakasegawa; **1.2% off the exact Erlang C wait at m = 2, u = 85%**); the queue by Little's law (units = arrivals a minute × the wait); c_d² ≈ u² c_e² + (1 − u²) c_a² (one machine); t_e = t_0 ÷ A and c_e² = c_0² + (1 + c_r²) A (1 − A) m_r ÷ t_0. **Accuracy against a Lindley-recursion simulation of G/G/1** (400,000 jobs, seed 7; gamma-distributed times): 2.2% low (u 0.85, c 1 & 1), 1.5% low (0.85, 0.9 & 1.5), 0.9% high (0.7, 1 & 0.5), **6.0% low at u = 0.92** — within the record's ±10% below 90% and understating above it, said on the page.
- *Assumed*, and said on the page: a steady state; the process time is the *effective* one (stops and slow running in — nameplate speed understates utilisation); the variabilities are those of the units, from tens of timed cycles or arrivals; one station, with the feeder's departures taken as the arrivals' variability the person gives.
- *Chosen (the builder, 1 November — the owner asked for the tools to be made and not to be asked):* **no default for any variability** (a typical c would be invented; the page says how each is found and the textbook's classes — low under 0.75, moderate to 1.33, high above); **one machine unless said**; the **comparison point is 85%** when the station is busier (the record's own illustration: "at 85% utilisation the wait falls to five minutes") and **95%** when it is lighter (to show the hyperbola); the verdict stays **neutral** (the method gives no threshold for a good wait — the calculator explains a pattern more than it decides, the catalogue's own value line); **the picture earns its place** (the whole answer is a curve) and is drawn from one description for screen and paper; the idle-labour side of the money is the crew at the station standing for the spare share of the time (a station a person tends), said as such.
- *Money line (MONEY.md §4):* **H** — the queue's stock (units waiting × what a unit has cost by now; offered from the line's materials a pack as a floor) and its carrying a year at a rate the person gives; **L** — the spare capacity's idle crew (people at the station × a wage × the share of the time it is idle), today and at 85%, so the buffer choice is seen in money (inventory against capacity). When missing: *"Give what one waiting unit has cost by now to say the queue in money."*, *"Give the cost of holding stock for a year (finance will have it; the industry quotes 15–35%) …"*, *"Give the people at the station and what one costs an hour to say the spare capacity in money."* — the wait itself still said, nothing waits.

## 4. Inputs
*The station* (text, optional — names the sentence) · *Identical machines side by side* (whole, ≥ 1, default 1 — a choice, said) · *Work arriving* (units an hour, > 0) · *How its own time is known* (choice: timed cycles · a fixed cycle that stops) · timed: *One unit takes* (minutes, > 0) and *How much that time varies (c)* (≥ 0) · stops: *Its cycle when it is running* (minutes), *How much that cycle varies (c)* (≥ 0), *Up and running* (% of the time, 1–100), *An average stop lasts* (minutes ≥ 0), *How much stops vary in length (c)* (≥ 0) · *How much the arrivals vary (c)* (≥ 0) · *In money, optional:* *What one waiting unit has cost by now* (£ — offered from the line's materials a pack), *Cost of holding stock for a year* (% a year), *People at the station*, *What one person costs* (£ an hour — offered from the line's wage with on-costs).

## 5. Data suitability
Utilisation must be below 100% (blocking, said in words with what to do). Variabilities of the arrivals and the machine need tens of observations — the help says so. A c of 0 is a fixed cycle (allowed). An availability under 1% or over 100% is refused. A station of several machines is the multi-server form (an approximation of an approximation, said in the working).

## 6. Calculations
u = λ t_e ÷ (60 m); V = (c_a² + c_e²) ÷ 2; U = u ÷ (1 − u) (m = 1) or u^(√(2(m+1)) − 1) ÷ (m (1 − u)); CT_q = V U t_e; CT = CT_q + t_e; queue = (λ ÷ 60) CT_q; c_d = √(u² c_e² + (1 − u²) c_a²) (m = 1); from stops: t_e = t_0 ÷ A, c_e = √(c_0² + (1 + c_r²) A (1 − A) m_r ÷ t_0). What-ifs: the wait at 85% (the arrival rate set to 0.85 × the capacity m × 60 ÷ t_e) or 95%; c_e halved; c_e and c_a halved. Money: queue × cost a unit; × rate ÷ 100 a year; (1 − u) × people × wage an hour.
**Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-toc.py` E1–E4, plain arithmetic; each a worked example in `utilisation-variability.ts` and a unit test in `queue.test.ts`): M/M/1, t_e 2 min: 2, 11.333 and 38 minutes at 50%, 85% and 95% (1, 5.667 and 19 process times); the wrapper (t_e 0.6, 92 an hour, c_a 0.9, c_e 1.5): V 1.53, U 11.5, CT_q 10.557 min = 17.6 process times, time through 11.157, queue 16.19, c_d 1.4244; at 85% 5.202 min (capacity needed 108.2 an hour, 8.2% more than the 100 it does); halving c_e 4.735 min; two machines at 85% 5.2675 min (Erlang C 5.2072); the depositor with stops in (t_0 0.5, A 90%, m_r 10, c_r 1, c_0 0.2): t_e 0.5556, c_e² 3.64, wait 5.1556 min at 86.4 an hour (u 0.8); the same machine with no stops, 0.6686 min; money: 16.19 units at 85p = £13.76, £3.44 a year at 25%, 8% idle of a crew of 3 at £14 = £3.36 an hour, 15% = £6.30.

## 7. Outputs
Verdict *"10.6 min waiting"* (neutral); the sentence; four more lines (what 85% — or 95% — buys, what halving variability buys, what a busy station passes on, what buffers the station today); tiles *Utilisation · Wait in the queue · Time through · Waiting in front of it · Variability · Queue held*; the working line by line (with Kingman's formula, its source, and the stops' derivation when used); the money; five caveats; **the picture** — wait against utilisation, the station marked; the closing line.

**The report.** The generic calculation paper (FC-235, `lib/report/calcPaper`) from the definition's own result: the band and tiles, the sentence, *The figures put in* with where each came from (typed, the line's, or a choice), *How it is worked out*, the picture drawn from the same description as the screen's, the money or what to add, *What this does not say*, Before → after when saved, the closing line. Nothing missing is made up.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Four numbers to an answer (arrivals, a process time and its c, the arrivals' c) at a desk or on a phone; the what-if halves a variability or moves the load before anything is changed on the floor; the saved before reads the next figures as before → after. One column on a phone.

## 9. Failure handling
A blank or out-of-range box: no answer, *Waiting for the figures* naming each; u ≥ 100%: refused in words; a missing money input named, never filled; a definition that throws shows the shape's own message; a client reads it and takes the paper.
