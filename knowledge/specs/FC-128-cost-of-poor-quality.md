# FC-128 — Cost of poor quality (a calculator)

**Catalogue:** FC-128 · **Knowledge:** `quality.copq` (full, textbook) · `knowledge/MONEY.md` §2 Q, §4 FC-128 · **Asked for:** Rowland, 31 October — the eight methods, each with its tools (`docs/METHODS.md` §11: FC-128 serves DMAIC's Define and A3's Background) · **Status:** built on `m5-dmaic` (31 October), not yet merged. **Written before the page.**

## 1. Purpose
Say what poor quality cost the line in a period, in money, by the four buckets — and where it is: *"Poor quality cost £12,000 in the period (3.9% of sales) — give-away £4,100 of it, then rework £2,800 — against £1,800 spent preventing it and £3,200 checking for it."* DMAIC's Define phase argues a project from a number the business reads (pounds and a share of sales), not from a percentage rejected; an A3 states the same figure in its Background. The sentence it lets the line say to the business: *quality is costing us £12,000 a week, two-thirds of it before it leaves the building, and this is the first thing to go after.*

**Scope.** One calculator (`key: copq`) over one period (a week, a month — the person's, said in the label): the four buckets of the PAF model, each a list of items priced from the one cost model or typed in pounds from the record that holds them; totals by bucket; the cost of poor quality (internal + external failure) and the cost of quality (all four); each as a share of sales and the poor quality a pack made, where those are given; the failure ranked (a Pareto in words); the share failure is of the whole. The saved *before* of the shape gives the trend and the change since ("Is spending on prevention moving the failure costs?"). **Not here:** the automatic reading of those items from the records (rejects by fate, holds, complaints — they are typed, each cell named for the tool that counts it; feeding them is the next piece, FC-134 complaints per million is not built); a Pareto drawing (the shape's picture is a line chart — the ranking is said in words and in the working); a trend over many periods on one page (each period is a record; the line's Calculators row lists them); allocating overhead (never — copq.md); any "hidden factory" multiplier (never — copq.md).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The ops lead, quality manager or CI lead at a desk or in a project meeting, with the week's figures from the loss count, the capability study's give-away, the reject log and the finance sheet. (1) *The record's week* (Define): 1,800 · 3,200 · 9,400 · 2,600, £17,000 all told, 5.5% of £310,000 sales, poor quality £12,000 = 3.9%, failure 71% of it, give-away the first thing to chase. (2) *A later week* (Control): prevention up to £2,400, give-away cut, poor quality £7,660 — the saved before says it is £4,340 less. (3) *A line that only knows its scrap*: 1,200 packs and a give-away figure typed, four complaints counted but no cost of a complaint — the internal failure is £950, the complaints are named as counted and not priced, nothing is invented.

## 3. Method selection
The PAF model (Feigenbaum 1956; Juran & De Feo 2010; BS 6143-2:1990) is the industry's way to put quality in money; the alternative views are WCM cost deployment by equipment loss (FC-057, a different cut of the same losses — its limits already say COPQ is a separate calculator), and the one line's cost model (`cost.line-cost-model`). The four buckets are summed from items each with a source and a formula (copq.md, *Procedure and calculation*); only counted costs; no overhead; prevention spend is not a cost to cut.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established:* the four buckets and their items (copq.md; Feigenbaum 1956; Juran & De Feo 2010; BS 6143-2:1990 — edition to confirm); hours × rate, packs × materials, rework packs × the cost of putting one through again, complaints × the cost of one, per `knowledge/MONEY.md` §2 causations L, M, Q (labour, materials, the PAF buckets). The worked week: `scripts/knowledge/cases-quality.py` and `scripts/knowledge/cases-calc.py` Q1–Q3.
- *Assumed:* the figures typed are for the one period named and are counted costs from records; a reject's materials appear once (as scrap), its lost contribution only through the quality-stop item and only where the line was stopped at the constraint (the two-parts rule, `MONEY.md` rule 3 — the person types the stop's pounds from *Lost time in money*, FC-189, rather than this page re-working a second cost model); no overhead is in any figure.
- *Chosen (the builder, 31 October — the owner asked for speed, not to be asked):* **the cost of poor quality is internal plus external failure** and **the cost of quality is all four** — both said, the first leading (Juran's COPQ; the record's own reading "Poor quality cost Line 2 £12,000 … we spent £1,800 preventing and £3,200 checking"); the verdict is **neutral grey, never red or green** — Juran's rough range of 5–25% of sales is a reading the record quotes, not a threshold, and a low figure can mean good counting as easily as good quality (copq.md, *Assumptions*), so no colour is invented; the bucket tiles say their share of the cost of quality; shares are said to one decimal of sales and whole numbers of the cost; a cost a pack is said in pence; the items offered are the ones copq.md lists for a packing line (scrap, rework, give-away, holds and downgrades, quality stops; complaints, credits and charge-backs, returns and recalls; prevention and checking hours; samples destroyed), with an "other" in pounds for prevention and appraisal; the wage and a pack's materials are **offered from the line** (`from`), never filled in unasked; the cost of rework a pack and the cost of a complaint are the business's own figures, typed and said so, never defaulted.

## 4. Inputs
All optional (MONEY.md rule 5); at least one item must be priced, else the page waits and says what to give. *The period:* a label (names the paper only) · Sales for the period (£) · Packs made in the period. *In money:* One person's cost an hour (offered from the line's wage with on-costs) · A pack's materials (offered from the line) · Putting a pack through again costs (£ a pack) · One complaint costs to handle (£). *Prevention:* hours spent · other pounds. *Appraisal:* hours spent · samples destroyed (packs) · other pounds. *Internal failure:* packs scrapped · packs reworked · give-away (£) · held or downgraded (£) · line time lost to quality stops (£). *External failure:* complaints (count) · credits and charge-backs (£) · returns, withdrawals and recalls (£). Counts of packs and complaints are whole numbers; none is negative.

## 5. Data suitability
A bucket filled by estimate becomes the number people argue about (copq.md): the help on each box says where the figure is counted. An item with a count and no price is counted, not priced, and the total says it is "a floor". A bucket nobody gave anything for is left out and said so — never zero. Poor quality larger than the sales typed is warned (the period is probably not the same).

## 6. Calculations
Hours × rate; packs × materials; rework packs × cost of rework; complaints × cost of a complaint; typed pounds as typed. Bucket = Σ its priced items. Poor quality = internal + external; cost of quality = Σ four; failure share = poor ÷ total; of sales = ÷ sales; a pack = ÷ packs made. Ranked failure = priced failure items by pounds, each ÷ poor. **Independent cases** (`scripts/knowledge/cases-calc.py` Q1–Q3, run with `python3 -I`; the unit tests take the same literals): Q1 the record's week — 80 h × £15 + £600 = £1,800; 160 h × £15 + 1,000 × 50p + £300 = £3,200; 3,400 × 50p + 2,000 × £1.40 + 4,100 + 800 = £9,400; 20 × £80 + 1,000 = £2,600; total £17,000 = 5.5% of £310,000; poor quality £12,000 = 3.9%; failure 70.6% ≈ 71%; ranked give-away 4,100, rework 2,800, scrap 1,700, complaints 1,600, credits 1,000, holds 800; 7.7p a pack made on 155,000. Q2 a later week — 2,400 · 3,200 · 6,300 · 1,360, total £13,260, poor quality £7,660. Q3 — 1,200 × 50p + 350 = £950, four complaints counted not priced.

## 7. Outputs
Band verdict "£12,000 poor quality" (or "£5,000 spent on quality" when no failure was given), tone grey. The sentence above; further lines: the cost of quality all told and the failure's share of it; the cost a pack made; the failure ranked ("Give-away £4,100 (34%) · Rework £2,800 (23%) · …"). Six tiles: *Poor quality*, *Prevention*, *Appraisal*, *Internal failure*, *External failure*, *Of sales* (the share poor quality is of the sales, else "give the sales"); each bucket tile "x% of the cost of quality" or "nothing given". The working: each item with its basis ("160 hours × £15 an hour"), then the buckets. The money: *"Prevention £1,800 + appraisal £3,200 + internal failure £9,400 + external failure £2,600 = £17,000 — each item priced at the line’s hours, wage, materials and the costs typed."* and every missing input as its own sentence (the wage, the materials, the cost of rework, **"Give the cost of a complaint to say external failure in money."**, the sales, the packs). Caveats: counted costs only and no overhead; a floor when anything is unpriced (with the count); the Pareto is where the money is, not the total; cutting prevention to cut "quality cost" is the classic misread. Closing line: the first thing to chase and what the saved before will prove. No picture: the ranked failure is a list of six numbers and a drawing would be decoration.

**The report.** The generic calculation paper (FC-235): the band, tiles, sentence and further lines, the figures put in with sources, the working (every item priced), the money with its missing sentences, the caveats, Before → after when saved (poor quality moved £4,340, prevention £600 …), the closing line. It reads the same module as the screen.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Phone first: grouped boxes by bucket, in the order of the loop (prevention, appraisal, internal, external), the unit costs under "In money — all optional" with the line's wage and materials one tap; the answer is live as the boxes fill; a what-if changes one box ("if give-away were 2,400") without keeping it.

## 9. Failure handling
Nothing priced: waits, naming what to give. Negative or not-a-number: refused in words. A count with no price: counted, named. An empty bucket: named. Failure above sales: warned. A client reads and changes nothing (the shape).
