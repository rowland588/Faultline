# FC-017 — Improvement kata: target condition and experiments — the specification

**Catalogue:** FC-017 · the Improvement Kata method's project — its questions, the learner's storyboard (challenge, current condition, target condition, obstacles), the experiments record, the five coaching questions answered from the board and the routine's pulse; the *Kata storyboard* paper, the Kata's sections of the project report and its reading of the dashboard (`docs/METHODS.md` §9, §16, §17) · **Knowledge:** `knowledge/methods/lean/toyota-kata.md` (Rother 2009, *Toyota Kata*; Rother 2018, *The Toyota Kata Practice Guide* — from knowledge, texts not re-read) · **Asked for:** Rowland, 31 October — *"Yes, seven plus Theory of Constraints"* (the Improvement Kata one of the methods); 1 November — *"I asked you to build the method and insert the tools where required"*; 20 November — *"Finish all of the partly made tools"* · **Status:** the storyboard, its paper, the report sections and the reading built 1 November (`m7-kata`, `r9-kata`); **this specification written on 20 November, after them** (the standard says before — said here), with one gap closed against it the same day: the time each prediction and result was first written, so a prediction written after its result is said. The criteria below were proved against what stands.

## 1. Purpose
A team leader improving toward a goal when the way there is not known — a little every day, a small experiment at a time, each with a written prediction before it — and a coach asking the five questions daily. The storyboard lets the learner say to the coach: *"Target condition 25 s by 30 November; current 29–30; experiment 7 taught us the reel is too heavy for one person — the next step is the reel lift. Seven experiments, seven predictions written first."*

**Scope.** The Improvement Kata as a method project (`#/projects?method=kata`, then `#/project/:id`), its board being the whole interface (no default boxes under the sections): the storyboard's four cells — the challenge with the learner and coach; the current condition with its measure and its pattern; the target condition with the day it is to be reached; the obstacles, with the one being addressed now; the experiments record of as many rows as the learning takes — the step, what we expect, what happened, did it match (yes · partly · no, read from the first word), what we learned, the day taken — **with the time each prediction and each result was first written, so a prediction written after its result is said**; the five coaching questions answered from the board, with the day to go and see; the routine's pulse (experiments run, open, days since the last step; three days with no step is a routine that has stopped); the project's own tools falling in, read only — the newest capability study and control chart under the current condition, the analysis under the experiments, the Gemba walk's freshness; the *Kata storyboard* paper; the Kata's sections of the project report and its reading on the dashboard. **Not here:** a run chart of the measure toward the target with the experiments marked on it (the control chart, FC-086, draws the readings; its sentence speaks under the current condition and its picture is one tap away); a log of the daily coaching cycles (the pulse reads the days the steps were taken); the target condition read as met from readings (the team says it); the Kata as a line's own board without a project; any money of its own (below).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The learner (a team leader) at the board each day, on a phone at the line or at a desk; the coach asking the five questions; the manager reading the paper or the report. (1) The record's worked case: a challenge of the end-of-line changeover from 38 s to 20 s by the quarter's end; a target condition of 25 s every cycle by 30 November; seven experiments, each with a prediction (38 → 35, 35 → 33, 33 → 36, 33 → 31, 30 → 30, 30 → 29, 30 → 45), the seventh teaching that the reel is too heavy for one. (2) A step taken and no result written two days on — go and see. (3) A prediction typed after the result was known — said.

## 3. Method selection
The improvement kata when the way to a goal is not known and the team should improve by small daily steps; the record's alternatives stand and the method's guide says them: a one-off big change in a week → the Kaizen event (FC-016); the cycle each experiment follows → PDCA; the daily meeting that hosts the coaching → the tiered daily meeting; the standard that holds the gain → standard work. Unsuitable: one big experiment a month (a project, not kata — the pulse says the routine has stopped); a target condition written as a solution (the hint says it is a state, not a solution); predictions written after the result (said).

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
- *Established (the record; Rother):* the four steps — understand the direction (the challenge), grasp the current condition (its pattern, not its average), establish the next target condition (a described state with a date two to four weeks away, not a solution), experiment toward it one step at a time; each step a small PDCA with a written prediction before and a written result and learning after; one obstacle at a time; the coaching kata's five questions; a prediction matched is confirmation, a prediction missed is the learning; a storyboard with no entry for three days is a routine that has stopped; predictions written after the result are the common fraud against oneself — the record carries the time of each; the verdict is on the pattern, not one day's number.
- *Assumed:* the words written are the learner's; the day a step was taken is the day written; an answer's first-written time is when it was first saved on a device (a project note's `createdAt`, kept through edits and sync).
- *Chosen (Faultline's, consistent with the record):* (1) the board is the interface: the sections' default boxes are not drawn; row 1 of the experiments keeps the plain field ids and row n > 1 keeps `field@n`. (2) *Did it match?* is read from its first word only — yes, partly or no — never from the numbers (the prediction and the result are words). (3) The pulse is three days with no step (amber on the dashboard) and seven (red), from the days written on the steps. (4) **A prediction is said to be written after its result when its first-written time is later than the result's; written together (the same moment) is not said; an answer with no time (an older record) is not judged.** (5) The tools fall in read only, the newest of each in this project, with Open or Start — never required.
- *Money (`knowledge/MONEY.md` §4, FC-017):* "— of its own; each experiment's measure says its money by its tool". The Kata has no money of its own; the project report's *What everything is telling us now* carries each tool's own money clause, never summed. Nothing is estimated.

## 4. Inputs
None needed to start. Written (project notes `method:kata:<field>`): *The challenge*, *The learner and the coach*, *The measure and its unit*, *The process as it is — its pattern*, *The target condition*, *The date we aim to reach it* (a date), *The obstacles in the way*, *The obstacle we are addressing now*; each experiment's *Step*, *What we expect*, *What happened*, *Did it match?*, *What we learned*, *Day taken* (a date); *When we go and see what we have learned*. Read: each answer's first-written time; the project's studies; the Gemba walk on its lines.

## 5. Data suitability
Said, never refused: a cell not written; a result with no prediction written first; **a prediction written after its result**; a step taken with no result after a day; a miss with no lesson; a result whose match is not said; the target condition with no day, or past its day; experiments with no target or no one obstacle named; no day written on the steps (the pulse cannot be read); the day to go and see passed with the step still open.

## 6. Calculations
Rows: the highest row with any answer, at least one. A step is *done* with a step and a result; *open* with a step and no result. Pulse: days from the latest *Day taken* to today; stopped at three or more. Tally: written, with a result, open, matched yes · partly · no · not said, results with no prediction. **Written after:** for each experiment with a prediction and a result, first-written(prediction) > first-written(result). **Independent cases** (hand-worked, after the record's seven experiments in `scripts/knowledge/cases-lean.py`; `kataBoard.test.ts`, `kataInsight.test.ts`): a storyboard of seven experiments — three said matched, one partly, one missed, one with a result and no prediction, one open — tallies 7 written, 6 with a result, 1 open, 3 · 1 · 1 matched, 1 not said, 1 with no prediction; *Did it match?* read from the first word only (*Yes, 50 held* → yes; *Not really* → no; *maybe* → not said); a step taken on 28 October and another on 29 October read on 1 November — 1 run, 1 open, last step 3 days ago, the routine stopped; a prediction first written at 10:05 for a result written at 10:00 — said; both at 10:00 — not said; a prediction with no time — not judged.

## 7. Outputs
On the board: the pulse in a line; each cell's state in words (Not written · Under way · Written); each experiment's state and, **when its prediction was written after its result, a line saying so**; the five questions answered from the board, a missing one named; the tools' sentences. On the dashboard: the routine stopped (amber at three days, red at seven), an open step with no result, a result with no prediction, **a prediction written after its result**, a miss with no lesson, the target day past or missing, the day to go and see passed; the next step (go and see the open step, else write the next step from what was learned).

**The report.** Two papers from the same module the board reads (`lib/kataBoard`). The **Kata storyboard** (Report ▾ on the project report page; `lib/report/kataPaper`): the pulse, the four cells (each missing one named), every experiment with its step, what was expected, what happened, whether it matched, what was learned and the day — **a prediction written after its result marked under it** — and the five coaching questions with their answers. The **Project report** (`#/project/:id/method-report`): the Kata's three sections — *The challenge, the target and the obstacles* (the target's days left or past, the obstacles with the one addressed now), *The experiments — what we expected and what happened* (the table, bars of results and matches, the latest lesson, results with no prediction **or with the prediction written after**), *The five coaching questions and the pulse* — with the dashboard's attention. A client may read and print both; nothing missing is made up.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
The cell in hand open (the first not fully written), every other cell and experiment one line with its state in words; once the cells are written the latest experiment is the step in hand. *Add an experiment* adds a row; every box keeps its words when the person leaves it; dates are date boxes. One column on a phone; the experiment boxes stack.

## 9. Failure handling
An empty storyboard names every missing cell and answer; nothing is required to write a cell (the tools are offered, never needed). A client reads every box as words and has no box or *Add an experiment*. An answer with no first-written time (older records, or written by another route) is never judged written after.

## 10. Integration
Records: project notes (`method:kata:*`) — no new noun, no migration; the notes' first-written time (`createdAt`) carried to the dashboard and the report through their `owed` rows (`DashOwed.createdAt`, optional); the project's studies, read only. Shows on: the method page (the board), the dashboard (`lib/methodInsights/kata`), the project report (`project/method/report/kata`), the Kata storyboard paper. Access: `useAccess` — `can.edit` writes and starts tools; a client reads. Sync: as project notes.
