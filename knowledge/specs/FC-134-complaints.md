# FC-134 — Complaints per million and their Pareto (a calculator)

**Catalogue:** FC-134 · **Knowledge:** `quality.complaints` (full, practice) · **Asked for:** the 8D method's D0–D1 and D2 (`docs/METHODS.md` §5, Rowland, 31 October: "assign the tools to each of them methods … a linear pathway") — the complaint rate and where the complaints concentrate · **Status:** built on branch `m5-eightd` (31 October 2026), not yet merged.

## 1. Purpose
Say how many complaints a million units sold the period has had, how sure that rate is, whether it is unusual against the periods before, and which kinds the complaints are — *"12 complaints on 1.25 million units sold: 9.6 a million (5.0–16.8 at 95%). That is inside the usual range (up to 14.6) and not shown different from last period (p = 0.41); foreign body and packaging or seal are 67% of them."* The sentence it lets the quality lead say to the site and the customer that they could not say before: *a bad month is not yet a bad process — and these kinds are where to look.*

**Scope.** One calculator (`key: complaints-per-million`, on `/calc/complaints-per-million`, a record like every calculator) for **one period on one line** (the calculator sits on a line, or a project's, or none): the units sold or despatched in the period; the complaints counted by six kinds (foreign body · packaging or seal · taste, smell or condition · quantity or weight · labelling or date code · other); the rate per million units with its exact Poisson 95% interval (none received: the most it could be, 95% one-sided); the Pareto of the kinds (counts, shares, cumulative share, each kind's own rate a million) and its picture; optionally the usual rate (complaints and units of the periods before) for the u-chart's range around it, and the period before (complaints and units) for the exact test of two rates; the cost of a complaint, optional, for external failure in money. **Not in this build, each with its number:** a trend of many periods on one page and the u-chart drawn across periods (the calculator is one period — a saved before reads the next against it; a periods-long chart is FC-086 charts, which can take the CPMU as a measurement); the kind × product cross-table and χ² (FC-107 groups-tables-relations takes the counted table; a calculator a product gives the product split meanwhile); matching complaints to the production week through the lot code (a lag model: the lag between making and complaining is a product's own, weeks for an ambient one — unknown until the lot codes are logged); seriousness weighting; logging each complaint as a record with its date code and opening an 8D from a serious one (FC-150 and the register, FC-154 — not built; the counts are typed by period); the cost of poor quality summed across buckets (FC-128).

## 2. Users and scenarios
The quality lead at a desk at month end, or in the customer meeting with a phone; the technical manager answering a retailer's scorecard; the 8D team at D0–D1 deciding whether this is one bad pack or a pattern, and at D2 saying how much and what kind. Food scenarios: a foreign-body complaint on the 500 g line (is it new? is it one kind?); a film change and the packaging complaints after it; a retailer scoring suppliers on complaints per million; a launch month with 400,000 units sold and three complaints.

## 3. Method selection
**Shape: a calculator, not a mode of the capability study.** The capability study's *faults found on each item* part (FC-099, FC-124) has the same Poisson arithmetic and the tally's Pareto, but its unit is an *inspection* (items looked at, faults found, many inspections, a u-chart across them) — a period's complaints against units sold is one count on one denominator a period, typed once from the retailer's report and the despatch figures, not tapped item by item. Making it a study mode would put a third reading shape (period, units sold, kinds) into `StudyScreen.tsx` (792 lines), `modes.ts`, `agreed` and the study paper for one extra sentence; the calculator shape already owns the record, the form, the what-if (*what if the six had been four*), the saved before (*this month against last*), the paper, the line's row and report, the phone, and the table-driven acceptance script. The arithmetic is the faults mode's `poissonLower`/`poissonUpper` (`lib/ie/dist`), shared, not copied.

**Method** (`knowledge/methods/quality/complaints.md`): CPMU = complaints ÷ units × 10⁶; the exact Poisson interval — lower χ²(0.025; 2c)/2N, upper χ²(0.975; 2c + 2)/2N, scaled to a million (c = 0: the one-sided upper χ²(0.95; 2)/2N = 2.996/N); is the period unusual — the u-chart's limits around the usual rate ū = Σc/ΣN: ū ± 3√(ū/n) for this period's n millions (the lower cut at 0); did the rate change — the conditional exact test: of c₁ + c₂ complaints, the earlier period's c₁ is Binomial(c₁ + c₂, N₁/(N₁ + N₂)) if the rates are equal, the two-sided p = min(1, 2 × the smaller tail); the Pareto by kind, descending.

Alternatives (the record's *When not to use it*): faults counted inside the factory → the tally in the capability study (FC-124); one serious complaint's investigation → 8D (FC-150); a supplier's defects → FC-136.

**Established · assumed · chosen.**
*Established:* CPMU as the food trade's measure of a supplier (retailer scorecards — practice, no single public text, from knowledge); the exact Poisson interval (Garwood 1936; the χ² form) and the c = 0 bound (≈ 3/N, the "rule of three" at 95% one-sided); u-chart limits ū ± 3√(ū/n) (Montgomery, *Introduction to Statistical Quality Control*, ch. on attribute charts); the conditional test of two Poisson rates by the binomial (the exact C-test; e.g. Przyborowski & Wilenski 1940). Every figure is checked against SciPy in `scripts/knowledge/cases-complaints.py` (printed values in §6).
*Assumed:* complaints arrive independently and rarely (the Poisson) — said in the caveats; the units are the same product mix and period as the complaints; complaints are not matched to the week made, so a fix's effect shows late (said).
*Chosen (Faultline's own, none Rowland's yet — flagged for him):* the six kinds, a short fixed list drawn from the record's own examples (foreign body, taste, packaging, quantity, labelling) plus *other*, so a calculator's boxes stay a fixed set; the usual rate comes from the periods *before* this one (not including it, so a bad period does not pull its own limits); 95% for the interval and 0.05 for "shown different" (the house default across the tools); a warning under 100,000 units sold, below which one complaint moves the rate by 10 a million; "under 20 complaints" as the point below which the interval is called wide in the caveat (the record's *Assumptions*). Tones: red only above the u-chart's upper limit; amber for a rate shown higher than last period's (p < 0.05) when no usual rate is given; green for a rate below the lower limit or shown lower than last period's; everything else neutral — never red for a number merely high.
**Money** (`knowledge/MONEY.md` §4, row FC-134): complaints × the typed cost of one — *external failure*, the cost of poor quality's fourth bucket (Q). Input: the cost of a complaint (handling, the retailer's charge-back or credit, goodwill) — typed, optional, never invented. Said as the period's cost, per million units sold and per unit sold. Missing: *"Give the cost of a complaint (handling, credit or charge-back, goodwill) to say external failure in money."* No causation is invented for a rate or a kind — one cost is typed for every complaint, so the Pareto is by count, never by money (a foreign body costs more than a crease; that needs a cost per kind, which the floor does not yet give — FC-128).

## 4. Inputs
All typed; nothing is read from the line (the cost model has no complaint figure); the shape's *Use it on* line only places the record.

| Box | Unit | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Period (text, optional) | — | names the sentence and the paper only |
| Units sold or despatched in the period | units | needed, whole, ≥ 1 |
| Foreign body · Packaging or seal · Taste, smell or condition · Quantity or weight · Labelling or date code · Other | complaints | whole, ≥ 0; each optional but **at least one must be typed** (0 counts as typed) — a blank period is not "no complaints" |
| Usual rate: complaints in the periods before / units sold in them | complaints, units | optional pair; one without the other is a warning, not a block |
| Period before: complaints / units sold | complaints, units | optional pair, same |
| Cost of a complaint (money, optional) | £ | ≥ 0 |

## 5. Data suitability
Counts need no minimum to be worked out, but are said honestly: under 20 complaints the interval is wide and the order of the kinds can change next period (a caveat, with the count); under 100,000 units sold one complaint moves the rate by more than ten a million (a warning on the box); zero complaints is a valid, good answer, said with its bound, never "0.0 ± 0". A period before with no complaints and none now has nothing to compare (the test is not run and says why). The Poisson assumes independence — several complaints from one bad batch are one event, not many: said.

## 6. Calculations
`src/tools/complaints/complaints.ts` (pure): `cpmu(c, N)` → rate, lo, hi, upper (via `poissonLower`/`poissonUpper` in `lib/ie/dist`); `usualRange(cBar, nBar, N)` → ū, lcl, ucl; `ratesDiffer(c1, N1, c2, N2)` → two-sided p by `binomCdf`; `pareto(kinds)` → kinds with count > 0 descending (ties keep the box order), share and cumulative share.

Independent cases (`scripts/knowledge/cases-complaints.py`, SciPy 1.17.1, printed values — each becomes a unit test in `src/tools/complaints/__tests__/complaints.test.ts` and an example in the definition):
- 7 on 1,200,000 → 5.8333 (95% 2.3453–12.0189); 12 on 1,250,000 → 9.6000 (4.9605–16.7693); 3 on 400,000 → 7.5000 (1.5467–21.9182); 20 on 1,200,000 → 16.6667 (10.1804–25.7403).
- 0 on 1,000,000 → one-sided upper 95% 2.9957 (two-sided 3.6889); 1 on 50,000 → 20.0 (0.5064–111.4329).
- Usual rate 106 complaints on 14.4 M units = 7.3611 a million; limits for 1,250,000 units 0.0810–14.6412, for 1,200,000 0–14.7913 (the lower cut at 0), for 400,000 0–20.2306.
- 12 on 1.25 M against 7 on 1.2 M → p = 0.4083 (not shown different); 30 on 1.2 M against 7 on 1.2 M → p = 0.000191; 3 on 0.4 M against 7 on 1.2 M → p = 0.9488.
- The Pareto of 5 · 3 · 2 · 1 · 1: 41.67 · 25.00 · 16.67 · 8.33 · 8.33%, cumulative 41.67 · 66.67 · 83.33 · 91.67 · 100%, each kind's own rate 4.00 · 2.40 · 1.60 · 0.80 · 0.80 a million.
- Money at £85 a complaint: 12 × 85 = £1,020 a period; £816 for every million units sold; 0.0816p a unit sold.
Rounding: rates to one decimal (two under 1), shares to whole percent, p to two decimals (three under 0.01, "< 0.001" below it).

## 7. Outputs
Verdict: *"9.6 a million"* (the rate). Sentence: *"12 complaints on 1.25 million units sold in October — 9.6 a million, 95% confident 5.0–16.8; inside the usual range of 0.1–14.6 a million; not shown different from the period before (5.8 a million, p = 0.41)."* — the clauses for the usual range and the period before only when those were given (otherwise a line under it saying what to add). Zero: *"No complaints on 1.25 million units sold — no more than 2.4 a million at 95%."* More: the Pareto sentence (*"Foreign body and packaging or seal are 67% of the complaints."*; one kind only: *"Every complaint is foreign body."*); under 20 complaints, the wide-interval sentence. Tiles (≤ 6): **A million** (the rate, "95%: lo–hi"), **Complaints** (the count, "on N units"), **Usual range** (lcl–ucl, "around ū"), **Against before** (p, "shown different / not shown different"), **Most of them** (the top kind, its share), **Cost** (the period's £, when the cost is given). Working: the rate sum with the numbers in; the interval; the usual range; the test; then **the Pareto as rows** (kind · count · share · cumulative · a million). Picture (≥ 2 kinds): the cumulative Pareto — rank across, cumulative share up, each point labelled with its cumulative share, the caption naming the kinds in order. Caveats, always: complaints under-report by a factor nobody knows — the rate compares like with like over time, it never says the true defect rate; the rate is by the period received, not the week made; one bad batch is many complaints from one cause. Closing line: *"Line 7: 9.6 a million (5.0–16.8) — inside the usual range; look at foreign body first."*

**The report.** The shape's generic calculation paper (`lib/report/calcPaper`, FC-235): the lean page's band (what, where, when), the tiles with the change since a saved before, the sentence and its further lines, the picture, the working (with the Pareto rows), the caveats, the money or the line saying what is missing, the closing line — read from the same `compute` as the screen, so the two cannot part; the line report takes the calculator's row. Nothing on it is invented: a figure not given is named as missing. Taken as PDF from Report ▾ on the calculator's page.

## 8. Manufacturing usability
Hub (Calculators) → Complaints per million → type the units and the counts; the answer is live beside the boxes, saved a moment after the last key. Phone first: the boxes stack, the six kinds in a row each, the optional ranges and money folded under *In money — all optional* and the rest as the shape lays them; a 44 px target each; nothing off the side. What-if: change one count and see the sentence move; save the figures as the before at month end, type the next month's, read the change since in each tile.

## 9. Failure handling
Units blank, zero or not whole → blocked, by name. No kind typed → blocked: *"Type how many complaints of each kind — 0 for none."* A negative or decimal count → blocked. A usual-rate pair or a before pair half given → a warning naming the missing box (the rest of the answer stands). Cost blank → the money line says what to add. A client reads the calculation and changes nothing (the shape). A calculator from before this build does not exist (new key), so no old record is read differently.

## 11. Verification

Acceptance script: `scripts/acceptance/calc-shape.mjs`, table-driven over every definition — it types this definition's examples through the real boxes at desk and phone, checks sentence, verdict, tiles and money against the examples, downloads the paper and reads it back.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. The rate and its exact Poisson interval equal SciPy's — 7 on 1.2 M, 12 on 1.25 M, 3 on 0.4 M, 20 on 1.2 M, 0 on 1 M (one-sided), 1 on 50,000 — `complaints.test.ts` "the rate and its interval"; the examples in the definition via `calc.test.ts`.
2. The u-chart range around the usual rate equals the independent limits, the lower cut at 0 — `complaints.test.ts` "the usual range".
3. The two-rate exact test equals SciPy's binomial tails (p 0.4083, 0.000191, 0.9488) — `complaints.test.ts` "two rates".
4. The Pareto's order, shares and cumulative shares equal the hand case; ties keep the box order; kinds at zero left out — `complaints.test.ts` "the Pareto".
5. The sentence, verdict, tone and tiles of every example equal the literals written from those cases (inside the range; above the range → red; below the period before → green; none received) — `calc.test.ts` examples; `calc-shape.mjs` typed through the boxes.
6. Money: the cost of a complaint given → £1,020 · £816 a million · 0.0816p a unit; not given → the missing-input sentence, and nothing blocked — `complaints.test.ts` "money"; `calc.test.ts` blank money box; `calc-shape.mjs`.
7. Invalid input: units blank/zero/decimal, no kind typed, negative count refused in the words named — `calc.test.ts` refused; `calc-shape.mjs`.
8. Half-given ranges warn and the answer stands — `complaints.test.ts` "half a pair"; `calc.test.ts` warns.
9. The picture lies inside its axes and carries a label per point — `calc.test.ts` picture; `calc-shape.mjs` (drawn, not clipped, on the phone).
10. The paper downloads and reads back the same sentence, tiles, Pareto rows and money — `calc-shape.mjs`.
