# Capability of readings that are not bell-shaped

## Purpose and principle
When the readings are skewed or bounded, the normal curve's tail misstates
how many would fall outside. Three established answers: model the readings
with a distribution that fits (lognormal, Weibull …) and take its tail;
transform them so they are normal (Box–Cox power, Johnson system) and take
the limits through the same transform; or define the index by percentiles —
the distance from the median to the 0.135% and 99.865% points stands in for
3σ (the "percentile" or Clements method; ISO 22514-2 gives the general
form). With few readings, the plain count of readings outside, with its
binomial bound, is the honest fallback.

## Questions it answers
How much would fall outside a limit when the readings lean to one side —
residual oxygen, leak rates, times, a weigher that fills at or above target?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
Readings in order; the limit(s); enough readings to see a tail: percentile
estimates of the 0.135% points need hundreds of readings, so with tens use a
fitted model and say how sure, or the empirical count.

## Procedure and calculation
- Percentile index: Ppk = min((USL − X₅₀)/(X₉₉.₈₆₅ − X₅₀), (X₅₀ − LSL)/(X₅₀ − X₀.₁₃₅)).
- Box–Cox: y = (x^λ − 1)/λ (ln x at λ = 0), λ by maximum likelihood, x > 0;
  transform the limits too, then the normal indices on y.
- Fitted distribution: estimate its parameters, share outside = F(LSL) + 1 − F(USL).
- Empirical: k of n outside, the exact binomial bound on the share (`stats.confidence-intervals`).

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
- Fishing for the transform that gives the best number is a misuse; choose
  by the process's physics (bounded at zero → lognormal or Weibull) and the plot.
- A mixture (two heads, two jaws) is not "non-normal" — split it.
- Tail estimates from few readings are guesses; say the bound.

## Reading the result
"Residual O₂ leans high (bounded at 0); fitted lognormal: about 3 in 1,000
over 2%. As measured, 1 of 60 over."

## When not to use it — and instead
Counted data → `spc.capability-attribute-proportion`. Normal enough →
`spc.capability-measured`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Residual oxygen in MAP packs; leak rates; time to seal; combination weigher
fills; microbial counts (log scale).
