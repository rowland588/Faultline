# GB food law for manufacturers — traceability, recall, hygiene and HACCP duty

## Purpose and principle
Food law in Great Britain combines UK statutes with **assimilated** EU
regulations kept at the end of the transition period. For manufacturers the
same duties apply in substance: food must be safe; the **food business
operator** (FBO) is responsible for everything under its control; traceability
must work one step back and one step forward; unsafe food must be withdrawn or
recalled and the authorities told; and after primary production the site must
run **HACCP-based** procedures. Faultline does not hold the site's legal
register or certify compliance — it helps people understand the duties, keep
evidence on the line, and open the right hygiene and check tools.

## Questions it answers
- What must a UK manufacturer be able to show on traceability (suppliers,
  customers, lots, quantities)?
- When must product be withdrawn, recalled, and who must be told?
- What does Article 5 of Regulation 852/2004 require for HACCP — the seven
  principles in law, not only in Codex?
- How do FSA *Managing food safety* and Safer Food, Better Business (SFBB)
  relate to the legal duty for smaller sites?
- What must change when the product, process or equipment changes?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
- The business's role in the chain (manufacturer, packer, storage, dispatch).
- Whether the site is in England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland (NI
  keeps EU food law for product on the NI market — see FSA guidance).
- The site's existing food-safety management system (HACCP plan, SFBB pack,
  or equivalent).
- Records that already exist: goods in, production, despatch, complaints,
  withdrawals.

## Procedure and calculation
This record states **legal facts and official pointers**, not audit
scores.

**Primary responsibility (Regulation 178/2002, Article 17).** The FBO is
responsible for ensuring that food law is met for food under its control; it
must verify that requirements are met throughout production, processing and
distribution, and that controls and procedures are proportionate.

**Traceability (Article 18).** Traceability must be established at all
stages. The FBO must identify suppliers (one step back) and customers supplied
(one step forward), with systems to make information available to competent
authorities on demand. Food placed on the market in Great Britain must be
labelled or identified to support traceability (Article 18(4)).

**Withdrawal, recall and notification (Article 19).** If the FBO considers or
has reason to believe food is not compliant with food-safety requirements and
has left its immediate control, it must withdraw it. If product may have
reached the consumer, the operator must inform consumers of the reason; where
other measures are insufficient, **recall** from consumers. The FBO must
**immediately inform** competent authorities when food placed on the market may
be injurious to health, and cooperate on risk-reduction measures.

**Hygiene — HACCP duty (Regulation 852/2004, Article 5).** FBOs carrying out
production, processing and distribution after primary production must put in
place, implement and maintain permanent procedures based on **HACCP principles**:
(a) hazard identification; (b) critical control points; (c) critical limits;
(d) monitoring at CCPs; (e) corrective action; (f) verification; (g)
documentation and records proportionate to the business. When product, process
or any step changes, the procedure must be **reviewed and updated**. FBOs must
provide evidence of compliance as authorities require, keep HACCP documents
current, and retain records for an appropriate period.

**FSA practical guidance (England and Wales).** *Managing food safety*
(food.gov.uk) explains that management procedures must be based on HACCP,
documents kept up to date, and procedures reviewed when production changes. SFBB
packs give HACCP-based safe methods and a diary for small catering and retail
businesses; manufacturing sites usually need a fuller documented system
(`foodsafety.haccp`), but the same legal logic applies.

**Withdrawal vs recall (FSA summary).** Withdrawal removes food from the
supply chain; recall asks consumers to return or dispose of product already
purchased. Traceability records support both within the expected time
(`foodsafety.traceability-exercise`).

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
- **Not legal advice.** Statute text and government summaries only; a food
  lawyer or local authority interprets a site's duties.
- **NI vs GB.** Northern Ireland market rules differ post-Windsor Framework;
  this record describes GB assimilated law unless NI is named.
- Faultline never marks a site "compliant" or substitutes for an
  environmental-health or certification audit.
- Citing HACCP principles does not set **critical limits** — limits come from
  the site's validated plan or measured evidence, not the model.
- SFBB is not a substitute for a factory HACCP plan when processes are complex;
  pointing small businesses to SFBB when they are manufacturers is a misuse.

## Reading the result
"The law expects one-step-back, one-step-forward traceability with records on
demand; if this lot is unsafe and has left your control you withdraw, recall if
consumers may have it, and tell the FSA/local authority at once. Your HACCP
plan must cover the seven Article 5 principles and be reviewed before the new
filler runs." Money: product at risk on withdrawal (`knowledge/MONEY.md`, H);
trace exercise time is labour (L).

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Every UK food and drink factory; recall drills; customer or retailer audits
asking how traceability and Article 19 are met; project readiness before a new
line or process change (plan review before run).
