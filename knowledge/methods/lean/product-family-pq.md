# Product families and P–Q analysis

## Purpose and principle
Group products that share most of their steps (a family), and rank products
by volume (P–Q, a Pareto of quantity), so a value stream and a layout are
designed for the products that matter.

## Questions it answers
Which products belong together on a map or a line? Which few make most of the volume?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
Products with their routing (the steps they use) and their volumes.

## Procedure and calculation
Family matrix: products × steps, grouped by shared steps; P–Q: volumes ranked, cumulative share.

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
A family by sales category is not a family by process.

## Reading the result
"Four families; family A is 70% of the volume on 8 of 30 products."

## When not to use it — and instead
For scheduling by frequency → `planning.runner-repeater-stranger`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Pack sizes and recipes grouped by line and allergen.
