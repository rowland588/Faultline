# The eight wastes — seen, classed and costed

## Purpose and principle
Ohno's seven wastes — overproduction, waiting, transport, over-processing,
inventory, motion, defects — and the unused skill of people, used as eyes on
a walk so waste is named, located and, in Faultline's rule, given a cost.

## Questions it answers
What waste is on this line, where, and what does each cost?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
A walk (or footage) with each waste noted, located and estimated.

## Procedure and calculation
Tally by waste type and place; cost each from its time × the line's rate or its material.

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
A list of wastes without a cost or an owner changes nothing.

## Reading the result
"11 wastes seen; the biggest, waiting for QA release, 6 hours a pallet."

## When not to use it — and instead
To find a cause of one problem → `rca.fishbone-5whys`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Over-production of short-life product; product waiting warm; over-wrapping.

- [textbook] Ohno, T. (1988), *Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production*, Productivity Press.
