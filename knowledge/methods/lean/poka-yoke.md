# Mistake-proofing (poka-yoke)

## Purpose and principle
Make the error impossible or immediately evident at its source rather than
inspect for it later — contact, fixed-value and motion-step devices that stop
or warn. Each device must be checked to work, regularly.

## Questions it answers
Which errors are proofed, by what, and did every device work at its last check?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
The register: the error, the device, its type (control or warning), how it is checked, how often.

## Procedure and calculation
Checks due and done; failed checks and the reaction.

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
A device bypassed or never checked gives false security.

## Reading the result
"14 devices; 2 not checked this week; the label-presence sensor failed its check."

## When not to use it — and instead
Ranking which errors to proof first → `quality.fmea`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Label and date-code verification scanners; reject-confirmation on metal detectors; allergen-specific coloured utensils.
