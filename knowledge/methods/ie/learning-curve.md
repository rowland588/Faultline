# The learning curve — time falling with repetition

## Purpose and principle
Each doubling of the cumulative quantity cuts the time a unit by a fixed
share (the learning rate), so a new starter's or a new product's times can be
forecast to standard.

## Questions it answers
When will a new starter reach standard? When will a new product run at rate?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
Times (or rates) against cumulative units.

## Procedure and calculation
Tₙ = T₁·n^b, b = log₂(learning rate) (an 80% curve: b = −0.322); fitted by log–log regression.

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
Plateaus and forgetting after breaks; machine-paced work does not learn.

## Reading the result
"An 85% curve: a new packer reaches standard after about 1,800 packs."

## When not to use it — and instead
A line's rate climbing after commissioning → `commissioning.ramp-up`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Seasonal agency staff; new product introductions with hand assembly.
