# Inspectors agreeing — attribute agreement analysis

## Purpose and principle
When people judge good or bad by eye, check that each agrees with themselves,
with each other and with the standard, beyond chance agreement (kappa).

## Questions it answers
Do the QA team and the line agree what a bad seal looks like?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
About 30–50 items, a known standard for each, 2–3 inspectors judging each 2–3 times blind.

## Procedure and calculation
Agreement %; κ = (p_o − p_e)/(1 − p_e); Fleiss's κ for many raters. κ above about 0.75 good, below 0.4 poor (common guide).

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
Items that are all obvious; inspectors knowing the answers.

## Reading the result
"Inspectors agree with the standard 84% of the time (κ 0.68) — the borderline leakers are the problem."

## When not to use it — and instead
A measured characteristic → `msa.gauge-rr`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Seal checks, label checks, bake colour against a chart, foreign-matter inspection.

## References
- [paper] Cohen, J. (1960), "A coefficient of agreement for nominal scales", *Educational and Psychological Measurement* 20(1), 37–46.
- [paper] Fleiss, J.L. (1971), "Measuring nominal scale agreement among many raters", *Psychological Bulletin* 76(5), 378–382.
