# Sensory difference test — the triangle test

## Purpose and principle
Assessors given three samples, two alike, pick the odd one; more correct picks
than the 1-in-3 chance says the products are perceptibly different.

## Questions it answers
Can people taste a difference after the recipe or supplier change?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
Randomised presentation; enough assessors (often 24–30+).

## Procedure and calculation
Correct ÷ assessors against the binomial with p = 1/3.

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
Assessors who know which is which; too few.

## Reading the result
"14 of 30 correct (p = 0.04): a difference is perceived."

## When not to use it — and instead
Measurable attributes → measure them.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Recipe changes, ingredient substitutions, shelf-life end points.
