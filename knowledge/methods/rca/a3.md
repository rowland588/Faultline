# A3 — the problem and its solution on one page

## Purpose and principle
One sheet of paper, A3 size, that tells the whole story of a problem
and its solution in seven blocks read top-left to bottom-right:
**background** (why this matters to the business — the gap in its
number); **current condition** (the facts drawn — the chart, the
Pareto, the map, with the number); **target** (the number wanted, by
when); **analysis** (the root cause — the fishbone, the five whys, the
stratification — with the evidence); **countermeasures** (what will be
done, by whom, by when, against which cause); **plan** (the steps and
dates); **follow-up** (how and when the result is checked, and later,
what it was). The page is not the point; the thinking it forces is —
one problem, its facts, its cause and its proof, agreed between the
owner and the people who must support it, through the questions the
page provokes. It is the lean counterpart of the 8D: the same logic for
an internal problem, in a shape a manager reads in two minutes.

## Questions it answers
- What is the problem, in the business's number, and where is it
  against the target?
- Why — the cause, with the evidence?
- What are we doing, who, by when — and against which cause?
- Did it work — the follow-up's number against the target?

## Inputs and minimum conditions
- The problem's number and its history (the current condition's chart).
- The target with its date.
- The analysis done (`rca.fishbone-5whys`, `rca.stratification`), with
  evidence per cause.
- The countermeasures as actions (owner, day — `visual.action-tracking`)
  and the follow-up's measure and date.

## Procedure and calculation
- Each block in its place; the current condition and the follow-up
  share one chart with the target drawn as a line, so the page says the
  gap and its closing at a glance.
- Countermeasures mapped to causes: every cause on the analysis has a
  countermeasure or a reason not; every countermeasure names its cause.
- The follow-up: before → after on the number (`rca.pdca`), the
  holding, the standard written; the page updated, not replaced — the
  story keeps its history.
- The dialogue: the owner drafts; the sponsor and the people affected
  question each block (is the background the real gap? is the cause
  evidenced?); the page carries their agreement.
- A proposal A3 (a change to propose) and a status A3 (a programme's
  state) use the same shape with the blocks renamed; the problem-solving
  A3 is the one here.

**Worked case** (`cases-rca.py`): leaking seals on lane 3. Background —
leakers cost £X a week and a retailer complaint; current condition —
the chart of leakers by lane, lane 3 at 3.2% against 0.3%; target — 0.3%
on lane 3 by 14 November; analysis — the is/is-not and the swap test:
the new jaw's heater; countermeasures — heater calibration at every jaw
change (engineering, 7 Nov), the jaw-change checklist (production, 10
Nov); plan — the dates; follow-up — 28 November: 0.2% over four weeks,
held. One page, read in the site meeting in two minutes.

## Assumptions, limits and misuse
- A pretty sheet without the thinking — the blocks filled with
  statements rather than facts and evidence — is the common failure.
- A background that does not say the business's number is a complaint,
  not a problem.
- Countermeasures with no cause named are a to-do list.
- A follow-up block left empty is an A3 that was never finished; the
  page lives until the follow-up is written.
- The A3 is for one problem; a page that tries to hold a programme is a
  status A3 and reads differently.

## Reading the result
"Lane 3 leakers 3.2% → 0.2% in four weeks; cause the new jaw's heater,
verified; two countermeasures done; held." Money: the gap's cost in the
background and its closing in the follow-up (`knowledge/MONEY.md` —
Q for the leakers, H for the holds), on the same page as the number.

## When not to use it — and instead
A customer's formal corrective-action report → `rca.eight-d`. The
cause-finding itself → `rca.fishbone-5whys`. The proof's arithmetic →
`rca.pdca`. The report's conventions → `visual.reports`.

## In food and FMCG manufacturing
Presenting a 6M to the site's management meeting; a line's chronic
give-away problem on one page; a changeover improvement proposed to the
sponsor.
