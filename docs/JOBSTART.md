# The start of a Stage Gate job — audit

Rowland, 9 October: audit the start of a job, from an empty app to a job
whose plan is agreed. Findings first; nothing is changed until he has seen
them.

Walked 9 October in Chromium, laptop (1360) and phone (390), from an app with
nothing in it:
- start a Stage Gate job;
- add eight machines from four suppliers (bag former, weigher, checkweigher,
  metal detector, case erector, case packer, case sealer, palletiser);
- give every machine its stages at every gate and its usual tests;
- date the plan, and agree what each test must show.

Every tap and every box filled was counted. Then the job was read as it
stands (front page, control room, plan, status report) and walked as owner,
team and client.

## Against the seven questions

**1. Can you start a job from what is in front of you, and know what to do
next?** Yes. Six actions from nothing to a job:
1. The empty control room says what it is for and offers "Start a project".
2. That opens three situations in plain words ("Bring new equipment into
   use — is the equipment proved to what was agreed, so it can be
   accepted?"), with no method names to know first.
3. Stage Gate asks for the name, the lead and the agreed handover date,
   then lands on Install with one instruction: add the machines.

Small words:
- **Projects says "Home".** The page it means is called the Control room
  now.
- **The Stage Gate card's "Prints" list is out of date.** It says "Client
  report · test and fix cards". It also prints the status report, the plan,
  the programs report, the day and now the handover report.
- **Install's empty page promises too much.** It says each machine "gets the
  job's stages". It does not until you give them, with one tap offered right
  after.

**2. Eight machines, their stages and tests — how many taps, and does it
scale?**
- **Machines: 32 actions, 4 per machine.** The add form closes after each
  one, so "Add a machine" is tapped again for every machine.
- **Stages: one tap per gate for all eight** ("give them all the 6"), with
  Undo. **Tests: one tap** gives every machine the six usual tests, 48 in
  all. This part scales well.
- **Arrival dates are one machine at a time.** Machines arrive "due on site"
  with no date; each machine's is set in its own panel (Edit → Expected on
  site).
- **Every line starts as the machine's supplier's**, including work that is
  the site's own:
  - "Air and power connected" and "Electrically complete";
  - "Safety sign-off (PUWER)" and "Client signed off".

  So before anything has happened, the control room says "Ilapak UK owes
  92" and the site owes nothing. The status report says "Waiting on: …
  Ilapak UK × 24". The core answer, who owes what, is wrong from day one.
  It can be put right per machine ("Who is doing every step left on it") or
  per line, one at a time.
- **Correcting a supplier's name on the machine leaves its lines owed by
  the old name.** Each line copies the name when it is made, and editing the
  machine does not touch them (read from the code; not walked).

**3. Can what was agreed be set, and is it clear it is then fixed?** Partly.
- **The handover date is asked at the start** and stays with the owner
  (Commission's "Dates", Details).
- **The stage lists and the usual tests are edited by the owner only**
  ("Edit the stages", "Edit the usual tests"), and the team is told so.
- **But a usual test is only a name.** What it must show ("passes if") and
  a performance run's agreed rate are written on each machine's test one at
  a time: 4 actions a test, 192 for eight machines' 48 tests.
- **Nothing counts the tests with nothing agreed to show.** The handover
  report then prints them with no "Had to show". The handover is judged
  against what was agreed, and the start barely asks for it.

**4. Can the plan be dated quickly across many machines?**
- **Install, Set up and Hand over:** a stage's column plans it for every
  machine at once, 4 actions a stage, 24 for Install's six. Every machine
  goes on the same days, with no "one after another". A machine's row plans
  all its steps in one window.
- **Commission: the column headings are not buttons.** Each of the 48 tests
  is dated in its own record.
- **The plan shows only what is dated.** "48 dates · 0 done · 48 still
  ahead", with nothing said about the 136 lines with no date.

**5. What does a job with nothing done yet say?** That it is on target.
- **The verdict:** "On target — handover Mon 23 Nov as agreed".
- **The status report:** "Why we are not where we should be: Nothing —
  everything is on plan."
- **At that moment:**
  - 136 lines have no date;
  - 47 of 48 tests have nothing agreed to show;
  - no machine has an arrival date.
- **Only the front page says it, and quietly:** "45 more booked · 136 with
  no date agreed" under Needs you.
- **The gate headers say work has started when none has.** "8 machines
  installing" and "8 machines handing over", with nothing done.

**6. What do the team and a client see on day one?**
- **The team** is told what they may do ("What was agreed … and deleting
  stay with Rowland"). They can add machines, give stages, plan and add
  lines.
- **A client** reads everything, with no control that changes anything,
  and "You can read this job and take its reports".
- **Inviting** is in Details → People → "Invite by email". It needs the
  cloud, so it was **not proved here**.

**7. Wherever something is entered, can it be edited, deleted and left?**
Yes, at the start:
- **The forms can be left.** The new project form and the add-machine form
  both have Cancel.
- **A machine can be corrected or removed.** It is renamed in place, and
  removed with a confirm (owner).
- **Giving stages can be undone.** "Give them all" has Undo.
- **A stage can be removed from every machine** that has not started it.
- **The usual lists stay editable.**

No gap found here beyond the supplier name in question 2.

## Proposed, for your decision — nothing built yet

Each item hangs off a list that is already there (no new noun) and says
where it shows on screen and on paper.

1. **Who owes each line starts right.** Each entry on the usual lists says
   who it is usually with: the supplier, or the site. The defaults would
   put "Air and power connected", "Electrically complete", "Safety sign-off
   (PUWER)" and "Client signed off" with the site; the owner changes any
   of them.
   - **A supplier's name, corrected on the machine**, moves its open lines
     to the new name.
   - **Shows on:** the control room's who owes what, Needs you, the status
     and client reports' "waiting on", and the handover report.
2. **A usual test carries what it must show.** "Passes if", and a
   performance run's agreed rate, are written once on the usual test, and
   every machine's test gets it. Each stays editable on its own test, and
   it stays with the owner, as agreed things do.
   - **Shows on:** each test, the test card, the client report and the
     handover report's "Had to show".
3. **Commission's columns plan like Install's.** A test's heading opens the
   same panel: plan it for every machine, who it is with, and add it to the
   ones without it.
4. **"One after another" when planning a stage for every machine** — each
   machine N days after the one before, in the order they are listed, as
   lines are installed. This is a choice in the same panel, beside "the same
   days". Yours to say if it is worth having.
5. **A job not yet planned says so.** Until every line has a day and every
   test something agreed to show, the verdict says it plainly, in grey:
   - "Not fully planned — 136 with no date · 47 tests with nothing agreed
     to show · 8 machines with no arrival date".

   It never says "everything is on plan". The plan page says what is not on
   it yet. The status report lists what is not planned under "why we are not
   where we should be". The gate headers count only machines with something
   started.
6. **Adding machines stays open for the next one** (save, next, save), and
   takes a pasted list, one machine a line with its supplier after a comma.
   Eight machines become one paste.
7. **The small words** above, fixed.

## Not proved here

  the cloud.
- **The live database's rules.** The Supabase connector did not connect
