# Faultline as Rowland's consultancy instrument — the bigger shape (8 November; cut back the same day at his word)

Rowland, 8 November, after three home-page options were drawn: *"No ... we need to think bigger ... We have great tools. We do have a
great user interface. What is missing is the customer. And how the app operates to service the customer. The customer is me ... when I
have your factory, it's yes, it's your factory, but it's the client's factory essentially ... I go in, I build what I see, an area,
production lines within the area ... Now I have that model. I can go into them areas and start using the tools. But then there's loads
about customer facing. So we have wonderful reports ... But then what does real customer facing reporting look like? ... that element
that's likely not there and not there really where it's supposed to be."*

## 1. Who is served, and how

The **customer of Faultline is Rowland** (Streamline Synergy). He serves **clients**: he is engaged by a business, goes into its factory,
models what he sees, uses the tools to find and quantify what is wrong, drives the fixes, proves them, and reports to the client in a
form that wins the next piece of work. Faultline has to carry that whole job — not only the tools in the middle of it.

## 2. What is there, and what is missing

| The consultant's job | In Faultline today | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| A client — the business he works for, its people | nothing: everything sits in one *Factory* | **the client** as a record: name, contacts (site director, sponsor), their logo |
| Their site, modelled as he sees it | *Factory* → lines → machines (one factory per account; dual lines) | **the site** (a client can have more than one) and **areas** (packing hall, despatch) between the site and its lines |
| Why he is there — the brief | a project with a method, on its lines | **the engagement**: the brief in the client's words, scope (areas, lines), dates, what success is, the baseline, the phases (diagnose · deliver · sustain) |
| Finding and quantifying | 32 tools, problems with their cost, the line report | — (strong) |
| What it is worth to the client | each problem's £ a day; before → after on a fix | **the benefits register**: every opportunity's value, its stage — *identified → agreed → in delivery → realised (measured again)* — the total against the fee, the client's ROI |
| Doing something about it | Actions, the tracker, the Gantt, owners and dates | — (strong) |
| Telling the client | papers per tool, per line, per project, Saved copies | **client reporting** — the engagement's own papers, branded and in the client's language (§3) |
| Moving between clients | — | a **client switcher** at the top, each client's world its own; past engagements kept, closed |

## 3. What real client-facing reporting looks like

Five papers a consultant actually hands over, each drawn from what is already recorded — never typed twice — branded Streamline
Synergy (and the client's logo), in plain words, the money first:

1. **The weekly update** (one page) — what we did, what we found, the money found so far, what is being fixed and by whom, decisions
   needed from you, next week.
2. **The diagnostic report** (end of the diagnose phase) — the baseline, where the time and money go (the losses), the top
   opportunities ranked by value and effort, the recommendation and what it would return.
3. **The steering pack** (a monthly or phase review, deck-shaped) — the story on a few pages: headline, benefits to date against the
   target, progress, risks, asks.
4. **The benefits statement** — every opportunity with its value and stage, identified vs realised, each realised one with its
   before → after evidence; the line the finance director signs.
5. **The close-out report** — what was found, what was done, what it proved, the realised savings, how it is held (the standards, the
   checks), what is left as a recommendation.

Plus **Present**: any of these on screen, full page, no menus, for the meeting itself.

## 4. How it would run, start to finish

1. **New client** → name, logo, contacts.
2. **Model the site** → areas, lines in each area, machines (what *Set up the lines* does today, one level up).
3. **Open the engagement** → the brief, scope, dates, baseline, phases.
4. **On the floor** → pick an area or line, use the tools (as today); a finding with its value lands in the benefits register.
5. **Deliver** → actions and fixes (as today); a fix proved moves its opportunity to *realised*.
6. **Report** → the weekly update each week, the diagnostic at the end of diagnose, the steering pack at reviews, the close-out at the
   end — each made in a tap from what is recorded.
7. **Close** → the engagement closed and kept; the client's next engagement starts from the same site model.

## 6. To ask Rowland

- Does a client ever have more than one site in one engagement? Are *areas* always the level above lines?
- Is an engagement one of today's projects, or above them (an engagement holding several projects and studies)?
- Which papers does he hand clients today, and how often? An example of one he is proud of would set the standard.
- Does the fee belong in the app (benefits against fee, ROI), or stay outside it?
- Does the client ever log in to see it, or is everything handed over as papers and Present?

## 7. His answers, and the shape cut back — the same day

Rowland: *"I actually think it went too far off."* His answers: a client may have **more than one site** in an engagement — *"Yes,
possibly"*; an engagement **holds several projects and studies** — *"Yes"*; the papers he hands clients are **sporadic** — *"Got no idea"*
(so reports stay on demand, never a schedule the app imposes); **no fee** in the app; the client logging in to a client view is for
later — *"don't do number 5 yet. We need to concentrate on what's good for me."*

So what stands, smallest first:

- **K1 · Client, site and area on the lines** — what he builds when he walks in: *"I go in, I build what I see, an area, production lines
  within the area."* Each line says its client, its site and its area (words on the line, like its type — no new record); Set up the
  lines asks them; the Factory page is titled by the client and shows the lines site by site, area by area; with more than one client,
  the client is picked at the top of the Factory page. Nothing else moves.
- **K2 · The engagement** — the client's brief holding its projects and studies, on its sites. Designed after K1 is in use.
- Then, only when he asks: what is worth what (identified → realised), the engagement's papers, Present.
