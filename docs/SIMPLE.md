# Simple with detail

Rowland, 7 October, after two days of real data in the app:

> "We've drifted to complexity equals detail. We want simple with detail."

> "Within operations, people work in very simple ways. They want to know the
> current status of something, and they want to know the failures of why
> we're not where we should be, and then what we're going to do about it next."

> "The reports are so detailed ... I can't send that report out. It's too
> massive."

> "If I click on something, what is it? Was it part of the plan? Was it done?
> Changed the dates? Who?"

Nothing is pulled apart. Every capability stays. What changes is what a page
shows FIRST, and how many times it shows the same thing.

## The rule: three questions, in this order

Every screen, every drawer and every report answers, in this order:

1. **Where are we?** One line of state per thing — in words, in the house
   colours (CLAUDE.md, visual management).
2. **Why aren't we where we should be?** Only the abnormal: late, failed, a
   problem, critical, high risk — each with its cause and what it cost or
   could cost. A thing that is on plan is not listed; it is counted.
3. **What are we doing about it?** The action, whose it is, by when.

Everything else — history, every stage's account, every attempt, the plan in
full — is **detail**: one tap away on a screen, an appendix on paper. Detail is
never deleted; it is never the first thing either.

## Three tests for anything on a page

- **Shown once.** A record appears in one place on a page. A grid and a list of
  the same records is two places — the list goes, the grid's square opens the
  record.
- **Normal is counted, abnormal is named.** "11 on plan" is a number. "Coder
  late — no power, 6 h" is a line.
- **The first screenful is the answer.** If somebody has to scroll to find out
  where the job is, the page is wrong.

## The record — the same five lines on everything

Whatever is tapped — a stage, a test, a fix, a problem — opens to the same five
lines, in the same order, before any button:

| | says |
|---|---|
| What | stage / test / fix · which gate · which machine |
| State | done · late · problem · ahead — in words |
| Planned → actual | the planned days, and what really happened |
| Who | whose it is |
| Changed | the last change and why ("moved 9 → 12 Oct: no power"), the rest one tap away |

Then its actions. Then everything else, folded.

## The pages

| Page | First screenful | Detail, one tap away |
|---|---|---|
| Job front page | the three questions for the whole job: a line per gate, what is wrong and why, what is owed by whom | every gate's board |
| A gate (Install, Set up, Commission, Hand over) | its board (machine × stage/test) and **Needs you** — only what is owed | each square opens its record |
| A record | the five lines and its actions | its story, its parts, its pictures |
| Control room | one line per job: on target / behind / at risk, and why | the job |

## The reports

Two, from one button, the short one first:

- **Status report — one page, the one that gets sent.** Where we are (the
  verdict and a line per gate), why we are not where we should be (the
  critical, the risks, the late — each with cause and cost), what we are doing
  about it (actions, who, by when) and, on a commissioning job, the
  performance runs. One A4.
- **Full report** — today's report, whole, for whoever wants the record. The
  status page leads it, so the two can never disagree.

## Commission, specifically

- Columns are the **usual tests only**, kept in one place (add, rename,
  remove). A test that is not on the list sits in its own machine's row as one
  "other" square — never a column across every machine.
- Under the grid: **Needs you** — ran with no verdict, late, failed with no
  re-test. Nothing that is normal.
- "Next up", "Tests so far" and the separate programs list go: each repeated
  the grid. The programs column opens the programs; a square opens its test;
  the history is in the test and the full report.

## The order it is built in — each slice live before the next

1. Commission — one board and Needs you.
2. The status report — one page, sent first; the full report behind it.
3. The record's five lines.
4. The job front page as "where are we".
5. Install, Set up and Hand over to the same pattern.
6. The rail and the phone bar trimmed to what is used.

Each slice says, for every section it removes, what it was for and where that
now lives (CLAUDE.md, "Nothing is removed until we can say why it was there").
