# One door

Rowland, 8 October, after the programs work:

> "If I'm opening up something, there are multiple doors to edit something.
> I'm highlighting it as critical, then it still asks me how many hours, but
> then I put the same hours down at the bottom. There's duplication happening
> ... as we create additions. It should be easy and clear to see exactly what
> door opens what ... small little notes saying 'open here', or text, or a mess
> of different ways to see the same thing, rather than something very simple.
> I'm trying to open a problem — I have to filter, go all the way down the
> page, then open the problem again to edit it. There are so many ways to get
> to something, and it shouldn't be."

`docs/SIMPLE.md` decided what a page shows first. This decides how you get to a
thing and change it. It applies to the entire app.

## What was found (8 October, the seeded job, on a phone)

- **A problem has no home.** Tapping one on the Fixes page opens its *stage*.
  The problem is then one of several under "What happened", three screens down
  the stage's drawer, edited inline by a second form.
- **Two forms for one problem.** Writing it (`ProblemForm`) and editing it
  (`ProblemEdit`) are different forms with different boxes — the edit cannot
  book a fix or move the finish; the write-up cannot move hours to "could cost".
- **Hours asked twice in each.** Flag it critical or high risk and "Could cost —
  hours" appears; "What did it cost? Hours lost" is asked again under it. In
  the edit form, "Hours lost" sits at the top and "Could cost" at the bottom.
- **A record has two homes.** The drawer, and the record's own page ("Everything
  about it › files · notes · PDF card") — both edit the same record.
- **Four doors to change one stage** in its drawer: "change", "Change the dates
  or who ›", "Say how it went", and the record's page.
- **The drawer is long.** The Programs loaded stage: 2,908 px — three and a half
  phone screens — and 33 buttons and links; the fix: 973 px, 12.
- **Notes that restate or instruct**: "not moved since it was planned", "· a day
  here is 8 h", "none yet", "tap one for its stage", "How it was left — a
  picture is the proof…", and text links ending "›" beside rows that could
  have been the door themselves.

## The rules

1. **Every thing opens in the one drawer, from wherever it is listed** — a
   stage, a test, a fix, a program, and **a problem**. The row is the door: no
   "Open it ›" beside a row, no note saying where a tap goes.
2. **One form per thing, the same for writing and editing.** "Hit a problem"
   and "Edit" open the same problem form, filled or empty. A form never asks
   for the same number twice.
3. **One edit door per thing.** The drawer opens on its five lines (SIMPLE.md);
   one **Edit** opens its form. Its actions (Done, Hit a problem, Fixed…) sit
   under the lines; everything else is folded below.
4. **The drawer is the only home.** What the record's page added — files,
   notes, the PDF card — moves into the drawer, folded. The page stays only as
   the printable card.
5. **A note earns its place** only when a box would otherwise be misread. No
   note restates what is on the screen, says where a tap goes, or says "none".

## The problem form — once

What happened · pictures · **Flag it** (not flagged · high risk · critical) ·
**What it cost** — one box: hours lost, or a new finish; *could cost* only for
a high risk, because it has not happened yet · what it means and the ways round
it, when flagged · book a fix. The same form edits it.

## Slices — each live before the next

1. **Problems.** A problem opens in the drawer on its own, from the Fixes page,
   a stage's story, a program, a run, the day and the front page; one form for
   writing and editing; hours asked once.
2. **One edit door.** The drawer: five lines, one Edit, the actions, then folds.
   "change", "Change the dates or who ›" and "Say how it went" merge into Edit.
3. **One home.** Files, notes and the PDF card fold into the drawer; the
   record's page is the printable card only.
4. **The notes and "›" links** across every screen: the row is the door; notes
   that restate go.

Each slice says, for everything it removes, what it was for and where that now
lives (CLAUDE.md, "Nothing is removed until we can say why it was there").
