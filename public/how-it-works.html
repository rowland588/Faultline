How Faultline works

Walk your line with a phone. Faultline turns what you see into evidence, a cost, a tracked snag list — and proof it's getting better. Every picture below is the real app, and the app itself plays a two-minute film of all of it.

Part 1

The Pareto system

Log what you see on the floor, and the losses rank themselves.

1

Walk the line. Log what you see.

Tap the asset, tap what you saw, put a time to it — stopwatch it live, type it, or just log that it happened. Photo or video evidence attaches in one tap. It works with no signal; everything syncs itself later.

2

See where the line is bleeding — in hours and pounds.

Every log lands on a live Pareto: the vital few problems ranked by lost time and what they cost in idle labour. The big bar on the left is where your week is going.

3

Tap a bar. Ask why.

Drill from the problem into the kind of problem, the machine it lives on, the shift it bites hardest. Three taps from "we lose time to changeovers" to "size changes on the filler, on lates". That's where the fix goes.

4

Present it. Boardroom standard, straight from the floor.

One tap turns the same live numbers into a full-screen stage — headline hours and cost, the ranked bars, the story so far. No export, no slide deck, no "let me get back to you".

Part 2

The snag system

Film the walk, and pin every fault to the exact spot on the machine.

5

Film the walk. Pin the faults.

Film the line on your phone, freeze any frame into a named machine, and drop a red dot exactly where the problem is. Every snag gets an owner, a status, and an age — so it can't quietly rot in a notebook.

6

The same machine, then vs now.

Walk the line again next month and Faultline lines the walks up: the same machine side by side across time, snags visibly closed, new ones visibly appearing, and the one that's been open 200 days impossible to ignore.

Part 3

The living line

Your machines in floor order — and every number lands on the machine it belongs to.

7

The line itself, glowing where it hurts.

Every marked machine becomes a card in walking order, built straight from your own footage. Flip to £ heat and the machine bleeding most literally glows — the Pareto's verdict, standing on the equipment that earned it.

8

Receipts, hanging on the machines.

The proof layer puts each case where it physically lives: a proven fix wears its ✓ £/week receipt on the machine it fixed, and the case still being worked says so. Tap either and you're on its A3.

Part 4

The report system

Proof it's working — without writing a single report.

9

Prove it's getting better.

Lost hours per week, falling — with a green flag on the week each fix landed. And the honest list underneath: which losses are responding, and which are flat and need a different idea.

10

The week on one page.

One tap turns it all into a clean page — lost time, cost, who closed what, what needs a push — ready to print (A4 or A3) or save as a PDF for Monday's meeting. No manual reporting, ever.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is Faultline?

Faultline is improvement software for food factories: teams log production-line losses on a phone, the losses rank themselves on a live Pareto priced in £/week, fixes become owned and dated actions, and a before/after study proves whether each fix held — recorded as a dated receipt, not a claim.

Who is it for?

Ops and continuous-improvement teams in small and mid-size food manufacturing sites, usually introduced by an improvement consultant. It answers "does the line work and what does it cost?" — it is deliberately not a compliance or audit-checklist tool.

Does it need sensors, integrations or an IT project?

No. Faultline is an installable web app that runs offline-first on the phones a team already owns. Losses are observed and logged by people, everything syncs itself when there is signal, and there is no hardware to fit and nothing to integrate.

How is it different from OEE or machine-monitoring systems?

Sensor-based OEE tools see that a machine stopped; they cannot see why, or the changeover, labour and quality losses around it, and they do not run the fix or prove it worked. Faultline captures the why with human eyes and video walks, prices every loss in pounds, and closes the loop with a proof study. The two approaches complement each other.

How does it prove a saving is real?

A study re-measures the same scope the same way and compares average time per event before and after the fix, with both sample sizes and the calendar they cover always shown. The verdict passes a statistical significance test, and calling it freezes the numbers into a dated receipt that later data cannot quietly rewrite. Fixes are then watched for slipping.
