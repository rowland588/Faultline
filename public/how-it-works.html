How it works

The Operational Improvement System

Faultline, an Opex Elevate product

A line is losing minutes, packs and pounds. Faultline takes it from finding where, through working out why and fixing it by a recognised method, to organising who does what by when and proving the result on the same number — measured by people, worked out by formula, every finding said in money.

1 · Identify the opportunity

Find where the line is losing

Time the stops, count the losses, walk the line, write the problems down where they happen.

A shift's lost minutes, and where they went

Counted on the phone at the line's own speed: the shift less its breaks, then every loss by reason — short stops told apart from slow running — in minutes, packs and pounds. The biggest bar is where to look first.

To the team: "Of the planned minutes, this is where they went, and what each cost."

Every problem on the line, in order

The register holds each problem with its priority, where it sits, who has it and its journey — what was tried and what came of it. Nothing is deleted; a problem is put away, and brought back with one tap.

2 · Diagnose the problem

Say why, with figures you can defend

The technical tools are built from manual inputs and calculated live, each with its working shown and its limits said.

Also: control charts, gauge studies, value stream maps, SMED, work sampling, root cause with the fishbone and the five whys, calculators, and more — each opens from the Tools page.

3 · Apply a methodology

A project is a method, with its tools in the right place

Pick the method; its sections come in order, and the tools that fit each section come with them. Add any other tool to any section.

Start a project by choosing how it will be run

Eight methods, each with its question and its sections. None is picked for you. The project is on a line, with its lines picked from the factory.

The method's sections, and the tools in them

Under each section sit the tools that fit it, said honestly: built, a mode of another tool, or not built yet. How often each has been used in this project is counted, and New starts one in the project. A tool you use that the method does not list still shows.

To the sponsor: "We are in Measure; the capability study is done and the gauge study is next."

The same page on the phone, at the line

Tap a tool to open it in the project; New starts one at once. What you do inside a project lands in the project — no "which project is this for?".

4 · Organise the delivery

Who is doing what, by when

Projects, actions with an owner and a day, programs and tests proved on the line, and the journey day by day.

Every action, the late first

Who is doing what, where, why and by when, with its priority. A row opens its record; a person's own actions go on paper for the meeting. Whatever you do in a project lands here with no extra step.

To the team: "These four are late, these are whose they are, and these are due this week."

5 · Verify the result

Prove it on the same number, in money

A fix is judged on the number it was meant to move, before and after, and said in pounds — allowed to say not proven.

Did it change — and by how much

Before and after on the same number, with the right test for the data, the interval in words, and the difference that matters. The verdict is red for the wrong way and green for the right way, never for a lucky one.

The papers to hand over

Every tool and project has its paper, built from the same records the screens show and in the same words: what the problem was, what was done, who owed what, and the result. On screen first, then as a PDF to send.

To the client: "Here is exactly what changed, what it is worth, and what each side owes."

Questions

Common questions

Is this a project-management tool?

No. Projects are one part of it — the delivery. Faultline covers the whole improvement lifecycle, from finding the opportunity to verifying the result.

Does it need sensors, integrations or an IT project?

No. Faultline is an installable web app on the phones and laptops a team already has. Everything is counted, timed, tapped, photographed or filmed by a person.

Does it work on the floor with no signal?

Yes. Every screen works offline and syncs itself to the rest of the team when coverage returns.

Who sees what?

It is invite-only. A project's owner adds people by email, as owner, team or client, and they see that project on their own device.

Does it replace OEE or machine monitoring?

No — those see that a machine stopped. Faultline finds what is costing the line, works out why, runs the fix by a method and shows whether it worked.
